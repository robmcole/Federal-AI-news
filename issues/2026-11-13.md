## Congress & Legislation

**[Calls for Nvidia probes and new chip export limits intensify after Huang resigns](https://rollcall.com/2026/11/12/calls-for-nvidia-probes-new-chip-export-limits-intensify/)** (6 minute read)

Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO and director on Nov. 11. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, and Harvey Jones is the board's first chair. Lawmakers from both parties say the resignation doesn't settle the national security questions raised by Huang's reported affair with an alleged Chinese spy. Senate Majority Leader Thune backs a Senate investigation. House China, Foreign Affairs and Intelligence chairs asked Huang for a transcribed interview Dec. 1 and Nvidia's general counsel for one Dec. 2, and they want BIS Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler at a Dec. 3 hearing. **What changed since Wednesday's digest:** the Senate NDAA manager's amendment already carries chip-security, equipment-control and chip-export-ban language, and the House NDAA doesn't. House Armed Services Chair Rogers says he "would like to see" export controls in the final bill.

**[Trump renews his call for a $350B party-line defense package as the GOP runs out of time](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/12/trump-iran-war-funding-reconciliation-01162603)** (4 minute read)

In a Nov. 12 Truth Social post, Trump urged Senate Republicans to pass $350 billion in defense spending through reconciliation "IMMEDIATELY," before Democrats take their new majorities. The House-passed budget blueprint has stalled in the Senate for nearly four months, and it carries only $60 billion for the Pentagon. Thune says Republicans must decide by Thanksgiving, and Budget Chair Ron Johnson wants a blueprint on the floor before next week's recess. **What changed since yesterday:** we reported that DoD's $29.5B AI Arsenal supercomputing request is among the items riding on reconciliation, and the White House is now publicly forcing a decision.

*Source: Politico · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Senate GOP sets up a lame-duck path to a new comptroller general](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/agency-oversight/2026/11/senate-gop-set-up-lame-duck-effort-to-appoint-new-comptroller-general/)** (4 minute read)

HSGAC Chairman Rand Paul scheduled a nomination hearing for next Thursday for an unnamed comptroller general nominee. His office gave the statutory 10-member bipartisan commission less than 24 hours, from Nov. 11 to 8 a.m. Nov. 12, to submit candidates. Schumer, Jeffries, Peters and Garcia called it a "sham." OMB Deputy Director Eric Ueland has reportedly been considered, and a Democratic aide says GAO received ethics paperwork for him this week. The comptroller general serves a 15-year term and leads the agency that audits federal AI and IT programs, so the choice will shape oversight well beyond this administration.

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Army CID wants AI-driven software to monitor the darknet and encrypted messaging apps](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/12/army-cid-software-darknet-encrypted-messaging/)** (3 minute read)

An RFI from the Army Criminal Investigation Division seeks a cloud-hosted commercial threat-intelligence platform. It would use behavioral analytics, machine learning and AI to spot "mobilization to violence" against troops and installations on Signal, Telegram, Discord and darknet forums. The capability matrix asks for NLP and computer vision to decode slang and symbols, cross-platform correlation of anonymous accounts, blockchain transaction graphing, preservation of ephemeral or deleted content, and attribution masking for undercover work. Responses are due Dec. 9, an early look at a likely OSINT and AI analytics buy with civil-liberties sensitivities.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Air Force seeks flight-ready AI autonomy software for cislunar space vehicles](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/10/air-force-wants-ai-for-cislunar-space-vehicles/)** (3 minute read)

A new Air Force RFI asks for existing AI/ML software for station-keeping, trajectory optimization and hazard avoidance beyond Earth orbit, where communication delays make ground-based command and control too slow. White papers should cover autonomous mission planning, predictive analytics, trajectory planning and computer vision. The software should need essentially no development within 90 days of authority to proceed and be fully qualified within 18 months. The RFI says the government "is not interested in investing in a cislunar SI research and development program," so this favors vendors with mature autonomy stacks. Responses are due Dec. 4.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[MANTECH wins a $307M CBP business intelligence and analytics contract](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/mantech-307m-dhs-cbp-business-intelligence-contract-award)** (3 minute read) ⚠️

MANTECH won a potential five-year, $307 million contract for business intelligence support to CBP. It will provide analytics, automation and threat visualization to detect terrorism, smuggling and trade violations in border and trade data. The award follows a $273 million GSA Alliant 2 task order MANTECH won for similar work in 2020, and it continues a CBP relationship dating to 2017. It's one of the larger recent DHS data-analytics awards as CBP leans harder on automated targeting. The award details come from the company's announcement.

*Source: GovConWire · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Military-made AI agent helped thwart a World Cup bomb threat, Pentagon GenAI chief says](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/military-made-ai-tool-helped-thwart-world-cup-bomb-threat/)** (2 minute read)

Army Capt. Ryan Hetrick, who leads the Pentagon's Generative AI Task Force, disclosed that an AI agent built with DoD, FBI and Coast Guard colleagues at the White House helped respond to a previously unreported bomb threat at SoFi Stadium on June 18. The agent cut the time to write a "spot report" for the World Cup Task Force from about 20 minutes to 90 seconds. He also said a tool his team built at the 101st Airborne cut a cyber operations workflow from 100 hours to 15, and it is now available to all cyber officers across the department. The White House, FBI and local police didn't respond to requests for comment, so the claim rests on Hetrick's account.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[HUD plans an OCIO overhaul with a new data and AI division](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2026/11/hud-plans-ocio-overhaul-new-division-data-and-ai/417190/)** (3 minute read)

A Federal Register notice of intent to reorganize HUD would rebuild the CIO's office around portfolio and product-based delivery. It would create a "division for data and super intelligence" under the CTO, move the chief data officer from Policy Development and Research into OCIO, and add design-and-experience and customer-experience divisions. Budget, acquisition and resourcing would be consolidated into one division. HUD aims to start by the end of FY2026 and finish in FY2027, as it adjusts to deep staffing cuts. Expect HUD IT and data contracts to be restructured along these product lines.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [Notice of Intent To Reorganize HUD (Federal Register, Nov. 10, 2026)](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/10/2026-22838/notice-of-intent-to-reorganize-hud-and-revise-headquarters-and-field-operating-model)*

**[CMS CIO Patrick Newbold to leave Nov. 27; Mark Plaugher to act](https://www.nextgov.com/people/2026/11/cms-cio-leave-agency-end-month/417181/)** (3 minute read)

Newbold, a former CIO at the Social Security Administration and global deputy CIO at NASA, will leave CMS after two years. Mark Plaugher, detailed to the Office of Information Technology since Nov. 2, becomes acting CIO. Newbold's LinkedIn credits him with $850 million in IT savings, $101 million in Medicare recoveries through analytics, a multi-cloud shift and CMS being first to use GSA's OneGov deals. He has said about 80% of CMS staff and contractors use AI weekly. His exit comes as Democrats prepare to scrutinize CMS's WISeR AI pilot.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Homeland Security Investigations says it needs auditable AI it can defend in court](https://fedscoop.com/homeland-security-investigations-auditable-ai/)** (4 minute read)

Peter Hatch, principal assistant director of HSI's Office of Intelligence, said everything HSI uses to build a case, AI inputs and outputs included, is subject to discovery. Vendors must show auditable outputs traceable to source documents, with humans in the loop. HSI has nearly a dozen AI use cases deployed, including tip triage, media-evidence classification and auto-redaction, and about five more in pre-deployment, such as lead generation and maritime data synthesis. Several are labeled "presumed high-impact but determined not high-impact," a designation experts have questioned. The message for vendors: explainability and citation, not more models.

*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[GAO's chief scientist warns federal AI use will slow as introductory pricing ends](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/federal-agencies-ai-token-cost-management-gao)** (4 minute read)

GAO Chief Scientist Sterling Thomas expects "a significant slowdown" in federal AI use over the next eight to 10 months as trial and discounted deals expire and agencies pay full token-based prices. He suggested managing agent context, resetting agents that lose context, considering open-weight models and building in-house agentic systems. A Cognition executive argued for performance-based, firm-fixed-price contracts over consumption-based deals. GAO's Nick Marinos suggested Congress revisit FISMA in 2027 for AI-driven cyber risk. It's a timely warning as OneGov promotional pricing, such as Gemini's $0.47 offer ending Nov. 15, runs out.

*Source: ExecutiveGov (summarizing GovCIO Media & Research) · Reliability Score: 3/5*
