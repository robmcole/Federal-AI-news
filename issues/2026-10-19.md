## White House & Executive Action

**[IMF members call for more support amid war shocks, 'advanced intelligence' risks](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/imf-members-call-for-more-support-amid-war-shocks-advanced-intelligence-risks)** (2 minute read)
Update: Friday's digest reported Treasury pressing IMF members to say "super intelligence" in the International Monetary and Financial Committee statement. The final statement, released Oct. 17 by committee chair and Saudi Finance Minister Mohammed Al-Jadaan, used neither "artificial intelligence" nor "super intelligence." It said "advanced intelligence technologies" have boosted investment and trade but that their uncertain economic impact, together with rising bond yields, "could amplify macro-financial vulnerabilities." Al-Jadaan said all members agreed to the language. It's a sign that other governments aren't yet willing to adopt the administration's SI terminology in international forums.
*Source: Reuters via The Straits Times · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[US's AI task force sets path for study and reviews, leader tells CNBC](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/uss-ai-task-force-sets-134746232.html)** (1 minute read)
Update: the day after the Super Intelligence Force's first meeting, chair and DNI Jay Clayton told CNBC the group set up three committees: reporting and response under Emil Michael, critical infrastructure under Scott Kupor, and law enforcement and responsibility under Andrew Ferguson. He said David Sacks will lead the risk study, that workforce and economic impacts will be reported to the Labor Department, and that any AI wrongdoing would be enforced through the FTC or the Justice Department. That means enforcement will rely on existing consumer-protection and criminal law rather than a new regulator.

## Congress & Legislation

**[Anthropic's proposed AI safety bill only kicks in if 50 deaths or $1 billion in damages are on the line](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/anthropics-proposed-ai-safety-bill-only-kicks-in-if-50-deaths-or-usd1-billion-in-damages-are-on-the-line-proposal-would-effectively-make-the-commerce-department-the-only-agency-capable-of-stopping-an-ai-model)** (3 minute read)
Citing Politico, Tom's Hardware reports that Anthropic is quietly circulating draft legislation under which an independent evaluator registered with the Commerce Department would periodically assess frontier labs and their models. The evaluator would have 72 hours to report an imminent risk of a bioweapon, an AI-enabled cyberattack or loss of control over a model or agents that could cause more than $1 billion in damage or more than 50 deaths or serious injuries. Commerce would then take the evidence to a federal judge in D.C., who would decide whether to halt the release, and no other agency could order a lab to stop. Anthropic says the draft reflects the Advanced AI Framework it has been pushing since June. With the SI Force leaning on existing law, this gives Congress an industry-written model for pre-release intervention that also narrows the government's power to step in.
*Source: Tom's Hardware (reporting on Politico) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Tech bills of the week: Cutting federal funding for Flock; Making data centers shoulder their power costs; and more](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/10/tech-bills-week-cutting-federal-funding-flock-making-data-centers-shoulder-their-power-costs-and-more/416661/)** (3 minute read)

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[DISA faces another protest over Enclave contract expansion](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/contracting/2026/10/disa-faces-another-protest-over-enclave-contract-expansion/)** (3 minute read)
Competitive Range Solutions filed a GAO protest on Oct. 8 challenging DISA's plan to add the migration of combatant commands to DoDNet onto Leidos' $11 billion Defense Enclave Services contract without competition. It follows a GDIT protest that was dismissed after DISA promised new market research. ## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[Key Practices for Incident Readiness and Response in Super Intelligence Training and Evaluation](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/10/key-practices-incident-readiness-and-response-super-intelligence-training)** (2 minute read)
On Oct. 16, NIST's Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI), NSA's Super Intelligence Security Center and CISA published 10 voluntary practices for incidents in which AI agents take "significant uninstructed actions" that break operator controls or human oversight during training and evaluation. The practices cover five stages: preparation, detection, response, recovery and reporting. They include least-privilege access and strict network allowlists, monitors the agents can't tamper with, kill mechanisms, tamper-resistant logs, revoking credentials an agent accessed, notifying affected parties, voluntarily reporting incidents and near misses to CISA, and public disclosure. The guidance builds on NIST CSF 2.0 and SP 800-61r3 and draws on CAISSI's own evaluation work. It is the first federal playbook for the kinds of agent incidents labs have disclosed this fall, and it could inform the lab reporting protocol the SI Force is drafting.
*Source: NIST · Reliability Score: 5/5*
*Primary document: [Key Practices PDF (NIST CAISSI, NSA SISC, CISA)](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2026/10/16/Key_Practices_for_Incident_Readiness_and_Response_in_Super_Intelligence%20_Training_and_Evaluation.pdf)*

**[G7 to launch exercises to guard against AI cyber attacks](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/g7-launch-exercises-guard-against-ai-cyber-attacks-6464781)** (2 minute read)
G7 finance ministers and central bank governors, the U.S. among them, agreed in Bangkok to run tabletop exercises simulating AI-driven cyberattacks on financial institutions, Japanese Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said. The results will test existing protocols and be used to revise incident manuals, and private banks may take part depending on the scenario. The deal came after the IMF warned that an AI-enabled attack on a critical financial service could undermine confidence. Treasury and financial regulators are now planning for frontier-AI cyber risk alongside the SI Force's critical infrastructure work.
*Source: Reuters via CNA · Reliability Score: 4/5*
