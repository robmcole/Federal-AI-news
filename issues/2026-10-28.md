## Acquisition & Contracts

**[GSA unveils token-based OneGov pricing for Claude](https://www.nextgov.com/acquisition/2026/10/gsa-unveils-token-based-onegov-pricing-claude/416855/)** (2 minute read)
What's new since last week's HHS sole-source story: GSA announced a new OneGov deal Tuesday giving federal, state and local agencies a 40% discount on pay-per-use (token-based) Claude pricing, with no minimum purchase, available through resellers and the major cloud platforms. It replaces the $1-per-user Claude for Enterprise and Government offer that expires Oct. 31; Anthropic says the discounts will save the government an estimated $1 billion. It mirrors OpenAI's 27-month OneGov deal with a 50% token discount that started this month, and GSA says OneGov has saved more than $1.8 billion overall. FedScoop notes analysts warn pay-per-use pricing makes costs hard to predict, and Google's $0.47 Gemini offer still expires Nov. 15 with no successor announced.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[What do agencies get now that USAi isn't free? GSA doing the 'boring' work](https://fedscoop.com/usai-gsa-shared-services-platform-cost/)** (3 minute read)
GSA started charging for USAi, its governmentwide AI platform, last month, with agencies now paying a "platform fee plus passthrough usage costs" after it launched free in August 2025. GSA AI architecture lead Ryan Palmer pitched the value as skipping months of authority-to-operate work and trials, with GSA handling acquisition, testing, reference architectures and telemetry so agencies can "just rapidly turn the key." He said GSA is moving from small pilots to broader operational use while still passing on OneGov savings, though he didn't directly address the new pricing.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Industry, legal experts urge FAR Council to set standards for AI in federal procurement](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/acquisition-policy/2026/10/industry-legal-experts-urge-far-council-to-set-standards-for-ai-in-federal-procurement/)** (5 minute read)
Comment letters on the FAR overhaul's second batch of proposed rules (comments closed Oct. 19) urge the FAR Council to set rules for how agencies use AI in bid evaluations, which no current FAR provision addresses. Protorae Law's Alan Chvotkin pointed to the TRAX protest, where the Court of Federal Claims ordered the Army to put its AI-generated proposal evaluations in the record even though it said it never used them, and proposed that agencies disclose which AI tools they use, have source selection officials independently verify every AI recommendation, and document each one in the contract file. AI acquisition vendor GovSignals also asked for governmentwide standards for AI in evaluation, price analysis and contract files.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Army Partners With AWS, WWT on AI Cyber Pilot Project](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/army-project-griffin-aws-wwt-ai-cyber-defense)** (3 minute read)
Amazon Web Services and World Wide Technology were picked for Project Griffin, an Army pilot worth a combined $2.6 million to build an agentic, autonomous AI platform that helps cyber defenders counter threats faster with less manual data review. It's one of three fast-tracked efforts under Project ARDS (Army Rapid Defensive of Cyber Systems), led by principal cyber advisor Brandon Pugh, who said the Army wants AI tools that "detect, divert and deceive" adversaries. The new Project Manager, Cyber Warfare runs the pilot, with Army Contracting Command-Aberdeen handling the contract.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Space Force to Issue Cislunar RFIs Seeking Commercial Tech, AI Autonomy Software](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/space-force-cislunar-rfis-commercial-tech-ai-autonomy-software)** (3 minute read)
Maj. Gen. Stephen Purdy said the Space Force will soon release two RFIs for capabilities it wants fielded between Earth and the Moon by the end of fiscal 2029: one for ready-made commercial hardware and software for space domain awareness and positioning, navigation and timing, and a second for AI and autonomy software that lets spacecraft detect hardware failures, process sensor data and adjust missions on their own. "I'm not going to pay you R&D funds for this stuff," he said, so vendors will need evidence of performance. China's activity in cislunar space is driving the urgency.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[RFI offers path forward for DoD with CMMC](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-news/2026/10/rfi-offers-path-forward-for-dod-with-cmmc/)** (6 minute read)
Part two of Federal News Network's investigation, following yesterday's report on DOD's paused cybersecurity certification program: a DOD task force summary from early September lists fixes the CIO could make without new rules, such as more consistent assessments, finalizing NIST SP 800-171 Revision 3 (on hold since CIO Kirsten Davies arrived in December) and consistent CUI marking. The task force suggested restarting CMMC Phase 2 for a narrower group of contractors within 90 days while longer-term changes go through rulemaking, and extending the window to close open security gaps (POA&M) to 270 days. Contractors should watch for which of these options DOD adopts when the pause ends.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[How the Pentagon wants to buy cyber outcomes, not equipment](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/how-the-pentagon-wants-to-buy-cyber-outcomes-not-equipment/)** (6 minute read)
The Pentagon is pushing "Cyber Effects-as-a-Service," a model for contracting for specific cyber outcomes rather than tools, with contractors playing a bigger role in operations themselves, which drafts of the forthcoming DOD cyber strategy also reflect. Assistant Secretary for Cyber Policy Katie Sutton says demand for cyber capabilities "far exceeds the supply" and wants industry to move from a "contractual relationship to an operational partner." Breaking Defense's sources say startups and traditional primes are both positioning to lead the model, which raises new questions about risk and oversight.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[NIST issues roadmap for transition to post-quantum encryption standards](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/nist-issues-roadmap-transition-post-quantum-encryption-standards/416841/)** (3 minute read)
NIST released "Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards," which lists the encryption algorithms quantum computers could break, the standards replacing them, and how and when older algorithms will be phased out or disallowed in federal systems. It supports President Trump's June executive order requiring high-value assets and high-impact systems to move to post-quantum key establishment by 2030 and digital signatures by 2031, with OMB giving agencies until 2035 to finish. It arrives after an October GAO report found agencies haven't fully identified their vulnerable systems.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Coast Guard Seeks Public Input on AI-Enabled Target Detection Tech](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/coast-guard-seeks-ai-search-rescue-crada)** (3 minute read)
A Federal Register notice published Monday says the Coast Guard Research and Development Center plans a cooperative research agreement with LOOKOUT to test AI-enabled automated target detection for search and rescue, measuring detection performance, interoperability and workflow benefits. The Coast Guard wants commercial, portable, self-contained edge-computing technology that works with any video feed, and other qualified companies can ask to join this or similar efforts. Comments are due Nov. 25.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[National cyber director rolls out latest pilot in South Carolina, this time for rural hospitals](https://cyberscoop.com/oncd-rural-hospital-cybersecurity-pilot-project-lifeline-250/)** (3 minute read)
National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross launched Project Lifeline 250 in South Carolina on Monday, a six-month pilot that pairs companies with rural hospitals to defend their networks, deploy backup devices after an incident and replace risky legacy equipment. Participants include Abnormal AI, Cisco, Cyera, Microsoft, Rilian Partners, Trellix and Zscaler, along with South Carolina state and local agencies. It's ONCD's second regional pilot after the Texas water-sector effort; healthcare had 804 large breaches affecting more than 140 million people last year, and ONCD hasn't said what happens when the pilot ends.
*Source: CyberScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Federal workforce cuts linked to lower satisfaction scores across government services](https://www.govexec.com/management/2026/10/federal-workforce-cuts-linked-lower-satisfaction-scores-government-services/416854/)** (4 minute read)
The American Customer Satisfaction Index found citizen satisfaction with federal services fell 2.6% to 68.6 out of 100, the first drop since 2021. Only State and GSA improved, Commerce, VA and Treasury held steady, and 10 other agencies declined. ACSI's Forrest Morgeson linked the drop to the net loss of about 264,000 federal employees and said agencies' AI investments aren't yet making up for it, because cutting staff before the tools are embedded "can be disruptive."
*Source: Government Executive · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[CISA gives federal agencies until Thursday to address exploited PHP flaws](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/cisa-gives-federal-agencies-until-thursday-address-exploited-php-flaws/416838/)** (3 minute read)
What's changed since our Oct. 22 item: CISA added both php2shell flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Monday, meaning it now says hackers are exploiting them, and set an Oct. 29 deadline for civilian agencies to fix them. The catalog doesn't name victims, and Sophos and Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 have both walked back earlier claims of successful attacks. Censys counts more than 10 million services patched since Oct. 21, but Nextgov found about 900 Shodan results tied to government sites using PHP.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Scale publishes first AI benchmark through CAISSI partnership](https://fedscoop.com/scale-chemistry-ai-benchmark-caissi-partnership-dualchem/)** (4 minute read)
Scale AI released DualChem, the first benchmark built under its research agreement with NIST's Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI, formerly the AI Safety Institute). It has 600 expert-written questions on realistic chemistry tasks that could help or harm, such as synthesis procedures, and measures how much an AI model helps a novice. Across about 30 frontier models, accuracy averaged about 50% (top models about 67%), and models usually answered even when they flagged a question as dual-use. Because the questions have never been published, the government gets a test models can't have trained on.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[All the security risks Anthropic is worried about before its IPO](https://cyberscoop.com/anthropic-ipo-filing-cybersecurity-risks/)** (4 minute read)
Anthropic's SEC filing ahead of its IPO says it expects foreign governments to try harder to get its frontier models, and that misuse of Claude in cyberattacks could bring liability, lost customers and insurance problems. It cites Commerce export controls on its Mythos 5 and Fable 5 models and confirms the FTC sent it a civil investigative demand this month about product safety and security and its consumer and privacy practices. For agencies buying Claude under the new OneGov deal, the filing is a public account of the regulatory and security risks the company itself sees.
*Source: CyberScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Before you file that AI bug report, OpenAI has some advice](https://cyberscoop.com/openai-ai-generated-bug-reports/)** (3 minute read)
OpenAI's cyber executive in residence Michael Aiello said AI-generated vulnerability reports are swamping open-source maintainers, some of whom have stopped accepting submissions. He urged researchers to make reports high quality and readable by a single person, and to pay for coordinating disclosure themselves, as OpenAI did with the PHP Foundation on the recent PHP flaws. OpenAI says its Patch the Planet program has developed 661 patches, with 458 accepted by maintainers.
*Source: CyberScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Anduril expanding into laptop manufacturing in partnership with Qualcomm](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/27/anduril-palmer-luckey-bluechip-computers-qualcomm/)** (4 minute read)
Anduril announced BlueChip, a line of U.S.-built laptops it's making with Qualcomm, whose Snapdragon X Series chips will provide the processing, graphics and on-device AI.
