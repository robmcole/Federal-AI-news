## Congress & Legislation

**[Trahan drops GOP partner and rewrites the FRONTIER Act so states can go further](https://spectrumnewsnecn.com/necn/new-england/politics/2026/11/10/bipartisan-ai-safety-bill-)** (4 minute read)

Rep. Lori Trahan (D-Mass.) is reworking the FRONTIER Act without Rep. Jay Obernolte (R-Calif.), according to a source familiar with the plan, after Politico first reported the shift Tuesday. Today's bill requires third-party audits of frontier labs, creates a Commerce undersecretary for AI security, and sets a process to halt models posing imminent catastrophic risk, while preempting some state laws. The new version, expected in the coming weeks, would instead treat federal law as a floor, the way minimum wage works, with states free to add stricter rules. It's the clearest early sign that the incoming Democratic majority will pursue a tougher, less preemptive AI safety bill in 2027, though Trump's veto and the Senate filibuster still loom.

*Source: Spectrum News NECN · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Nvidia spy allegations give NDAA chip-export bills new momentum](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/nvidia-spy-allegations-likely-to-spur-laws-totighten-chinas-access-to-ai-chips-5013421)** (3 minute read)

Lawmakers and advocates told Reuters that reports Jensen Huang had a relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence agent make it likelier Congress passes three chip measures now in the Senate NDAA manager's package: the AI Overwatch Act (congressional power over advanced chip exports), the Chip Security Act (chip tracking to fight smuggling), and the Match Act (pushing allies to restrict chipmaking tools). Sens. Elizabeth Warren and Jim Banks said Monday the episode makes the concerns "even more urgent," and Rep. Bill Foster pushed his Chip Security Act, which passed committee unanimously. Nvidia is seen as the biggest opponent, ITI opposes the measures, and the White House didn't comment. Advocates say White House buy-in is the key hurdle as NDAA conference talks wrap up this session.

*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Democrats put ending Medicare's AI prior-authorization pilot in their first 100 hours](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2026/11/democratic-congress-likely-scrutinize-medicaid-work-requirements-medicare-ai-test/417157/)** (4 minute read)

A House Democratic Healthcare Working Group memo lists ending CMS's WISeR model, which tests AI-supported coverage review in Medicare, as a first-100-hours priority, using Rep. Suzan DelBene's Seniors Deserve SMARTER Care Act. Unlike Medicaid work-requirement repeal, a WISeR ban has bipartisan support: House Appropriations unanimously adopted an amendment to halt it earlier this year. Sixteen Senate Democrats also asked for a two-year delay of Medicaid work requirements, which launch nationally Jan. 1 and are driving state eligibility-system tech work. Vendors on WISeR and state Medicaid modernization contracts should plan for real policy risk in 2027.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Incoming House Science chair Lofgren puts AI, quantum and fusion atop her agenda](https://fedscoop.com/house-democrats-plot-make-trump-science-cuts-difficult/)** (6 minute read)

Rep. Zoe Lofgren, expected to chair House Science, told FedScoop the panel will investigate DOE grant cuts, apparent NSF impoundment, EPA's research reorganization and the administration's roughly 40 equity stakes in private companies, and will write "more prescriptive" spending language. Her legislative priorities are quantum, fusion and AI, and she hopes to finish committee-passed bills this year, including the CREATE AI Act (H.R. 2385, a permanent NSF AI research resource) and the AI Security and Innovation Act (H.R. 9363, codifying NIST's AI standards body). She called AI the hardest of the three because of the partisan split on regulation. House Democrats are also weighing an AI select committee pushed by Reps. Bill Foster and Ted Lieu, which could reshuffle AI jurisdiction.

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[DISA plans a $500M single-award ADAPTS IDIQ for a lead integrator across ServiceNow, Salesforce, Workday and Palantir](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/disa-500m-adapts-salesforce-palantir-servicenow-sources-sought)** (4 minute read)

A SAM.gov sources sought notice posted Tuesday seeks a lead systems integrator for the All-Domain Adaptive Platforms and Technologies Support (ADAPTS) vehicle, covering the DISA Connect portal plus ServiceNow, Salesforce, Workday and Palantir: sustainment, continuous integration, PaaS engineering and operational visibility. DISA wants experience across at least three platform ecosystems, IL6-or-higher cloud deployments, cross-domain solutions and API gateways. It also requires a final Top Secret facility clearance (or timeline), cleared U.S.-citizen staff and CMMC Level 2 at award. Ordering would run February 2028 to February 2038, and responses are due Nov. 30. Small business BCMCG does part of the work today under a SETI task order ending April 2028, and DISA calls the rest a new requirement.

*Source: GovConWire · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[GAO: 17,000 F-35 parts worth up to $2.5B are missing from the Pentagon's inventory system](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/pentagon-cant-track-thousands-of-f-35-parts-gao/)** (5 minute read)

As of March 2026, more than 17,000 F-35 parts in maintenance or repair, worth an estimated $1.3 billion and possibly $2.5 billion, weren't recorded in the program's Enterprise Logistics Management System (ELMS). ELMS still can't interface with Lockheed Martin's and Pratt & Whitney's property systems, a gap GAO first flagged in 2022, so the JPO relies on quarterly contractor data dumps. A December 2025 submission had serial numbers for only 29% of records, and some parts were listed at a cent or less. DoD agreed with all four recommendations, the JPO says it's building real-time government–contractor data interfaces, and a full DCAA-supported inventory is targeted for March 2027. It's a reminder that sustainment data integration, not just AI, decides whether readiness tools can work.

*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[GAO's chief scientist warns that expiring AI promo pricing could slow federal adoption for 8 to 10 months](https://govciomedia.com/gaos-take-on-ai-costs-cyber-risks-in-federal-tech-strategies/)** (4 minute read)

GAO Chief Scientist Sterling Thomas said agencies have leaned on trials and promotional pricing that are now expiring, exposing them to the true cost of token-based AI pricing. He predicted "a significant slowdown of use of AI until we deal with that policy" over the next eight to 10 months. He suggested managing agent context to curb token burn and considering open-weight models, while noting many are foreign-built. GAO's Nick Marinos said agencies still lack complete AI use-case inventories and floated revisiting FISMA in 2027 to account for AI. With Gemini's $0.47 OneGov offer ending Nov. 15 and USAi now carrying a fee, buyers should look hard at outcome-based rather than consumption-based AI contracts.

*Source: GovCIO Media & Research · Reliability Score: 3/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[CISA rolls out military-style "critical information requirements" to spot threats before they become crises](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/cisa-outlines-internal-plan-swiftly-address-cyber-infrastructure-threats/417167/)** (3 minute read)

A Nov. 5 internal email from acting Director Nick Andersen, viewed by Nextgov/FCW, sets six operational focus areas. These cover national risk assessment, defense against major cyber threats, incident preparedness, infrastructure resilience and partner coordination. It also defines critical information requirements (what leaders must know to decide) and priority intelligence requirements (where hackers are operating against U.S. and allied networks and industrial systems, and what the warning signs look like). Andersen called it "the starting point of a deliberate, requirements-driven approach," with division-level guidance to follow in coming months, consistent with the CISA 2030 strategy. Nominee Michael LoSapio would inherit this framework if confirmed. Meanwhile, expected HSGAC chair Maggie Hassan and House Homeland's Bennie Thompson have already raised concerns about his Palantir ties.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[DOJ took over a Commerce chip-smuggling probe after Nvidia's general counsel contacted investigators](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-intervention-preceded-transfer-of-chip-probe-to-justice-department-5012839)** (3 minute read)

According to a New York Times report, Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter emailed Commerce investigators in August 2025 seeking information on their Megaspeed investigation, saying senior export-control official Jeff Kessler had permitted the contact. Megaspeed is the Singapore-based data center firm suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China. Kessler told investigators to proceed by the book, but DOJ later asked Commerce to pause its work and took over the case, which is now a criminal investigation. NBC News separately confirmed the handoff. The Times also reported Commerce hasn't added a company to the Entity List in more than a year, the longest gap in 18 years. Expect the incoming Democratic majority to make BIS enforcement independence an early oversight target.

*Source: Investing.com (summarizing The New York Times) · Reliability Score: 4/5*
