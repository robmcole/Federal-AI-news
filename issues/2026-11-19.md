# Federal AI & Tech Digest — Thursday, November 19, 2026

**TL;DR:** GAO says covert testers got viable attack plans out of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok in under 12 hours each. At their Shenzhen meeting, Trump told Xi the U.S. opposes "globalist regulation" of AI, while Xi pressed for joint work on AI safety. U.S. security officials told Congress last week's outages weren't attacks and tied most of them to rapid, AI-driven patching, as a new bipartisan bill proposes a Cyber and AI investigations board.

## White House & Executive Action

**[US President Trump rejects global AI regulation as China's Xi calls for cooperation on safety](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/19/us-president-trump-rejects-global-ai-regulation-as-chinas-xi-calls-for-cooperation-on-safe)** (3 minute read)
At APEC in Shenzhen today, Trump told leaders the U.S. opposes "any form of globalist regulation or anything to control and stifle SI," though he said "we have to be very careful" with its "bad elements." In their bilateral meeting, Xi said the two countries should strengthen coordination to "guard against artificial intelligence safety risks" and keep AI "always under human control." China's readout says both sides called the meeting positive and agreed to draw up an action plan for a "constructive relationship of strategic stability." Details of the AI-incident communication channel the two agreed to in September were expected to be worked out Thursday. The meeting comes days after Commerce's remote-access chip rule and Nvidia's halt of licensed H200 shipments, so watch for any chip or AI-channel follow-through in the U.S. readout.

## Congress & Legislation

**[Bipartisan bill would create cyber and AI review board amid series of tech outages](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/11/bipartisan-bill-would-create-cyber-and-ai-review-board-amid-series-tech-outages/417299/)** (4 minute read)
Reps. Morgan Luttrell (R-Texas) and Ritchie Torres (D-N.Y.) introduced the ROOT CAUSE Act on Wednesday. It would create a National Cyber and AI Investigations Board inside CISA to find out why major cyberattacks, AI incidents and technology failures happened and recommend fixes. The board could investigate faulty updates, configuration errors and AI failures even without evidence of an attack, including harmful actions by AI systems operating beyond their authorization. National security systems like NSANet would be out of scope. The executive branch couldn't abolish or consolidate the board, or alter its findings, without another act of Congress, a direct response to the administration emptying the Cyber Safety Review Board during its Salt Typhoon probe. Two companion House Homeland Republican bills target federal AI agents and CISA's work with tech companies.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Trump's GAO pick was a done deal before Congress got a say](https://www.govexec.com/oversight/2026/11/trumps-gao-pick-was-done-deal-congress-got-say/417310/)** (6 minute read)
What's new since our Nov. 17 item: ahead of this morning's 9:30 a.m. ET HSGAC hearing for Eric Ueland (comptroller general) and Michael LoSapio (CISA director), opposition to how Ueland was chosen is growing on both sides of the aisle. Former Comptrollers General Gene Dodaro and David Walker urged that the hearing be canceled, and 20 former inspectors general cited "irreconcilable conflicts of interest" because Ueland is OMB's deputy director. The 15-year post oversees impoundment probes and much of the government's AI and IT oversight work.

**['I'm not here to declare victory': Oracle and VA face Senate panel on rising EHRM costs](https://fedscoop.com/oracle-and-va-face-senate-panel-on-rising-ehrm-costs/)** (5 minute read)
Senate Veterans' Affairs members pressed Oracle Co-CEO Mike Sicilia and VA's Neil Evans on Wednesday over why VA's EHR modernization contract ceiling rose from $10 billion to $27 billion, largely without Congress knowing. Sicilia's prepared remarks for today's House Veterans' Affairs hearing say Oracle proposed an $18.5 billion increase this summer that was cut to just under $17 billion and made official in August. Sen. Thom Tillis said "nobody can rationalize a fourfold increase" and bet VA would be back for more than the $37 billion lifecycle estimate. A same-day GAO report says the system was deployed to 13 sites in 2026, with work planned to accelerate. It's the largest civilian IT program under scrutiny this lame duck.

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Special Operations Command seeks AI platform for its acquisition staff](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/special-operations-command-seeks-ai-platform-its-acquisition-staff/417306/)** (2 minute read)
In a sources sought notice posted Tuesday, SOCOM said it wants a commercial platform that "natively integrates super intelligence" across the whole acquisition lifecycle. It would serve as a common workspace for program managers, contracting and agreements officers, contract specialists, business financial managers and leadership. The command calls its current document and approval processes disjointed, and it wants to move away from fragmented point tools and custom software. Only complete solutions are of interest, from a single prime or an integrated commercial team. Responses are due Dec. 18 at 3 p.m. ET, a clear opening for AI-enabled contract-writing and acquisition-workflow vendors.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[MDA Seeks AI Tools to Modernize Legacy Missile Defense Software](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/mda-machine-aided-software-modernization)** (4 minute read)
The Missile Defense Agency's Machine Aided Software Modernization (MASM) topic, under Topic Area 6 of its NOBLE multi-authority announcement, asks for ML, LLM and AI tools to refactor aging Ada, C and early-C++ tactical code into modern C++, Rust or DevSecOps pipelines without changing logic or timing. MDA wants a "data flywheel" that repeatedly refactors, tests and optimizes code, plus AI vulnerability scanning, machine-generated test suites and autonomous agents for multistep translation and regression workflows. Offerors can propose under a BAA, research or prototype OTs, or a CSO, and some routes could lead to production. MDA will review white papers first, expects multiple selections, has set no budget and plans no industry events. It follows the September DoW CIO instruction to use AI for software modernization.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[HII loses $708M Navy contract after Salary-dot-com check inflates its bid](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/hii-loses-708m-navy-contract-after-salary-dot-com-check-inflates-its-bid/417317/)** (3 minute read)
GAO has denied HII's protest of the $708 million Navy Integrated Training Environment II award to Amentum (decision unsealed Monday). The Navy couldn't access the internal system HII used to set wages for unfilled positions, so it checked them against Salary.com's median and raised HII's evaluated cost. That left HII $12.8 million above Amentum, even after Amentum's own cost was raised by $115 million. GAO said the solicitation allowed salary databases, and that HII's location discounts would double-count because Salary.com already adjusts for location. It's a lesson for bidders: cost-realism support has to be something evaluators can actually check.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Google again extends GSA OneGov deal for Gemini](https://fedscoop.com/google-again-extends-gsa-onegov-deal-for-gemini/)** (2 minute read)
GSA's OneGov site now shows Google's Gemini for Government deal ($0.47 per agency, 20% off first-party cloud services) running through Feb. 28, 2027, after it expired Sunday. This is the second extension; the deal was first pushed from Sept. 30 to Nov. 15. Nextgov reports it also keeps 50% off network and Compute Engine egress. Anthropic and OpenAI have moved to consumption-based OneGov pricing: 40% off Claude tokens through Jan. 31, 2029, and 50% off ChatGPT tokens through the end of 2028. Google declined to say whether its long-term deal will follow suit, so agencies face another pricing decision point in late February.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[$350 billion reconciliation bill 'not going to happen': HASC chair](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/350-billion-reconciliation-bill-not-going-to-happen-hasc-chair/)** (4 minute read)
What's new since our Nov. 13 item on Trump's $350 billion demand: outgoing HASC Chairman Mike Rogers said at a Hudson Institute event Wednesday that the full package "is not going to happen." A "skinnied down" bill of around $100 billion could still pass to cover munitions and the budget hole from Venezuela and Iran operations. Appropriators are working "alternatives" to move reconciliation priorities like Golden Dome and multiyear munitions into the base FY27 bill. Senators were briefed the same day by Deputy Defense Secretary Steven Feinberg. With the CR expiring Dec. 11 and Democrats taking over Jan. 3, the Pentagon's AI and autonomy plus-ups now depend on how the $1.1 trillion Senate bill, the $1 trillion House bill and an $87.6 billion war supplemental are combined.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Navy to test new software platform for Project Roundtable](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/18/navy-software-platform-project-roundtable/)** (3 minute read)
The Department of the Navy Rapid Capabilities Office picked GovSignals, under a commercial solutions opening from NIWC Pacific, to prototype a platform where vendors submit and update information on their technologies and get feedback from Navy officials. It will include a public, non-CAC intake that preserves vendors' IP. The system logs every engagement, maps each offering's Technology Readiness Level, and gives Navy offices real-time readiness dashboards. The Navy says it can then send demand signals earlier "so no time is wasted developing unwanted products." For nontraditional vendors, this could become a key front door to Navy rapid-capabilities work.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Air Force Materiel Command Issues Data Strategy & Implementation Plan](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/afmc-data-ai-strategy-implementation-plan)** (3 minute read)
AFMC published its Data and Super Intelligence Strategy, signed by Commander Lt. Gen. Linda Hurry, along with a four-phase implementation plan. Each phase is mapped to the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and overseen by weekly working groups and a monthly governance board. The strategy adopts the VAULTIS data standard and DoW's 2021 responsible-AI guidance. It follows the Air Force's April AI and data strategies and its move to GenAI.mil as the enterprise generative AI platform. AFMC also contracts for much of the service's AI acquisition, so its data rules will shape what vendors deliver.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[DOJ's top IT official wants to 'tear down' legacy systems](https://fedscoop.com/justice-department-cio-nikki-collier-legacy-systems-ai/)** (4 minute read)
At FedTalks, Justice Department CIO Nikki Collier said "reality set in last week with legacy systems." She said she would rather tear down old systems than keep spending to patch them, while acknowledging there will be setbacks. She pointed to the Bureau of Prisons, which moved inmate records to a cloud platform in August and began a major radio replacement last month. Her message to vendors was to show what's possible and name the providers in their ecosystem.

**[Energy Department leans on AI to harden the grid, military installations](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/technology-main/2026/11/energy-department-leans-on-ai-to-harden-the-grid-military-installations/)** (4 minute read)
DOE CESER Director Andrew McClure said at AFCEA Bethesda's Energy, Infrastructure and Environment Summit that AI is helping find grid vulnerabilities and model interdependencies, but warned that inference models without defensive capabilities could make the grid less secure. DOE's new Project Armor works with DoD's Energy, Installations and Environment office to map vulnerabilities at critical installations under DoD's broader Fortress initiative. Argonne is training GridAI, which it calls the first foundation AI model for interconnected grid systems, on data from three national-lab supercomputers. Grid security is becoming a joint DOE–DoD AI mission.

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[Major AI platforms generated viable plans for terrorist attacks, GAO finds](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/major-ai-platforms-generated-viable-plans-terrorist-attacks-gao-finds/417305/)** (4 minute read)
In covert testing this spring, GAO investigators used common jailbreak techniques to get ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Grok to produce viable plans for a mass-casualty IED attack and an attack on the electrical grid, including targets, locations, timing and methods. Each platform took under 12 hours, and outside experts verified the plans could work. GAO withheld which model produced which plan and the techniques used, and it made no recommendations, calling the findings a point-in-time, non-statistical assessment. Sen. Maggie Hassan, who requested the work and is expected to chair HSGAC next year, said AI safety legislation will be an early priority and floated pre-release safety testing. Expect this report to anchor the incoming Democratic majority's AI safety push.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [GAO-27-108814, Artificial Intelligence: Covert Testing Used Common Platforms to Create Viable Attack Plans](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108814)*

**[Security officials tell Congress tech outages are not malicious](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/security-officials-tell-congress-tech-outages-are-not-malicious/417312/)** (4 minute read)
At a House Homeland Security worldwide-threats hearing Wednesday, DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, DNI Jay Clayton and FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia said last week's flight groundings, Microsoft outages and banking disruptions stem from routine technical issues. Clayton has tied the NSANet outage, still causing problems Wednesday, to patching major PHP vulnerabilities found last month. Two people familiar told Nextgov that most of the broader outages appear linked to rapid AI-driven patching. Mullin said DHS will convene an industry call and suggested vendors notify customers before big patches. The story is shifting from "who attacked us" to whether AI-speed patching is outrunning change management.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
