# Federal AI Daily: Wednesday, November 25, 2026

**TL;DR:** Hackers breached an unclassified CIA/ODNI open-source intelligence system, and investigators believe ARTEX, an AI tool that finds and breaks into systems on its own, was used. Schumer accused Jensen Huang of violating export control law as Senate Banking set a public Dec. 3 hearing with the reinstated Nvidia CEO. And two dozen House Democrats want answers by Nov. 30 on why the FAA took its AI air traffic tool nationwide before a 90-day validation period ended.

## White House & Executive Action

**[Intelligence chief Jay Clayton to lead White House cyber office in interim capacity](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/intelligence-chief-jay-clayton-lead-white-house-cyber-office-interim-capacity/417397/)** (3 minute read)
Update to last week's firing of National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross: instead of Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies serving as acting director, as first reported, DNI Jay Clayton will run the Office of the National Cyber Director on an interim basis. Davies will be a senior adviser in ONCD while she seeks Senate confirmation for the permanent job. That gives Clayton a third major portfolio on top of the intelligence community and the administration's AI coordination. He takes over as agencies face questions about this month's outages at NSA, the FAA and Microsoft, and now a breach of a CIA/ODNI system (below). With Democrats taking the Senate Jan. 3, Davies' confirmation timeline is uncertain.

## Congress & Legislation

**[Schumer accuses Nvidia CEO of violating US export control law](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/schumer-accuses-nvidia-ceo-of-violating-us-export-control-law-5048879)** (3 minute read)
New development a day after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang: Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Huang "shipped extremely advanced chips to China, violating US export control law." The top Democrats on four House committees (Pallone, Meeks, Raskin and Garcia) wrote to Trump asking what he knew about Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese agent and requesting records of all administration communications with Nvidia's board. Republicans are pushing too: Sen. Dave McCormick called for stronger export controls, and Sen. Kevin Cramer said "we might even have to put a babysitter on Jensen Huang." Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott set a public hearing with Huang for Dec. 3, two days after his closed-door session with three House panels. Expect export control language to be fought over in the NDAA and to come back once Democrats hold the gavels in January.
*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Ranking Members Larsen, Carson, House Democrats to FAA: SMART Rollout Must Be Safe and Transparent](https://democrats-transportation.house.gov/news/press-releases/ranking-members-larsen-carson-house-democrats-to-faa-smart-rollout-must-be-safe-and-transparent)** (2 minute read)
New development on the FAA's AI routing tool, which T&I Democrats asked GAO to audit in October: Reps. Rick Larsen, André Carson, Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, April McClain Delaney and 20 other Democrats wrote to FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford on Nov. 24. The FAA expanded SMART (Strategic Management of Airspace Routes and Trajectories) to air route traffic control centers nationwide starting Nov. 10, without notifying Congress, before a planned 90-day validation period in the National Capital Region was finished, and without clear safety and performance metrics. The letter also cites reports that the FAA may have retaliated against employees who raised safety concerns. Reuters reports the system comes from Air Space Intelligence under an $875 million, 12-year contract, and that the lawmakers want answers by Nov. 30. It's the clearest test yet of how Congress will oversee AI in safety-critical federal operations.
*Source: House Transportation and Infrastructure Committee Democrats · Reliability Score: 5/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[FEMA Seeks AI Tools for Fraud-Risk Proof of Concept](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/fema-seeks-ai-tools-for-fraud-risk-proof-of-concept/)** (2 minute read)
FEMA's Fraud Investigations and Inspections Division, working with its procurement office, posted an RFI (70FA3127I00000002) on Nov. 17 for commercial anti-fraud and entity-risk modeling tools, ahead of a possible competitive, multi-vendor proof of concept. The agency lists AI, machine learning, predictive analytics, rules engines, entity resolution, anomaly detection, graph analytics, identity and business verification, and OCR as possible approaches, and wants tools that keep false positives and false negatives low and run in cloud, hybrid, API-based or batch environments. Vendors would not screen live cases or make grant eligibility or adverse-action decisions. Responses are due Dec. 4 at 3 p.m. ET. Expect results to shape how FEMA vets business applicants for its grant programs.
*Source: MeriTalk · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[ACC3 awarded US Air Force AFMC Agentic AI Platform support contract](https://orangeslices.ai/acc3-awarded-us-air-force-afmc-agentic-ai-platform-support-contract/)** (1 minute read)
Newly released award data show ACC3 International, an 8(a), service-disabled veteran-owned small business, won a two-year, $4,528,637.52 contract (FA860426CB016) to deliver an "Agentic AI Platform" for Air Force Materiel Command, funded through HQ AFMC A4. The record lists one bidder. DoD can delay award details for up to 90 days, so the award itself may be older. It's another small sole-bid buy that puts agentic AI into a logistics and sustainment organization, the same pattern as this week's Army GenAI sandbox award to Deloitte.

## Defense & National Security

**[Questions about DOD's frontier AI organization linger after Trump-ordered rebrand and website redesign](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/24/trump-ai-rebrand-pentagon-super-intelligence-office-sio/)** (6 minute read)
Since the Chief Digital and AI Office became the Super Intelligence Office and moved from AI.mil to SI.mil, its public leadership page lists only Cameron Stanley and principal deputy Andy Mapes, and earlier versions named more officials. This week Pentagon spokespeople declined to confirm who serves as Maven Smart System program director or to describe other senior positions. Former DoD chief experience officer Savan Kong said "who runs a program like Maven isn't a minor detail." Stanley says existing programs, solicitations and partnerships continue "uninterrupted under the new banner." For vendors and Hill staff, the practical problem is knowing who is accountable for the department's most widely used AI programs during the transition.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Intel community to expand unclassified capabilities to boost OSINT](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/intelligence-community/2026/11/intel-community-to-expand-unclassified-capabilities-to-boost-osint/)** (5 minute read)
At an AFCEA DC luncheon on Nov. 19, ODNI's OSINT executive Jason Barrett said the intelligence community is planning "a pretty significant expansion of capability in the unclassified space." The current workflow pulls public and commercial data into classified networks for analysis, which is increasingly strained because "the value and the volume of data is increasingly on the low side." Barrett said the IC Data Consortium launched in May and other contract vehicles "have largely taken care of access" to commercial data, so the problem now is speed and awareness. DoD's Cameron Stanley said open-source data gets commanders answers faster without relying on constrained government networks. The breach of a CIA/ODNI OSINT system reported the same day shows the security stakes of moving more of this work onto unclassified systems.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[HHS unveils new FOIA portal, pilots AI to speed up record reviews](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/open-datatransparency/2026/11/hhs-unveils-new-foia-portal-pilots-ai-to-speed-up-record-reviews/)** (3 minute read)
On Nov. 24, HHS launched "Radical Transparency," a searchable portal that replaces its online FOIA reading room and pulls together tens of thousands of records from FDA, CDC, NIH and CMS. It disclosed a human-in-the-loop AI pilot for FOIA processing at the same time. According to the HHS fact sheet, the AI ingests records, flags duplicates and proposes privacy and trade-secret redactions, and it processed 1,102 documents in eight hours and applied 148,813 redactions. HHS says every suggestion is approved by a human analyst and reviewed records have met a less-than-5% error rate. Ohio State FOIA expert Margaret Kwoka cautions that HHS must be transparent about how it chooses what to publish. It's one of the most specific productivity claims yet for generative AI in federal records work, and other agencies with FOIA backlogs will notice.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [HHS Launches 'Radical Transparency' Website (HHS press release)](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-launches-radical-transparency-website-government-records.html)*

**[VA secretary: EHR was not fully compliant with accessibility law 'at the time of acquisition'](https://fedscoop.com/va-secretary-ehr-section-508-compliance-oracle-cerner/)** (4 minute read)
In a letter to House Veterans' Affairs ranking member Mark Takano, released Nov. 24 as part of a batch of committee correspondence, VA Secretary Doug Collins acknowledged that "VA was aware at the time of acquisition" that the Cerner (now Oracle) EHR was not fully Section 508 compliant. He said VA chose the same core platform as the War Department to keep care continuous for service members moving to the VA. The admission comes a week after Oracle and VA defended raising the contract ceiling to nearly $17 billion from the original $10 billion, and as a 2024 accessibility lawsuit continues. For federal IT buyers, it's a pointed reminder that a contract clause doesn't satisfy an agency's own Section 508 obligations.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[CIA, ODNI unclassified intelligence system hacked](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/cia-odni-unclassified-intelligence-system-hacked/417438/)** (4 minute read)
The CIA and ODNI found a breach of an unclassified system used to analyze public and commercially available information. One source says the matter is resolved and classified systems were not affected, and it's unclear who did it or whether data was taken. The New York Times, which first reported the intrusion, says NGA was also affected. Investigators believe ARTEX was used, an AI tool built by a Chinese engineer known as "Autumn-27" that automatically finds and breaks into systems and has become popular with criminal hackers. Two people say the same tool was recently used in a low-impact intrusion into DoD's Cyber Defense Command. Experts note that an OSINT system can reveal what analysts are tracking, and with it U.S. intelligence priorities and gaps. It follows the FBI NCIC and USDA breaches and is the most concrete federal case yet of AI-enabled offensive tools.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Recent tech outages raise concerns over how agencies, businesses maintain critical systems](https://www.nextgov.com/modernization/2026/11/recent-tech-outages-raise-concerns-over-how-agencies-businesses-maintain-critical-systems/417424/)** (6 minute read)
Former federal cyber officials warn that AI-accelerated vulnerability discovery is pushing agencies and companies to patch faster than they can test, a pattern behind this month's NSA, FAA and Microsoft disruptions. The NSA's NSANet came back Friday after a three-day outage that DNI Clayton tied to patching PHP flaws researchers found with AI. The FAA's Nov. 13 nationwide ground stop followed work replacing older telecom links, and Microsoft blamed a months-old software flaw. Former CISA deputy cyber chief Matt Hartman said "the race to make systems more secure can inadvertently make them less reliable." Expect more pressure for testing, rollback and backup requirements in federal patching directives.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
