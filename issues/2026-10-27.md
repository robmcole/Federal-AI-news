## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Pricing leak taints $1.9 billion Air Force contract award, GAO says](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/10/two-wrongs-dont-make-right/416815/)** (3 minute read)
GAO sustained Fluor's protest of the $1.9 billion Eglin Operations and Maintenance Services II award to the Strategic Test Solutions joint venture, after the Air Force accidentally disclosed pricing data to one bidder and then tried to fix it by releasing the data to everyone. STS cut its price by $72 million after the leak, dropping below Fluor, and GAO said the Air Force's finding that the leak had no impact "strains credulity" because even STS couldn't quantify it. The Air Force now has to reevaluate the leak's impact and make a new award decision, or take another of GAO's recommended remedies. It's a pointed reminder that giving every bidder leaked data doesn't cure the original disclosure.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Navy RFI is looking for firms with experience supporting large ERP systems](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/10/navy-rfi-looking-firms-experience-supporting-large-erp-systems/416827/)** (2 minute read)
An Oct. 26 Navy RFI seeks a prime contractor for systems engineering and integrated logistics support across the life cycle of Navy ERP, a system with more than 70,000 users, needing at least 15 full-time equivalents. Respondents must show they can staff and fully transition within 30 days, give three prime past-performance examples on DOD systems engineering work (each over 20,000 users, 15+ FTEs, three-plus years), and have an adequate accounting system. Responses are due Nov. 2, and subcontractors won't be considered.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CBP Requests Industry Input on Secure Data Platform Support for Border Patrol](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/cbp-border-patrol-data-platform-request-for-information)** (3 minute read)
CBP posted an RFI asking how vendors would provide a secure software-as-a-service data platform for the U.S. Border Patrol, covering implementation, daily administration, data integration and pipelines, data governance, security and privacy controls, and three tiers of help desk support. CBP wants configurable commercial products with minimal custom development that can work with existing DHS identity, cloud and data services. Responses are due Nov. 10 by email; CBP says it doesn't plan to award a contract from the RFI itself.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [SAM.gov notice](https://sam.gov/workspace/contract/opp/981dee6f523e4776ac546381be6b2ec9/view)*

## Defense & National Security

**[As Project Gatekeeper goes live, Army plans integration with frontier AI models](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/26/army-project-gatekeeper-goes-live-integration-frontier-ai-models/)** (3 minute read)
What's new since the Oct. 13 announcement: Project Gatekeeper went live Friday, letting vendors with a DOD Common Access Card commit code to the Army's DevSecOps Platform and test it in containerized enclaves against Army enterprise standards, with access to expand to the public through GSA's Login.gov. The Army also plans to add top frontier AI models to the environment to strengthen security tools, aggregate reporting and prioritize fixes, though it didn't say which models. The goal is to turn the months-long authority-to-operate process into near-real-time feedback, so vendors' code is already vetted before they're on contract.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Smack Technologies lands new deal for AI prototype to enhance joint military campaigning](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/26/smack-technologies-ai-prototype-special-operations-command/)** (3 minute read)
Texas startup Smack Technologies will prototype its "Omega" AI planning and decision-support platform with SOCOM under an Other Transaction agreement worth up to $7.5 million. Omega generates courses of action and lets commanders "repair the plan in seconds," using small domain-specific models that run on-premises at distributed nodes and keep working when communications drop, with third-party LLMs used only in narrow roles. The company, founded by Marine special operations veterans, already has prototyping deals with the Marines, Air Force and Navy and expects production use in the coming months.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DoD's reasons for CMMC pause falling apart in face of RFI responses](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-news/2026/10/dods-reasons-for-cmmc-pause-falling-apart-in-face-of-rfi-responses/)** (6 minute read)
DOD paused the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification program in July for a 75-day review that has now run past 100 days, and documents obtained by Federal News Network show the 997 RFI responses mostly blamed the cost of meeting NIST SP 800-171 security controls, not CMMC assessments. Respondents cited GCC High licensing, dedicated enclaves, inconsistent CUI marking across DOD, and the need for specialized staff. Sources say CIO Kirsten Davies wanted to end third-party assessments, but the data doesn't support that, leaving the final report delayed; part two of the investigation runs today.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[SSA 'corrects' millions of Social Security Number database files](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2026/10/ssa-corrects-millions-social-security-number-database-files/416822/)** (3 minute read)
SSA says it has corrected nearly 15% of active Social Security Numbers, adding 17 million missing death dates using SSA and State Department data and filling in about 25 million missing citizenship records through DHS's SAVE service, with another 8.5 million records with inaccurate immigration status in progress. Commissioner Frank Bisignano called it the first effort of this scale. The National Committee to Preserve Social Security and Medicare warned that SAVE is "incomplete and prone to errors," so its mistakes could now be copied into SSA's files, which many other agencies rely on for identity checks.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[VA completes 2026 EHR system deployments with final two site go-lives](https://www.nextgov.com/modernization/2026/10/va-completes-2026-ehr-system-deployments-final-two-site-go-lives/416823/)** (3 minute read)
The Oracle Health electronic health record went live Saturday at the Cleveland VA Medical Center and the Alaska VA Healthcare System, moving more than 163,000 veterans and 7,800 staff and completing VA's 13-site plan for 2026. VA says 26 hospitals are next in 2027, with the EHR in every facility by 2031. The milestone follows last month's contract extension adding a $17 billion ceiling and three option years, which drew bipartisan pushback from the House Veterans' Affairs Committee.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[NOAA NESDIS Considering Cloud Migration/Modernization Project in FY27](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/noaa-nesdis-cloud-migration-modernization-rick-miner-nwave-adam-nemethy)** (3 minute read)
Rick Miner, NESDIS's acting CIO, said NOAA's satellite service is weighing a combined cloud migration and modernization project in fiscal 2027 to finish work started in 2026, and has no active cloud migration procurement yet. He said it could reuse the approach from the NCEI OneGov buy, where AWS ProServe is moving about 1,000 virtual machines and up to 20 petabytes of environmental data to a FedRAMP Moderate cloud. Cloud vendors should watch for a NESDIS solicitation modeled on that deal.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Records show White House, DOGE involvement in National Science Foundation grant terminations](https://fedscoop.com/records-show-white-house-doge-involvement-in-national-science-foundation-grant-terminations/)** (4 minute read)
FOIA records obtained by Democracy Forward show DOGE affiliate Zachary Terrell recommending NSF grant terminations in April and May 2025, including from congressionally mandated programs and from a list supplied by the Florida governor's office. In a May 19, 2025, message he wrote, "WH just sent me a list of misinformation related grants that should be terminated." That appears to contradict OSTP Director Michael Kratsios's July testimony that the White House isn't involved in which NSF proposals get funded.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[CISA publishes strategy with eye on the next four years](https://cyberscoop.com/cisa-2030-cybersecurity-strategy/)** (3 minute read)
CISA released "CISA 2030: Defend, Harden, Prevail," a 10-page strategy that largely reaffirms its role but commits to start reviewing its core services within 60 days, deliver an implementation and measurement approach within 180 days, and report progress after a year. Acting Director Nick Andersen calls for evicting adversaries from defense-critical infrastructure and shifting away from "threat-agnostic, all-hazards approaches" toward consequence-based prioritization, with objectives on operational technology, software security guidance and emerging tech including AI. It comes as CISA rebuilds after cuts affected roughly a third of its staff, so expect some services to be scaled back, though the plan doesn't say which.
*Source: CyberScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [CISA 2030 strategy (PDF)](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-10/CISA-Strategy-2026.pdf)*

**[Experts temper emergency-level alarms over critical Brotli defect](https://cyberscoop.com/brotli-critical-vulnerability-decompression-library-node2shell-broccoli2shell/)** (4 minute read)
A buffer overflow in Google's Brotli decompression library, CVE-2026-111497 ("Node2Shell" or "Broccoli2Shell"), was found by OpenAI Codex Security and disclosed by Node.js on Oct. 21; it can cause crashes and remote code execution, and Microsoft and Python have pushed emergency patches. Wiz says 91% of cloud environments have vulnerable resources, but researchers say real impact depends on how each product uses the library and no exploitation has been seen. Coming a week after the AI-found php2shell flaws, it shows how AI bug-hunting is producing a steady stream of "emergencies" that agencies have to triage.
*Source: CyberScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Policy proposal would require firms to disclose how they use AI to develop new models](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/policy-proposal-would-require-firms-disclose-how-they-use-ai-develop-new-models/416812/)** (4 minute read)
Americans for Responsible Innovation proposed that major AI developers file quarterly reports with a new Commerce Department office on how much of their model research is automated, and disclose significant changes, such as fully automating a research cycle, within four business days. Commerce could demand more reporting and refer false filings to the Justice Department, with standards drawing on NIST's Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence and a whistleblower program. It's pitched at Jay Clayton's Super Intelligence Force, which so far favors company-led safeguards under the September industry accord.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Texas sues OpenAI for deceiving consumers about safety, seeks sweeping reforms and fines](https://therecord.media/texas-sues-openai-deceiving-customers)** (3 minute read)
Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton sued OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman personally under state deceptive trade practices law, alleging ChatGPT is marketed as safe while its safeguards fail, including for children. Texas seeks up to $10,000 per violation and an injunction requiring OpenAI to bar minors, get court-appointed expert approval of new models, and stop giving tips on suicide, eating disorders and violence. OpenAI said it works with experts and supports "pragmatic AI policies that apply to the entire AI industry." It adds state-level legal risk for a major federal AI supplier while Washington debates its own oversight.
*Source: The Record · Reliability Score: 4/5*
