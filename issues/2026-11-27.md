# Federal AI Daily: Friday, November 27, 2026

**TL;DR:** It's a quiet post-Thanksgiving news day. The White House says it will fold its cyber policy and operations teams into the National Security Council and Homeland Security Council, the first big structural answer to this month's run of federal breaches. Microsoft says Chinese and North Korean state hackers, including Salt Typhoon, have added ARTEX, the AI hacking tool tied to the CIA/ODNI breach, to their toolkits. And Reuters reports that U.S. chip rules and AI labs' anti-distillation measures are already delaying China's next frontier models.

## White House & Executive Action

**[President Trump Unleashes Whole-of-Government Effort to Protect America's Critical Infrastructure](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/11/president-trump-unleashes-whole-of-government-effort-to-protect-americas-critical-infrastructure/)** (5 minute read)
New development on the cyber leadership shake-up we covered Nov. 25: a White House release issued the evening of Nov. 25 says the Office of the National Cyber Director will be "integrated into a reorganized White House operational and policy structure," and that the White House's cyber policy and operations teams will be reorganized under the National Security Council and Homeland Security Council. It also confirms Pentagon CIO Kirsten Davies is the nominee to lead ONCD, says CISA has waived its usual hiring limits to add more than 200 people with "400-plus" more coming, credits OPM's U.S. Tech Force with hiring nearly 500 private-sector engineers, and says DOJ and the FBI will unveil a follow-on to Operation Winter SHIELD next week. Placing ONCD inside an NSC/HSC structure raises questions about how independent the Senate-confirmed office will be. Those questions could come up at Davies' confirmation, along with the FinCEN, CIA/ODNI, FBI and USDA incidents.
*Source: The White House · Reliability Score: 5/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[DHS Announces New 27.1 Small Business Innovation Research Pre-Solicitation](https://www.dhs.gov/science-and-technology/news/2026/11/25/dhs-announces-new-271-small-business-innovation-research-pre-solicitation)** (2 minute read)
DHS Science and Technology released three topics for its FY27.1 SBIR round. The AI-relevant one, DHS271-001, seeks a "Human Digital Twins Verification System" that can tell real people apart from deepfake digital replicas online. Small businesses can put technical questions directly to topic contacts through Dec. 7. Public questions are due Dec. 30 and proposals Jan. 20, 2027 (both 1 p.m. ET). Phase I awards are firm-fixed-price, $175,000 to $265,000 for 5 to 8 months, and Phase II awards run $1 million to $1.5 million over 24 to 36 months. It's a small dollar amount, but it shows DHS treating AI impersonation of real people as a funded research problem rather than just a deepfake-detection one. The full package is on SAM.gov under ID DHSSBIRFY27PRE.

## Defense & National Security

**[Military payday? There might be an app for that.](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-main/2026/11/military-payday-there-might-be-an-app-for-that/)** (4 minute read)
In a Nov. 13 memo, "Modernizing Total Force Pay," published this week, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth directs DFAS to replace the Defense Joint Military Pay System with a cloud-based, self-service joint pay system and a phone app where troops can check pay and request help. Troops will also keep getting paid while errors or record updates are worked out. The memo changes course on years of integrated personnel-and-pay programs: "To date, none of these programs have successfully processed a single payroll," Hegseth wrote. The military services will now modernize personnel systems on their own, while DFAS builds the pay system. DFAS processes more than 132 million pay transactions a year and is asking Congress for more than $216 million for military pay modernization in FY27. That points to new cloud and mobile work as DFAS moves to build it.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [Hegseth Directs Military Pay System Modernization Prioritizing Warfighters (war.gov)](https://www.war.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/4660118/hegseth-directs-military-pay-system-modernization-prioritizing-warfighters/)*

**[Exclusive-US chip curbs, distillation crackdown threaten Chinese AI model development, sources say](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusiveus-chip-curbs-distillation-crackdown-threaten-chinese-ai-model-development-sources-say-5052154)** (5 minute read)
Reuters reports that Z.AI (Zhipu) has missed its planned November release of its roughly 3-trillion-parameter GLM-5.5 model, and that the launch could slip into the first quarter of 2027 or later. Moonshot AI internally expects the gap between major releases to double from six months to a year or more if it loses overseas computing, which could push Kimi K4 to around mid-2027. Two things are behind the delays: Commerce's remote-access rule, in effect since Nov. 19, which requires licenses for overseas data-center computing above roughly 750 H100s' worth, and OpenAI's and Anthropic's anti-distillation safeguards. Reuters also cites an FT report that Nvidia told manufacturing partners to stop shipping servers to the four Southeast Asian countries the rule covers. It's early evidence the rule works as intended, and it gives the administration a talking point going into the Dec. 3 Huang hearing.
*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[FCC signs off on SpaceX's plan to put up to 1 million data center satellites into space](https://www.theverge.com/science/1042278/spacex-fcc-starmind-ai-space-data-centers)** (2 minute read)
On Nov. 25 the FCC's Space Bureau authorized SpaceX's "Starmind" constellation of up to 1 million AI chip-equipped satellites that would operate as orbital data centers. It's the first approval the agency has granted for a full constellation of AI satellites. SpaceX must launch at least one satellite by 2033 and reach the full constellation by 2040. The FCC deferred SpaceX's request to dispose of satellites above 600 km in "graveyard" orbits rather than letting them burn up in the atmosphere, citing insufficient information. Federal regulators are now approving AI compute infrastructure beyond terrestrial data centers, a point to watch as the administration pushes to keep AI buildout in the U.S.
*Source: The Verge · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [FCC Space Bureau Order and Authorization, DA 26-1273](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-1273A1.pdf)*

**[NIST facial recognition identification tests reopen with new entry rules](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202611/nist-facial-recognition-identification-tests-reopen-with-new-entry-rules)** (2 minute read)
NIST's Face Recognition Technology Evaluation (FRTE) 1:N, which closed to submissions Oct. 30 for technical and policy updates, reopens Monday, Nov. 30, with new datasets and stricter entry rules. New entrants must first pass FRTE 1:1 with a false non-match rate of 2.5% or less on the Visa-Border dataset at a false match rate of one in a million, and templates must generate in under two seconds on a specified processor. NIST is also resetting the participation clock so existing participants can submit right away instead of waiting the usual four months. FRTE rankings are a common reference point in government biometric procurements, so the higher bar will likely narrow the field of vendors that can cite 1:N results.
*Source: Biometric Update · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[Microsoft: China's Salt Typhoon hackers are using Artex](https://therecord.media/microsoft-china-salt-typhoon-using-artex-ai-tool)** (3 minute read)
New development on the CIA/ODNI breach we covered Nov. 25: in a Nov. 25 report, Microsoft said Chinese state groups Salt Typhoon and Crystal Typhoon, North Korea's Onyx Sleet and Pearl Sleet, and financially motivated actors have added ARTEX to their tools. ARTEX is an open-source AI agent framework that automates reconnaissance, credential attacks and web-app exploitation, and Microsoft says it can go "from reconnaissance to persistent, privileged access to a target organization in a matter of minutes." The original developer pulled the code in October, but forks continue to circulate, and China's ThreatBook now sells an "ARTEX Pro." Microsoft says the most reliable way to spot ARTEX is behavioral: bursts of varied, machine-speed requests from a single source. Microsoft did not attribute any specific Salt Typhoon campaign to the tool, but the report ties a nation-state telecom hacker to the same AI tooling reportedly used against the CIA, NGA and ODNI, which raises the stakes for the FBI's earlier ARTEX advisory and agency patching.
*Source: The Record by Recorded Future News · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [What defenders need to know about ARTEX (Microsoft Security Blog)](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/25/what-defenders-need-to-know-artex-agentic-ai-framework-threat-actors-abuse/)*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Small Firm on $151B SHIELD Contract Pitches AI to Help Golden Dome Operators](https://www.military.com/small-firm-on-151b-shield-contract-pitches-ai-to-help-golden-dome-operators)** (6 minute read)
Aptima, one of more than 2,400 holders of the Missile Defense Agency's $151 billion SHIELD IDIQ, told Military.com that early SHIELD opportunities have mostly been network capacity and infrastructure work. The small business is waiting for task orders on decision support and human-machine teaming. Aptima already works with MDA on DR WATSON, a tool that finds relevant information across distributed sources, and uses "compound agentic" systems to flag AI hallucinations, missing context and unreliable sourcing before operators act. The company also catalogs 14 categories of AI problems. Golden Dome leaders have called command and control the program's "secret sauce" and are targeting operational capability by summer 2028. For small firms on SHIELD, AI decision support for operators looks like the opening, but the orders haven't come yet. Aptima has not announced a SHIELD task order.
*Source: Military.com · Reliability Score: 4/5*
