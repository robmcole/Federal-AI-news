## Congress & Legislation

**[House Democratic Committee Leaders Request Frontier AI Companies Report Misalignment & Safety Incidents to Congress Within 48 Hours](https://democrats-science.house.gov/news/press-releases/house-democratic-committee-leaders-request-frontier-ai-companies-report-misalignment-and-safety-incidents-to-congress-within-48-hours)** (2 minute read)
Ranking Members Frank Pallone (Energy and Commerce), Zoe Lofgren (Science) and Jamie Raskin (Judiciary) sent letters Wednesday to Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceXSI asking for a full accounting of known misalignment and safety incidents by Nov. 11, notice within 48 hours of any new incident, and within 24 hours if critical infrastructure is involved. They argue the administration's reliance on voluntary disclosure "will not suffice" after a rise in reports of models taking unintended actions during testing. It's a minority request with no legal force, but these are the labs agencies are buying from through OneGov, and it previews what incident-reporting legislation could look like.
*Source: House Science Committee Democrats · Reliability Score: 5/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[DHS sunsets its Acquisition Planning Forecast System](https://fedscoop.com/dhs-apfs-shut-down-4site-acquisition-planning-forecast-system/)** (3 minute read)
DHS will stop posting procurement forecasts to APFS on Oct. 30, and forecasts won't be viewable until a new landing page goes up Nov. 5; existing APFS subscriptions will start sending automatic notifications again Dec. 9. The replacement is a tool called 4SITE (Forecasting System for Industry Tracking and Engagement), which the Coast Guard flagged in August. Contractors who plan bids off DHS forecasts, and watchdogs who use them to track ICE projects, will have a gap of roughly a week with no view of upcoming buys.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DHS Plans Up to $100M Biometric Identity Services Support Contract](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/dhs-biometric-identity-services-support-contract)** (4 minute read)
A DHS acquisition forecast published Tuesday shows a follow-on contract worth $50 million to $100 million for multimodal biometric examination, identity management support, accuracy and quality analysis, and data analysis, using government-provided software. AVER is the incumbent. DHS expects to release the solicitation Dec. 1 and award in the second quarter of fiscal 2027.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Leidos secures $99 million OTA for cyber operator dashboard](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/10/leidos-secures-99-million-ota-cyber-operator-dashboard/416883/)** (2 minute read)
U.S. Cyber Command awarded Leidos a $99 million other transaction agreement to keep developing a single environment where cyber operators plan, coordinate and assess missions, which Leidos says has cut some planning cycles to under 24 hours. The work began as DARPA's Target Acquisition and Continuous Monitoring System under Kudu Dynamics, which Leidos bought for $300 million in May 2025, and the award marks its transition from DARPA to CYBERCOM. It's Leidos's second CYBERCOM win this week after a $160 million operations planning contract.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Army designates 2 additional 'permissive' sites for commercial vendors to test their drone and counter-drone tech](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/28/army-test-range-access-commercial-vendors-drone-technology/)** (3 minute read)
Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle added Indiana's Camp Atterbury and Muscatatuck Training Center to the Permissive Test Range Expansion Initiative, which already includes Camp Grayling, Dugway, West Cibola Range, Camp Shelby and AMTEC in Morocco. The effort, pushed by former Secretary Dan Driscoll, aims to cut the 12 to 18 months it can take a company to get range time for drones and counter-drone systems. More open ranges mean faster paths for small vendors to prove autonomy and C-UAS tech in front of Army buyers.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Army C5ISR Center Tests Aided Target Recognition for Drone Missions](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/army-c5isr-center-aided-target-recognition-uas)** (4 minute read)
The Army's C5ISR Center tested its AI-based aided target recognition (AiTR) with an operational unit, using soldier feedback to adapt it for drone missions, and is pairing it with the Aviation and Missile Center's drone-teaming and autonomy software ahead of a live flight test at Yuma Proving Ground. The models are trained to spot hidden or camouflaged targets in degraded conditions, and the center is working with industry to integrate AiTR into commercial drone platforms and payloads.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[U.S. military building resilience against emerging OPSEC risks from automated surveillance networks](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/28/us-military-opsec-risks-automated-surveillance-networks/)** (4 minute read)
U.S. Transportation Command officials told DefenseScoop that commercial AI-enabled license plate readers, cloud-connected cameras and tracking tools from companies like Flock Safety, Axon and Motorola Solutions create an operational security risk, since adversaries could exploit them to trace troop and logistics movements at scale. One official compared the networks to "a picket line" of observers along routes. It's a sign that the same commercial computer vision agencies buy for public safety is becoming a force-protection concern.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[ICAM, Cloud and AI: Five Insights Into the VA's $10.7B FY27 IT Systems Budget](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/veterans-affairs-va-fy27-it-systems-budget-icam-ai-cloud-ehr)** (5 minute read)
VA is requesting $10.7 billion for IT systems and electronic health record modernization in fiscal 2027 under the theme "Ready. Secure. Faster." Highlights include $330 million for mission support investments such as the FMBT and iFAMS financial and acquisition systems (up $37 million, or 13%), plus increases for identity and access management, application hosting, and decision intelligence and automation. It's a map of where VA IT dollars, and contractor opportunities, are headed next year.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[NASA, Google test AI-powered medical robot for deep space missions](https://www.nextgov.com/emerging-tech/2026/10/nasa-google-test-ai-powered-medical-robot-deep-space-missions/416874/)** (2 minute read)
NASA's Human Research Program, Johnson Space Center and JPL worked with Google Public Sector on a proof-of-concept robot that handles cargo and equipment and can provide medical support, such as fetching an ultrasound, without waiting on mission control, since Mars communication delays can reach 44 minutes round trip. It runs Physical Intelligence's open-source Pi0 model on Google Cloud and hit an 85% baseline success rate at locating and moving a medical bag in lab demos. It builds on the Crew Medical Officer Digital Assistant NASA and Google built earlier.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[OPM staffing losses are still straining agency operations, watchdog finds](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/agency-oversight/2026/10/opm-staffing-losses-are-still-straining-agency-operations-watchdog-finds/)** (6 minute read)
OPM's inspector general named staffing losses a top management challenge for fiscal 2027 after the agency's workforce shrank by a third last year. The OIG warns that the CIO's office is taking on IT modernization, in-house tech work and AI adoption with fewer people, risking more cyber exposure and delays. Retirement Services still has customer service problems, though the claims backlog fell from more than 65,000 in February to about 15,000 in August thanks to the digital retirement platform.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[DHS personnel leak exposes contact details and sensitive job information](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/dhs-personnel-leak-exposes-contact-details-and-sensitive-job-information/416901/)** (3 minute read)
DDoSecrets on Wednesday published searchable records on more than 41,000 current and former DHS employees and contractors, including names, phone numbers, emails, job titles and internal group memberships across ICE, CBP, CISA and other components, some tied to surveillance, forensics and undercover work. A hacktivist group calling itself the Department of Peace says it got the data through an Okta single sign-on service used by DHS; Okta says it has no evidence its systems were compromised, and DHS says it's "aware of a potential breach." Nextgov/FCW corroborated parts of the data, which appears partly historical, and it raises doxxing and phishing risks for the people listed.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DHS bans Swiss cyber firm from federal systems](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/dhs-bans-swiss-cyber-firm-from-federal-systems/)** (3 minute read)
A FASCSA exclusion and removal order signed by Secretary Markwayne Mullin, dated July 31 and set for Federal Register publication Oct. 30, bars Acronis AG and its affiliates from civilian agency procurements and requires agencies to remove its products within 180 days. DHS says Acronis's cyber and backup tools "have privileged or root access" to customer systems and pose significant risk, the first public rationale after the intelligence community banned the company last year and GSA pulled it from its schedules. It's DHS's second such order in two weeks after DeepSeek, so agencies and resellers should check their backup stacks.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[GSA exposed sensitive information to all agency users on Google Drive, OIG finds](https://fedscoop.com/gsa-exposed-sensitive-information-on-google-drive-watchdog/)** (3 minute read)
GSA's inspector general found 116 files with controlled unclassified information shared agency-wide in GSA's Google Drive, including Social Security numbers, vendor proposals with pricing, and building floor plans showing security vulnerabilities. It blamed poor management of a data loss prevention tool GSA has spent $7.8 million on, which missed "for official use only" markings and can't scan files over 15 megabytes. CIO David Shive agreed with all seven recommendations and said nothing was exposed outside GSA, but vendors whose proposals sit in that drive will want to know.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[FEC takes down electronic filing platform after attempted hack](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/fec-takes-down-electronic-filing-platform-after-attempted-hack/416866/)** (2 minute read)
The Federal Election Commission took several filing systems offline, including filing search and report validation tools, after Reuters reported it was targeted in a cyberattack. The FEC doesn't run elections, but the outage days before the midterms blocks the 24- and 48-hour disclosure reports on political spending. A Tuesday night update said a fix was being put in place.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Researchers uncover Volt Typhoon's covert, ephemeral network](https://cyberscoop.com/volt-typhoon-silent-train-covert-ephemeral-network-black-lotus-labs/)** (4 minute read)
Lumen's Black Lotus Labs says the China-backed Volt Typhoon group has used "Silent Train," a constantly rotating chain of hijacked home routers, IoT devices and enterprise gear, to reach hundreds of critical infrastructure networks since late 2023, rebuilding a new chain in under two minutes. Victims it has notified include government entities, defense contractors, telecoms and water, energy and transportation operators. It shows the group rebuilt its covert access after the earlier U.S. crackdown on its infrastructure.
*Source: CyberScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[US sanctions Russians, North Koreans for laundering IT worker earnings](https://therecord.media/north-korea-russia-sanctions)** (3 minute read)
Treasury sanctioned two Russian nationals accused of laundering millions in crypto for North Korean IT workers in Russia, along with several North Koreans and the First Financial and Technical Development Center, which Treasury says helped workers use AI to get hired at U.S. companies. Six IT workers named have earned at least $750,000 since 2025. Federal contractors hiring remote technical staff remain a target of these schemes.
*Source: The Record · Reliability Score: 4/5*
