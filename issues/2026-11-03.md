## Congress & Legislation

**[Industry groups urge Congress to renew cyber threat-sharing law through defense authorization](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/industry-groups-urge-congress-renew-cyber-threat-sharing-law-through-defense-authorization/416985/)** (2 minute read)
In a Nov. 1 letter to House and Senate Armed Services leaders, 35 industry groups, including the U.S. Chamber of Commerce, Business Software Alliance and American Bankers Association, asked NDAA negotiators to keep the House bill's provision extending the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 through 2035. The law's liability protections for companies that share threat data with agencies like the FBI and NSA lapsed during last year's shutdown and have since survived on short extensions, the latest of which runs out Dec. 11. The Senate NDAA has no long-term extension, and HSGAC Chairman Rand Paul wants a provision barring officials from pressuring social media companies before he'll agree to one. The groups argue that AI now lets attackers find and exploit flaws "at machine speed," so another lapse would chill the private-sector reporting agencies rely on.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Musk is the target of a new Democratic bill to curtail political donations from federal contractors](https://www.govexec.com/oversight/2026/11/musk-target-new-democratic-bill-curtail-political-donations-federal-contractors/416980/)** (3 minute read)
Sen. Chris Van Hollen and Rep. Robert Garcia, the House Oversight ranking member, said they'll introduce the Close the Campaign Cash-for-Contracts Loophole Act. Federal contracting companies already can't give to parties, committees or candidates, and the bill would extend that ban to their officers, affiliates and anyone holding at least a 5% stake, from the start of contract negotiations until one year after the contract ends. Politicians would have to turn improper contributions over to the government rather than refund them. The bill is aimed squarely at Elon Musk, who has spent nearly half a billion dollars backing Republicans in the midterms while SpaceX and his other firms hold billions in federal contracts, and is endorsed by the Brennan Center, Public Citizen and CREW. If passed, it would reach executives and major shareholders at many large AI and defense vendors, not just Musk.
*Source: Government Executive · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[ATI seeks emergency order to end year-long ban from federal contracting](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/companies/2026/11/ati-seeks-emergency-order-end-year-long-ban-federal-contracting/416965/)** (3 minute read)
Tribally owned ATI asked Judge Ana Reyes of the U.S. District Court for D.C. for a 14-day temporary restraining order and a preliminary injunction lifting its SBA suspension from all FAR-based contracting and removing its SAM.gov exclusion. SBA suspended ATI in October 2025, a day after James O'Keefe released a video ATI says was heavily edited and featured a part-time data-entry worker. SBA lifted the 8(a) suspension in June after an administrative judge found no effort to back up the claims, but extended the broader FAR suspension for six more months on Oct. 21. ATI says it has laid off nearly all 67 employees and lost close to $130 million in awarded contracts and a projected $500 million in prospective work, and argues SBA denied it due process. The case tests how far SBA can go in sidelining a contractor on unverified third-party allegations.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Leidos challenges $572M NORAD contract that went to Amentum](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/leidos-challenges-572m-norad-contract-went-amentum/416966/)** (1 minute read)
Leidos filed a GAO protest of the Space Force's $572 million, seven-year NISSC III award to incumbent Amentum, an Alliant 2 task order made in September to support the NORAD Cheyenne Mountain Complex. Leidos argues the Space Force didn't consider price in its best-value decision, made unsupported assumptions about proposal revisions, held unequal discussions and misjudged Amentum's incumbent past performance. The work covers system administration, vulnerability management, maintenance and test support for the system that tracks adversary launches and attacks. GAO expects to rule by Jan. 29.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Government watchdog backs Air Force decision to limit contract bids to cleared companies](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/air-force/2026/11/government-watchdog-backs-air-force-decision-to-limit-contract-bids-to-cleared-companies/)** (3 minute read)
GAO denied Sawdey Solution Services' protest of an Air Force advisory and assistance solicitation covering arms control treaties, countering weapons of mass destruction and the nuclear enterprise, which required bidders to hold an active top-secret facility clearance when they submit proposals. Sawdey argued it couldn't get the clearance without a contract in hand and that its subcontractor already had one. GAO found "no basis to question the reasonableness" of the requirement and said relaxing it would expose the Air Force to more risk. It's the same chicken-and-egg clearance problem DIU's new CLASS ACT program is meant to ease by letting some firms start facility clearances before the government is ready to buy.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[FEMA Issues RFI to Modernize Cloud-Based Supply Chain Management System](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/fema-rfi-supply-chain-management-system)** (3 minute read)
FEMA posted an RFI on SAM.gov last Wednesday for commercial software-as-a-service and support to run, maintain and modernize its disaster Supply Chain Management System, which tracks inventory at seven distribution centers and three annex warehouses. The planning targets are 400 concurrent users and 5,000 shipment transactions a day, with a possible surge to 1,000 users, plus integrations for EDI, RF handheld devices, Android apps and APIs. The anticipated contract would have a 12-month base and four option years and cover operations, cybersecurity and authorization support, help desk, training and disaster surge capacity. Responses are due Jan. 4, 2027.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

The Pentagon has moved some cyber operators from the Defense Cyber Defense Command at Fort Meade to Hawaii, assigning them to DISA-Pacific under a concept called Cyber Defense Forces Pacific. Brig. Gen. Chinese hackers have targeted military critical infrastructure in places like Guam, and CYBERCOM has been pushing more cyber defense money into the region.

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[EPA watchdog launches audit of agency's AI practices](https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/2026/1102/artificial-intelligence-epa-audit-inspector-general)** (3 minute read)
EPA's inspector general disclosed on Oct. 19 that it's auditing the agency's compliance with OMB's AI directive, which requires a public AI use-case inventory, extra pre-deployment scrutiny and independent review for "high impact" AI, and a human appeals process. The audit follows Christian Science Monitor reporting that AI tools meant to speed approvals for new pesticide and chemical uses weren't in EPA's inventory, and experts said two disclosed high-impact tools, including one that prioritizes hazardous waste inspections, were out of compliance. EPA didn't respond to the Monitor's questions. Its FY27 request cuts the budget 52% but includes more than $202 million for AI, including $3.5 million to use AI in pesticide permitting. No completion date was given, and the findings will be published online. Other agencies with gaps in their AI inventories should take note.
*Source: The Christian Science Monitor · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Medicare's AI-assisted coverage review pilot hit early snags, documents show](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/medicares-ai-assisted-coverage-review-pilot-hit-early-snags-documents-show/416978/)** (6 minute read)
FOIA records released by the Electronic Frontier Foundation show CMS raised concerns with four of the six vendors in its WISeR model, which has used AI in six states since January to help clinicians decide whether Medicare will cover services like skin substitutes and nerve stimulator implants. Genzeon (New Jersey), Virtix Health (Washington) and Zyter (Arizona) received requests for information over case backlogs, slow or blank determination letters and questionable denials. Innovaccer (Ohio) got two corrective action plan requests because its provider portal wasn't working. CMS says no corrective action plans are active, all vendors are in compliance, and a six-month performance report is coming. The vendors blame a compressed launch timeline. WISeR is the most visible test of AI in federal benefit decisions, and hospitals and lawmakers already say it delays care.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Bisignano says IRS will use 'super intelligence' to speed service, collections](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/bisignano-says-irs-will-use-super-intelligence-to-speed-service-collections/)** (3 minute read)
IRS CEO Frank Bisignano, who sits on the White House super intelligence task force, told a CERCA conference on Oct. 29 that the IRS will use the technology "to the max" in 2027 to speed taxpayer service and raise collections. "The things that would have taken five years will get done in one year," he said, promising responsible use. A March GAO report found IRS AI use cases jumped from 10 in August 2022 to 126 by June 2025, but warned that 2025 staff cuts, including 63 AI staff in its research and analytics group, plus weak inventory and investment governance could undercut the effort. The IRS agreed with all eight GAO recommendations. Vendors should expect the IRS to push hard on AI for service and enforcement next year, even with fewer in-house AI staff.
*Source: Thomson Reuters Checkpoint · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[USDA seeks to curb fraud through EBT card security requirements](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/it-modernization/2026/11/usda-seeks-to-curb-fraud-through-ebt-card-security-requirements/)** (5 minute read)
A USDA proposed rule would require state SNAP agencies to issue EBT cards with chip and NFC technology, require SNAP retailers to accept them, and set EBT operating standards that USDA can update as payment technology changes. USDA's inspector general found $322 million in benefits replaced between October 2022 and December 2024, likely because of fraud, and projected another $233 million in losses over FY25 and FY26 without chips. Eight states already issue chip cards, and California says chip, tap and other measures cut theft 83%. The Aspen Institute warns that most states can't fund the upgrade, especially now that the One Big Beautiful Bill Act has raised their share of SNAP administrative costs to 75%. Comments are due Dec. 22.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[US order to purge Acronis tied to years of scrutiny over Russian ties](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/us-order-purge-acronis-tied-years-scrutiny-over-russian-ties/416970/)** (4 minute read)
Update on the DHS order covered Oct. 29: two former officials told Nextgov/FCW that the Acronis removal order grew out of foreign ownership, control or influence reviews dating back to the first Trump administration. Those reviews focused on whether Russian developers were still working in the Swiss firm's codebases and whether it had mitigated the supply chain risk. Neither official said reviewers found evidence of backdoors to Moscow. Secretary Markwayne Mullin's July 31 directive followed a Federal Acquisition Security Council recommendation and gives civilian agencies until Jan. 27 to remove all Acronis software. Unlike Kaspersky, Acronis isn't banned from the private sector. Agencies have often bought Acronis through resellers, and contractors that use it could be next.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Tech companies brace for AI-driven election misinformation](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/tech-companies-brace-ai-driven-election-misinformation/416983/)** (3 minute read)
OpenAI and Anthropic have set up election "war rooms," according to Politico and Semafor, and Meta has activated its Election Operations Center for today's midterms. An Oct. 22 Brennan Center test of six image generators found ChatGPT "significantly improved" its refusals since May, while Google's Gemini and Meta AI became more willing to make convincing fake election images, and Flux, Runway and Grok showed little change. Sen. Mark Warner, who with other Democrats pressed the companies last month to answer election questions only from "highly authoritative sources," welcomed the efforts. Today's vote is a live test of the companies' voluntary safeguards, and the Brennan Center's results suggest they're uneven.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[New Report: Trump Administration Has Benefited Elon Musk's Businesses Across 25 Federal Agencies](https://www.democracydefendersfund.org/news/new-report-trump-administration-has-benefited-elon-musks-businesses-across-25-federal-agencies/)** (3 minute read)
An Oct. 30 report from the advocacy group Democracy Defenders Fund counts at least $10 billion in new federal contracts to Musk's companies since he left DOGE in May 2025, on top of an estimated $6.3 billion during his tenure. The largest are two Pentagon Golden Dome awards to SpaceX, worth $4.2 billion and $2.3 billion, which the report says used requirements so tailored that some rivals didn't bid. Other items include $946 million from NASA for three ISS missions and a potential Defense Department deal worth up to $200 million for xAI. It also lists 25 agencies that eased enforcement or took other actions benefiting his businesses. The tally is a partisan group's analysis, but it is already fueling Democrats' contractor donation bill.
