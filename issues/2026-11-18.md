# Federal AI & Tech Digest — Wednesday, November 18, 2026

**TL;DR:** The administration is weighing a rule that would make U.S. companies publicly disclose their use of Chinese AI models. Nvidia says it has stopped shipping licensed H200-class chips to China, two days after Commerce's remote-access rule. NSA's classified NSANet network went down during what the agency calls routine maintenance.

## White House & Executive Action

**[Exclusive: Trump administration weighs forcing US firms to disclose Chinese AI use](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusivetrump-administration-weighs-forcing-us-firms-to-disclose-chinese-ai-use-5035455)** (3 minute read)
Three people told Reuters that Treasury and the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy have discussed requiring U.S. companies to post on their websites which Chinese AI models they use. Airbnb is one example: it runs its customer-service agent on Alibaba's Qwen. The idea is a "name and shame" approach that discourages reliance on open-weight models like Qwen, DeepSeek and Kimi without new restrictions that could anger industry or Beijing. It's unclear whether the idea is gaining traction or would become a formal rulemaking. The report comes as House Homeland Security and China Select Committee chairs Garbarino and Moolenaar investigate corporate use of Chinese AI, and after Reuters reported in September that a U.S. government website used an Alibaba model to search proposed regulations. Agencies and contractors should expect more scrutiny of model provenance in their own stacks.
*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Trump touts Quad initiative to expand AI access in Indo-Pacific](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/trump-touts-quad-initiative-expand-ai-access-indo-pacific/417286/)** (2 minute read)
At the Quad Leaders' Summit in Manila on Tuesday, the U.S., Australia, India and Japan reaffirmed the "Quad SI Accelerator." It is backed by more than $85 million in planned technical and financial support to coordinate and fund "trusted Super Intelligence (SI) access, deployment, and technical assistance" across the Indo-Pacific. The funding was first announced by Quad foreign ministers on Oct. 31. The leaders also announced "enhanced Quad Cybersecurity Cooperation." At the U.S.-ASEAN dinner, Trump said the U.S. has "the world's most powerful SI ecosystem." It's the export-promotion side of the administration's AI strategy, paired with this week's tighter chip controls, as Trump heads to China for talks with Xi and Putin.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Some in cyber industry have qualms about Trump's latest CISA nominee](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/some-cyber-industry-have-qualms-about-trumps-latest-cisa-nominee/417275/)** (5 minute read)
What's new since we covered the nomination Nov. 10: Palantir CISO Michael LoSapio's confirmation hearing is set for Thursday (Nov. 19) before Senate HSGAC, alongside several other nominees, including GAO pick Eric Ueland. Several industry sources told Nextgov/FCW they knew little about him ("an unknown, a ghost," said one). Some had hoped acting director Nick Andersen would get the job after he steadied an agency that has lost roughly a third of its staff. Others welcomed a permanent leader with deep technical experience, and the White House called him uniquely qualified. Thursday is senators' last chance to question him before Democrats take control of Congress in January, and CISA's private-sector relationships are central to the job.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Senate Democrats demand hearing for NSF director pick before committee vote](https://fedscoop.com/senate-democrats-demand-hearing-nsf-director-before-committee-vote/)** (5 minute read)
Senate HELP is scheduled to vote today on James O'Neill's nomination as NSF director without having held a public hearing. Sens. Maria Cantwell and Tammy Baldwin have asked Chair Bill Cassidy to hold one first. O'Neill, a technology investor who served at HHS and CDC, would be the first NSF director in the agency's 76 years who isn't a scientist or engineer. NSF has had no permanent leader since April 2025, and acting head Brian Stone plans to leave at the end of the year. NSF funds much of the nation's basic AI research, so whoever leads it will shape the grants, workforce and oversight disputes that Democrats plan to press next year.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Alliant 3's second round adds two dozen companies](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/alliant-3s-second-round-adds-two-dozen-companies/417281/)** (2 minute read)
On Tuesday GSA named 24 more awardees on Alliant 3, its next governmentwide IT services contract, bringing the total to 67 of a planned 76. The next selection phase is expected in the first quarter of calendar 2027, and no bidder has been formally eliminated yet. The potential 10-year "Anything IT Anywhere" vehicle covers systems engineering, cloud, cybersecurity, data and software development, including help adopting emerging technology. Agencies have obligated roughly $52.5 billion through Alliant 2, which sunsets in June 2028, so Alliant 3 is set to become a major channel for agency AI and modernization task orders.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Trax, Army settle AI evaluation dispute](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/trax-army-settle-ai-evaluation-dispute/417272/)** (2 minute read)
The Army has paid Trax International's legal fees (amount undisclosed) to settle a fight over how it described its use of an AI tool called "Fast Track" to evaluate proposals for a $450 million White Sands Missile Range support contract. Trax lost its Court of Federal Claims protest in September, and the award to Southwest Range Services (SA-Tech, Amentum and EWA Warrior Services) stands. Along the way, though, the Army admitted it had left the AI tool's use out of the administrative record and kept changing its account. Judge Carolyn Lerner then issued a sealed show-cause order that could have led to sanctions. It's an early warning for agencies using AI in source selection: protest records must document exactly how the tool was used.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Army kicks off "manufacturing rodeo" with focus on small drones](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/army-kicks-off-manufacturing-rodeo-with-focus-on-small-drones/)** (3 minute read)
A solicitation published Monday says the Army will select vendors to build 400 Group 1 one-way attack drones costing no more than $3,000 each, plus 20 ground control stations each, by March 1. The government will furnish batteries, motors, MK118 lethality kits and frames. Ten percent of the drones are due by Feb. 15, and DCMA teardown inspections will check that no components come from outside the continental U.S. The drones will compete at the third Drone Dominance Gauntlet in March, and winners unlock priced options for up to 10,000 drones on shared government-vendor coproduction lines that will be stress-tested with simulated supply disruptions. Initial responses are due today.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Pentagon elevates AI as a "fundamental requirement" for cybersecurity staff training](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/17/pentagon-ai-requirement-cybersecurity-training/)** (4 minute read)
At FedTalks on Tuesday, DoD CIO official Mark Gorak said AI will now be a required part of training for all 79 defined cybersecurity roles, up from 76 last year. He also said the department is moving to skills-based hiring that tests candidates on cyber ranges instead of relying on credentials. Gorak said DoD is short about 24,000 people out of a cyber workforce of roughly 240,000, and he announced a cyber workforce summit April 7–9 at National Defense University. Expect AI proficiency to start showing up in DoD cyber labor categories and in contractor staffing requirements.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DISA eyes shift from Pegasus to Arcadia for allied information sharing](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/disa-eyes-shift-from-pegasus-to-arcadia-for-allied-information-sharing/)** (2 minute read)
At DISA's Nov. 16 Forecast to Industry Day, Mission Partner Environment portfolio acquisition executive Nicholas Creswell said DISA will move beyond Pegasus, the Five Eyes information-sharing platform, to Arcadia. Arcadia is a "data-centric ecosystem" meant to integrate with a broader set of allies. It will be assembled from existing components rather than bought as a single program. Work may come through DISA's C3E, cyber or transport portfolios, and solicitations may not carry the Arcadia name. Contractors should watch those portfolios for engineering, operations and sustainment work tied to coalition data sharing.
*Source: MeriTalk · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[FAA details $50B request as lawmaker pushback grows](https://fedscoop.com/dot-faa-funding-request-atc-lawmaker-pushback/)** (4 minute read)
In a Tuesday letter to appropriators, DOT Secretary Duffy, FAA Administrator Bedford and OMB Director Vought requested more than $50 billion for air traffic control modernization. That is $20 billion more than September's ask and far above the $4 billion in the FY27 budget. It includes $10.1 billion for a common automation platform, cloud, telecom, traffic-flow management and workstations, plus $10 billion for five new consolidated en route centers. All Senate Commerce Democrats asked for an immediate pause on deploying more L3Harris-installed Juniper routers after last week's nationwide ground stop. Only 69 of 400 upgraded routers are operational, out of 3,600-plus planned conversions. The suspect router is being analyzed at an L3Harris lab with the FBI on site. This is one of the largest civilian IT modernization asks in years, and it's colliding with oversight of the outage.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Kupor teases department-specific Tech Force tracks](https://www.nextgov.com/people/2026/11/kupor-teases-department-specific-tech-force-tracks/417291/)** (2 minute read)
OPM Director Scott Kupor said at FedTalks that OPM is working on agency-specific U.S. Tech Force tracks, modeled on the NASA track unveiled earlier this year, for agencies that need specialized cyber or engineering talent. The two-year program has onboarded about 500 engineers toward a first cohort goal of 1,000. Tech Force is also starting to experiment with AI on the front end of hiring, including AI-run structured interviews candidates can complete on their own time. That follows OPM's August memo pushing AI to speed hiring, and the program is the one targeted by Rep. Walkinshaw's Keep Federal Tech Talent Act.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[Classified NSA communications network down, people familiar say](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2026/11/classified-nsa-communications-network-down-people-familiar-say/417277/)** (2 minute read)
Six people familiar with the matter told Nextgov/FCW that NSANet, NSA's primary network for top-secret data, went down Tuesday. The outage slowed analysts' work and forced employees to rearrange their arrivals at Fort Meade. Four of the sources said there were no immediate signs of a cyberattack and that it may be linked to software upgrades. An NSA spokesperson called it "temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance" and said mission-critical capabilities remained operational. It follows a week of major outages, including the FAA ground stop, Microsoft service disruptions and banking problems, which is increasing pressure on agencies to prove the resilience of systems undergoing modernization.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Nvidia stops shipping US-approved AI chips to China](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-stops-shipping-usapproved-ai-chips-to-china-5035204)** (2 minute read)
On Tuesday's earnings call, Nvidia interim CEO Colette Kress said the company sold $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper-generation data center products, a family that includes the H200, to China in the quarter ended Oct. 25. She said Nvidia is "no longer shipping" them, and guidance assumes none. She didn't say why or whether the halt is permanent. The H200 arrangement allowed sales to 10 approved Chinese companies with a 25% tariff, after Trump eased licensing in January following his summit with Xi and Huang's lobbying. Beijing has since restricted imports, and Reuters previously reported about 600,000 H200s sitting in Hong Kong. Combined with Monday's BIS remote-access rule, the halt effectively shuts the main licensed channel for advanced U.S. AI compute to China as Trump arrives for talks with Xi.
*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*
