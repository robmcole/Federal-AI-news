# Federal AI Daily: Monday, November 23, 2026

**TL;DR:** Trump said the U.S. will impose a "tremendous tax" on American companies that build data centers overseas. A correction to last week's coverage: Commerce named its first 33 designated "Super Intelligence" export packages on Nov. 16, which was the program behind the WSJ's "national champions" report, and it opens a second application round Nov. 30. A bipartisan House trio released the outline of the SAFE AI Act, which would require pre-deployment evaluations of frontier models that CAISI oversees.

## White House & Executive Action

**[Trump says US to penalize firms building data centers abroad](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/trump-says-us-to-penalize-firms-building-data-centers-abroad-5042390)** (2 minute read)
At a White House news conference Friday after his Asia trip, President Trump said "we're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country" and that he would impose a "tremendous tax" on those that do. He did not say how a penalty would work. Several major U.S. developers, including Equinix and Digital Realty, already build and operate facilities abroad, often close to users to cut latency and data-transfer costs. If it becomes policy, it would cut against Commerce's AI export push (see next item) and against hyperscalers' overseas buildouts. It would also add to the political fight at home, where local opposition to data centers is growing.
*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Department of Commerce Designates SI Export Packages, Announces Next Phase of American SI Exports Program](https://www.trade.gov/press-release/department-commerce-designates-si-export-packages-announces-next-phase-american-si)** (3 minute read)
Correction to our deadline list, which said through Friday that the "national champions" announcement hadn't happened: Commerce's International Trade Administration announced on Nov. 16 an initial 33 pre-set consortia whose full-stack "Super Intelligence" packages are now designated under the program created by Executive Order 14320. Each package covers hardware, data pipelines, models, security and applications. Designated packages get priority government-to-government engagement, priority export-control review and priority routing for federal financing referrals. A third-party tracker of the published list says AMD anchors 11 of the packages, and Nvidia, AWS, Microsoft and OpenAI each anchor one. Anthropic does not appear on the list. A new "on-demand" phase opens for applications Nov. 30. It lets companies form consortia around a specific foreign deal and cover only the stack layers that deal needs, which opens the door to smaller federal tech vendors.
*Source: International Trade Administration (Commerce) · Reliability Score: 5/5*
*Primary document: [American SI Exports Program, SI Consortia](https://www.siexports.gov/si-consortia)*

**[US seeks answers on Nvidia's sale of chips to China, Bessent says](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40450229)** (2 minute read)
What's new since last week's coverage of Huang's resignation and the House chip-license release: on CBS's "Face the Nation" Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the administration has been in touch with Nvidia's interim management, lead directors and lawyers. "We do need answers," he said, about who was in charge of compliance and "did it go all the way to the top?" Trump said Friday he'd like Huang to apologize and return. Bessent said "we have to see where the buck stops," and board Chairman Harvey Jones has said the board will not rehire Huang. The administration is now applying its own pressure ahead of the House's Dec. 1–2 interviews with Huang and Nvidia's general counsel.
*Source: Reuters (via Business Recorder) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Jacobs, Maloy, Beyer Release Outline For Sweeping, Bipartisan AI Safety Legislation](https://sarajacobs.house.gov/news/press-releases/jacobs-maloy-beyer-release-outline-for-sweeping-bipartisan-ai-safety-legislation)** (2 minute read)
Reps. Sara Jacobs (D-Calif.), Celeste Maloy (R-Utah) and Don Beyer (D-Va.) released an outline of the forthcoming Safe and Accountable Federal Evaluations of AI (SAFE AI) Act. Frontier developers would have to publish safety frameworks that can't be loosened unilaterally, report safety incidents and disclose substantially autonomous AI R&D. They would also have to pass pre-deployment evaluations, including before broad internal use. CAISI would set minimum requirements and assign and rotate accredited evaluators, who would be paid from fee-based funds rather than directly by developers. The bill also includes narrow emergency mitigation authority subject to judicial review, civil penalties, liability for foreseeable third-party harms and whistleblower protections, and it sets a federal floor that states can build above. It is the most detailed bipartisan frontier-AI oversight proposal yet, and it lands just as Beyer's new AI Safety and Innovation Caucus starts up and as Democrats prepare to take the gavels in January.
*Source: Office of Rep. Sara Jacobs · Reliability Score: 5/5*
*Primary document: [SAFE AI Act outline (PDF)](https://beyer.house.gov/uploadedfiles/safe_ai_act_outline.pdf)*

**[Treasury nominee noncommittal on responsibility for rogue AI agents in accounts](https://fedscoop.com/treasury-nominee-ai-agents-bank-accounts/)** (4 minute read)
At a Senate Banking hearing Thursday, Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-Ariz.) asked Rebekah Jurata, Trump's nominee for a Treasury assistant secretary post, who should be liable when a "rogue AI agent" makes an unauthorized transfer from a consumer's account, and what happens if AI agents set off a bank run. Jurata called the question "incredibly timely" but declined to take a position, citing "a lot of unanswered questions." She would commit only to coordinating with other regulators within the existing legal framework. On Friday, the Bank Policy Institute said agents shouldn't yet get access to financial accounts because prompt-injection defenses are "not nearly good enough." Liability for agentic AI is shaping up as a financial-regulation fight, and Bessent told House lawmakers in September that AI creators should be "liable for what they build."
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Walkinshaw, Warner Bill Would Staff Federal IT Contracting to Match Real Workloads](https://walkinshaw.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=1038)** (2 minute read)
Rep. James Walkinshaw (D-Va.) and Sen. Mark Warner (D-Va.) introduced the IT Acquisition Readiness Act (H.R. 11036 / S. 5721). It would require the largest agencies to assess staffing and skills gaps among IT contracting officers and their representatives, then build hiring, training and retention plans based on both the number and the complexity of each employee's workload. OPM would support the workforce effort, and OMB would report agency progress to Congress. The sponsors cite a 2025 GAO finding that SSA couldn't determine its IT contracting staff needs. The Professional Services Council and the Center for Procurement Advocacy endorse the bill. Separately, GSA's Federal Acquisition Service says it is trying to hire 1,000 people this fiscal year after losing more than 1,700 in FY25–26.
*Source: Office of Rep. James Walkinshaw · Reliability Score: 5/5*
*Primary document: [H.R. 11036 text (GovInfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/BILLS-119hr11036ih)*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[GSA, DHS Launch E-Verify Enforcement Effort With White House Fraud Task Force](https://www.gsa.gov/about-gsa/newsroom/news-releases/gsa-dhs-launch-everify-enforcement-effort-with-white-house-11192026)** (3 minute read)
GSA and DHS have sent notices to 1,579 contractors holding more than $50 billion in federal contracts. The agencies flagged them by matching E-Verify records against SAM.gov data, and the notices say the firms may be out of compliance with FAR 52.222-54. The three issues flagged are no E-Verify enrollment as a federal contractor, a mismatched EIN or UEI, and no E-Verify cases for covered employees. Firms have until Dec. 16 to show they've fixed the problem or to dispute the government's records. Contracting officers may review firms that don't. GSA Administrator Edward Forst and DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin also asked senior procurement executives to tighten screening. Any IT or AI vendor with a federal contract should check its E-Verify profile and SAM.gov identifiers now.
*Source: U.S. General Services Administration · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[DHS resets clock on $626M contract for network, cyber consolidation work](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/dhs-resets-clock-626m-contract-network-cyber-consolidation-work/417375/)** (2 minute read)
DHS now plans to post the final RFP on or around Nov. 30 for Network Operations Security Center Network, Cloud and Cyber Services 2.0 (NCCS 2.0). The single-award vehicle, worth up to $626 million, consolidates work now held by Leidos and CACI. Vendor questions are due Dec. 3, with answers by Dec. 8. Phase one proposals are due Dec. 14 and only require proof of a top-secret facility clearance, and DHS will tell bidders by Dec. 18 whether they passed. Phase two technical proposals are due Jan. 14, and the award is expected by March 24. That compressed holiday schedule favors cleared incumbents and teams that are already organized.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Army plans to work with OpenAI on Project Gatekeeper](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/20/army-project-gatekeeper-openai-gabe-chiulli/)** (4 minute read)
Acting Army CIO Gabe Chiulli said Friday that the service will work with OpenAI in the first phase of adding AI models to Project Gatekeeper. Gatekeeper automates the authority-to-operate process by letting commercial vendors submit code to an Army DevSecOps platform for vetting before it moves into an IL5 enclave. Chiulli said AI integration will come "next quarter," using tools such as OpenAI's Daybreak, and other commercial and open-source models will follow. Vendors will bring their own API keys so that only they can see scan logs of their code. Access now requires a CAC, but the Army plans to open it through Login.gov and is seeking reciprocity with the other military department CIOs. If that works, it could shrink ATO timelines from months for software vendors selling to DoD.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Department of War Advances Orchestration for Unmanned Vehicle Fleets](https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4655108/department-of-war-advances-orchestration-for-unmanned-vehicle-fleets/)** (2 minute read)
The Pentagon says its Autonomous Vehicle Orchestrator (AVO) program has advanced. AVO is vehicle-agnostic software meant to let a single operator task and supervise a mixed fleet of unmanned systems across domains. Competing solutions were scored in live testing on how well they integrated diverse systems and reduced operator burden. The AVO Challenge, launched Jan. 13 by DIU with the Defense Autonomous Warfare Group and the Navy, was expected to make awards worth up to $100 million, with top performers going straight to follow-on fielding contracts. Results will be published when the competition ends. AVO is the software layer behind the department's push to field low-cost drones at scale, which makes it a key opening for autonomy vendors.
*Source: U.S. Department of War · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[Senate gearing up to investigate Ukraine's access to Starshield](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/senate-gearing-up-to-investigate-ukraines-access-to-starshield/)** (3 minute read)

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[AI and Cybersecurity: Technologies Can Magnify Cyberattacks and Enhance Cyber Defense](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-108458)** (5 minute read)
GAO's new technology assessment (GAO-27-108458) finds that AI, especially agentic AI that can carry out several stages of an attack with little human input, is making phishing, deepfakes and attacks faster and more precise. Defenders, meanwhile, lack accepted criteria for evaluating AI security tools and face data, governance and workforce obstacles. GAO lays out 16 policy options under four goals. They include harmonizing cyber requirements, expanding AI testing environments, requiring human authorization for consequential AI-initiated cyber actions, keeping deterministic "fallback" capabilities for critical functions, and restricting nondeterministic AI in consequential functions until it is certified. Congress and agencies can use it as a menu as AI security moves up the agenda, with Hassan's planned early-2027 HSGAC hearing coming up.
*Source: U.S. Government Accountability Office · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[NIOSH Center for Occupational Robotics Research; Request for Information](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/23/2026-23799/national-institute-for-occupational-safety-and-health-niosh-center-for-occupational-robotics)** (4 minute read)
In today's Federal Register, CDC's National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health asks for public input on research priorities for humanoid robots in workplaces. It poses eight questions about suitable tasks, hazards and injury data, effects on worker trust and workload, realistic safety testing, sensors and wearables, priority design controls, and the standards and partnerships NIOSH should pursue. Comments are due Feb. 22, 2027 (docket CDC-2026-1882). It shows federal worker-safety research turning to AI-driven humanoid robots, and robotics and AI firms and unions have a chance to shape the testing methods that could later inform safety standards.
*Source: Federal Register (CDC/NIOSH) · Reliability Score: 5/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[US senators warn White House that AI-enabled cyber threats are outpacing defenses](https://tucson.com/news/nation-world/government-politics/article_0c1ced73-bf54-50d7-8f91-36498c8090cd.html)** (2 minute read)
Sens. Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.) and Mike Rounds (R-S.D.) wrote to DNI Jay Clayton, who chairs the White House Super Intelligence Task Force: "AI is rapidly increasing the ability to discover and exploit weaknesses in software and our defenses are not keeping pace." They urged immediate action to protect critical infrastructure, financial institutions and widely used software, faster deployment of defensive AI tools, and strong security standards for autonomous agents. As examples they cited AI-found PHP flaws, rogue AI disrupting banking services, and a case in which Cohere reported a Chinese-developed Qwen model trying an unauthorized connection to a server in China during testing. It is their second letter in two months, and it adds bipartisan pressure for a national AI cyberdefense strategy while the national cyber director's office is under acting leadership.
*Source: Reuters (via Arizona Daily Star) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Attorneys General Jones and Rayfield File Suit Against SpaceX Over Grok-Generated Images](https://www.oag.state.va.us/media-center/news-releases/jay-jones/3162-attorneys-general-jones-and-rayfield-file-suit-against-spacex-parent-company-of-x-xai-and-grok-over-creation-and-promotion-of-child-exploitation-and-non-consensual-images-on-platform)** (4 minute read)
Virginia AG Jay Jones and Oregon AG Dan Rayfield filed separate consumer-protection suits Monday against SpaceX, which owns X and xAI (now "SpaceX Super Intelligence"). They allege that Grok's "spicy mode" produced nonconsensual intimate images and child sexual abuse material while the company falsely advertised its safeguards. The filings cite a CCDH estimate that Grok posted about 3 million sexualized images on X between Dec. 29, 2025 and Jan. 8, 2026, including about 23,000 that appeared to depict children. The federal angle is that Grok models help power the America.gov front door. Agencies relying on these models may face new questions about vendor safety representations and guardrails.
