**TL;DR:** Trump nominated Palantir CISO Michael LoSapio to lead CISA, the agency's first director pick since Sean Plankey withdrew in April · Trump told *60 Minutes* he "doesn't mind guardrails" on AI, his first opening to Democrats on safeguards since the midterms · DOT, DoW, DHS, DOE and DOJ signed a White House-led MOU setting a single process for testing and fielding counter-drone tech at home.

## White House & Executive Action

**[Trump nominates Palantir security chief Michael LoSapio to lead CISA](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/trump-nominates-palantir-security-chief-michael-losapio-lead-cisa/417141/)** (3 minute read)

On Monday, Trump sent the Senate the nomination of Michael LoSapio, Palantir's chief information security officer since September 2024, to be CISA director. CISA hasn't had a Senate-confirmed leader since Jen Easterly left in January 2025, and Nick Andersen has been acting director since February. Previous nominee Sean Plankey withdrew in April after more than a year stuck in a dispute with Sen. Rick Scott over Coast Guard contracting. The Senate calendar is packed with funding and the NDAA, so if LoSapio isn't confirmed this session, Trump would have to renominate him to the incoming Democratic Senate. There, Palantir's DHS work and Sen. Ron Wyden's planned inquiries as Finance chair would bring tougher scrutiny. The winner inherits an agency that has lost about a third of its workforce and is now refilling a few hundred positions.

**[Trump signals openness to AI guardrails after the "blue tsunami"](https://www.cfodive.com/news/trump-signals-openness-ai-guardrails-blue-tsunami/874155/)** (3 minute read)

In a CBS *60 Minutes* interview that aired Sunday, Trump said "I don't mind guardrails" on AI and that he'd consider working with Democrats on safeguards. He added that regulation must not put the U.S. in "a deep second position" behind China, and said AI's existential risks are "not nearly as real as" some claim. That's a change in tone from his Sept. 15 Truth Social post calling AI-danger warnings a "hoax," though he named no specific safeguards or framework. Combined with Democrats' testing and liability agenda covered yesterday, it hints there may be room for a negotiated federal AI bill in 2027 rather than a veto standoff. A Morningstar analyst tied last week's AI-stock selloff partly to the risk of new regulation.

*Source: CFO Dive · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[OMB's Ueland: FAR rewrite "in the middle of being completed" as OMB targets 117 HR and 229 procurement systems](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/omb-pursues-major-overhaul-of-federal-purchasing-rules-government-systems/)** (3 minute read)

Speaking Nov. 9 at the National Academy of Public Administration's conference, OMB Deputy Director for Management Eric Ueland called the FAR overhaul led by OFPP Administrator Kevin Rhodes the "most significant simplification" in decades, noting the FAR hasn't been comprehensively revised since 1984. He described more than 117 federal HR systems and 229 non-defense procurement systems as "completely foolish and counterproductive" and said they're slated for consolidation. Federal websites have already dropped from more than 7,000 to about 5,500 as of August, with further cuts planned through America.gov. Grant-making rules are being revised as well. Contractors should expect fewer, standardized buying and back-office platforms, and changing FAR language, in 2027.

*Source: MeriTalk · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Congress returns to a stack of Dec. 11 cyber deadlines and unfinished AI safety bills](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/congress-returns-slew-cyber-deadlines-and-unfinished-ai-safety-bills/417123/)** (4 minute read)

The lame-duck session started Monday with three authorities expiring Dec. 11: CISA 2015 information sharing, the Federal Cybersecurity Enhancement Act of 2015 (which underpins federal intrusion detection), and the Technology Modernization Fund. The FY27 NDAA, the likeliest vehicle for a 10-year CISA 2015 extension, passed the House July 22 but stalled in the Senate after a failed July 14 procedural vote. A Ramirez–Whitesides amendment to formally authorize the CVE program at CISA didn't make the House text. On AI, pending bills include the Obernolte–Trahan FRONTIER Act (risk disclosures and incident reports), the Lieu–Moran AI Kill Switch Act (shutdown capability plus DHS emergency orders), and the Sanders–Casar superintelligence ban. FISA Section 702 reauthorization may also come up.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Incoming Democratic cyber chairs make CISA restaffing and AI security top priorities](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/dems-point-to-cisa-staffing-ai-security-and-other-cyber-priorities/)** (5 minute read)

Expected House Homeland chair Bennie Thompson said he'll push CISA to speed rehiring after it lost more than 1,000 staff, about a third of its workforce, so it can help infrastructure owners face "AI-enabled cyber-attacks." Expected Senate HSGAC chair Maggie Hassan pledged to "reverse these cuts" and named AI security risks as a priority. Expected Senate Intelligence chair Mark Warner put CISA 2015 reauthorization and MS-ISAC funding at the top of his list and plans to examine the administration's use of private firms for offensive cyber operations. The final CIRCIA incident-reporting rule (72-hour reporting) is expected early next year. Taken with the LoSapio nomination, CISA's direction will be contested from both ends of Pennsylvania Avenue in 2027.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Defense industry fights NDAA "right to repair" data-rights provisions](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/defense-industry-takes-aim-at-right-to-repair-as-ndaa-decision-looms/)** (6 minute read)

Both the House and Senate FY27 NDAA versions would give DoD "default government purpose rights" to technical data and software, letting it use that data for repairs and share it with other contractors. Industry groups warn this would hand trade secrets to competitors. POGO argues it would break vendor lock on sustainment, which makes up about 70% of major programs' life-cycle costs, and points to a 2025 GAO report linking data-rights gaps to higher costs and slower repairs. Armed services leaders must decide in conference this session. The outcome will shape software and IP terms for every defense tech vendor, AI providers included.

*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Air Force buys $26M in Flyways AI licenses for the 618th Air Operations Center, sole-source](https://www.war.gov/News/Contracts/Contract/Article/4645461/contracts-for-nov-9-2026/)** (2 minute read)

Monday's DoW contract announcements included a $26,020,100 firm-fixed-price purchase order to small business Air Space Intelligence Federal (Cambridge, Mass.) for commercial licenses to use and customize its cloud-hosted Flyways AI platform at the 618th Air Operations Center at Scott AFB. The 763d Enterprise Sourcing Squadron at Scott AFB awarded it on Sept. 28. It was sole-source, runs through Sept. 28, 2031, and obligated $5M in FY26 Transportation Working Capital Funds at award. It's a clean example of DoD buying commercial AI as a multi-year license rather than a custom development program.

*Source: U.S. Department of War · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[DISA opens a CSO for a next-gen enterprise ICAM prototype across IL5 and IL6](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/09/disa-enterprise-icam-prototype-zero-trust/)** (3 minute read)

DISA issued a commercial solutions opening Friday and plans to award an Other Transaction Agreement for a modular, cloud-hosted COTS/SaaS "NextGen E-ICAM" covering NIPR (IL5) and SIPR (IL6). The solicitation cites a fragmented directory architecture, "severe latency" in the FedHub interoperability layer, legacy authorization models, and separation-of-duties gaps that create audit weaknesses. Questions are due Nov. 15 and solution briefs Nov. 30. DISA has to deliver enterprise ICAM up to IL6 by September 2027, and DoD's target-level zero-trust deadline is the end of FY2027, so identity vendors have a short window.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CBP seeks drone-as-first-responder dock systems for Border Patrol](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/cbp-rfi-drone-first-responder-suas-dock-systems)** (3 minute read)

A SAM.gov RFI (RFI_DFR_sUAS_2027, posted Nov. 6) asks industry about small drones pre-positioned in automated docks that can launch remotely to track items of interest crossing the border between ports of entry. Responses are due Dec. 18 at 5 p.m. ET. CBP may invite selected vendors to demonstrations at their own expense. The American Security Drone Act bars Chinese-made systems such as DJI's, so this favors U.S. DFR providers already selling to police departments.

*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Five departments sign White House-led MOU to test and field counter-drone tech without endangering aviation](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/trump-administration-deepens-interagency-collaboration-protect-nations-skies-unmanned)** (3 minute read)

DOT, DoW, DHS, DOE and DOJ announced Monday an MOU, led by the White House Task Force to Restore American Airspace Sovereignty, that sets an interagency process for evaluating and deploying systems that detect, track, stop, or take control of drones. The agencies will coordinate with the FAA and FCC on technical requirements, field testing, and data sharing. The FAA will quickly run safety risk assessments and can restrict airspace if more mitigation is needed. The deal builds on the southern-border TFR and the FAA's safety assessment of DoW's high-energy laser. FBI Director Kash Patel said agents seized more than 700 drones during the World Cup. The MOU text wasn't released.

*Source: Federal Aviation Administration · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[Pentagon's GenAI Task Force says its spot-report agent helped deter a World Cup bomb threat](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/military-made-ai-tool-helped-thwart-world-cup-bomb-threat/)** (3 minute read)

Army Capt. Ryan Hetrick, who leads DoD's Generative AI Task Force, said Nov. 6 that an AI agent his team built with the FBI and Coast Guard at the White House cut World Cup Task Force spot reports from about 20 minutes to 90 seconds. He said that time savings helped deter a previously undisclosed June 18 bomb threat at SoFi Stadium. The White House, FBI and local police didn't comment, so the claim rests on Hetrick's account. He also said a tool built with the 101st Airborne cut a cyber-operations workflow from 100 hours to 15 and is now available to cyber officers department-wide.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Army fields launched effects to 11 of 12 divisions; Griffon Aerospace supplies most](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/army-fields-launched-effects-to-11-of-12-divisions-with-griffon-aerospace-delivering-the-majority/)** (4 minute read)

PAE Maneuver Air says launched effects have reached 11 of 12 headquarters divisions and every multi-domain task force, with the last division starting fielding next month. That keeps the Army on track for the end-2026 deadline in Hegseth's 2025 Drone Dominance memo. Officials credited OTAs and DoD's drone marketplace for the speed and singled out dual-use Griffon Aerospace for scaling quickly on soldier feedback. Payloads span ISR, electronic warfare, and kinetic effects. It shows the commercial-marketplace, OTA-first model that is now steering drone and autonomy money.

*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[GSA's TTS now requires daily work reports, and names a new America.gov lead](https://www.nextgov.com/people/2026/11/gsa-tech-shop-asks-employees-submit-daily-updates/417134/)** (2 minute read)

Emails obtained by Nextgov/FCW show Technology Transformation Services employees must email ttsdailies@gsa.gov by 6 p.m. local time each day with what they did, what's next, and any blockers. A message signed by DOGE alum Sam Corcos says "failure to comply may result in adverse action." The same email says U.S. Tech Force fellow Justin Adelman, formerly of the IRS mobile app and Treasury AI projects, now leads America.gov under Corcos' oversight. It's a leadership change for the AI "digital front door" that DOGE's Amy Gleason described last week.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CMS credits 300+ fraud models and AI "war rooms" with $42B in annual savings](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/cms-messick-fraud-prevention-42b-annual-savings-ai)** (3 minute read)

Bethany Messick of CMS' Center for Program Integrity said its Fraud Prevention System runs more than 300 models that monitor providers around the clock and flag unusual activity, producing $42 billion in annual savings against roughly $2 trillion in yearly Medicare and Medicaid spending. AI tools support the Fraud Defense Operations Center and the Medicaid Fraud War Room, launched in April with the White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud. CMS can suspend payments while it investigates, though analysts, not AI, make final payment decisions. It's a large-scale example of production AI tied directly to payment integrity.

*Source: ExecutiveGov (reporting Federal News Network) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[CISA revamps its incident reporting form around MITRE ATT&CK while the CIRCIA rule sits at OIRA](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/cisa-updates-cyber-reporting-form-broader-incident-mandate-awaits-approval/417130/)** (2 minute read)

CISA updated its Incident Reporting Portal questionnaire Monday with more specific questions on malware, hacking techniques, insider threats, and social engineering. It now maps reports to MITRE's ATT&CK framework so analysts can link incidents across organizations. The final CIRCIA rule, which will require reporting substantial incidents within 72 hours and ransom payments within 24, has been under White House review since Oct. 1, after CISA missed its October 2025 statutory deadline. Standardized ATT&CK data will also make AI-enabled campaigns easier to spot across victims.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[ILC Dover pays $13M to settle False Claims Act cyber-compliance case over Army, MDA and NASA work](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/companies/2026/11/aerospace-firm-pays-13m-cover-cyber-gaps-army-mda-and-nasa-work/417126/)** (2 minute read)

ILC Dover LP and ILC Astrospace LLC will pay $13 million to resolve allegations, announced by DOJ on Nov. 3, that they falsely claimed cybersecurity compliance. Specifically: storing CUI with a cloud provider not verified as FedRAMP Moderate, failing to update system security plans, submitting no current NIST SP 800-171 assessments to DoD, and having no plans of action. Former cybersecurity director and whistleblower Philip Root gets $2.35 million. No breach was alleged and there was no admission of liability. It's another reminder that contractors face False Claims Act exposure for cyber-compliance paperwork gaps even when nothing was stolen.

*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Nvidia board probes whether CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by Chinese intelligence](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/explainerwhat-we-know-about-nvidias-probe-into-ceo-jensen-huang-5010580)** (4 minute read)

A committee of independent directors with outside counsel is investigating after a New York Times report that Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang, a former executive of Singapore-based Megaspeed whom U.S. authorities concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. Megaspeed, an Nvidia Cloud Partner, has been under U.S. investigation for allegedly helping Beijing get export-controlled AI chips. Huang told employees Sunday he'd been unfaithful and supports the review. Nvidia says it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were affected, and Reuters couldn't verify the Times' claims. Senior House Republicans have sought briefings, so expect the probe to feed into the chip export-control and AI-security fights in the next Congress.

*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CISA's former Secure by Design leads launch the nonprofit Software Safety Institute](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/cisas-former-secure-design-chiefs-launch-their-own-software-safety-organization/417115/)** (4 minute read)

Lauren Zabierek (CEO) and Bob Lord (chief strategy officer), who ran CISA's Secure by Design push until resigning in April 2025, launched the Software Safety Institute on Monday. It aims to make software safety measurable, something like crash-test ratings for buyers, and to use evidence of product failures to shape incentives for manufacturers and policymakers. CISA's voluntary pledge grew from nearly 70 signers in May 2024 to about 300. Zabierek has proposed to Massachusetts officials that state breach reports be mined for recurring product flaws. With CISA's own capacity cut, independent groups may become the main driver of secure-by-design pressure on vendors selling to government.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
