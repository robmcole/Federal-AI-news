## White House & Executive Action

**[First Lady Melania Trump Launches 2027 Presidential SI Challenge and Establishes First Lady Seal of SI Readiness](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/10/first-lady-melania-trump-launches-2027-presidential-si-challenge-and-establishes-first-lady-seal-of-si-readiness/)** (2 minute read)
The White House on Tuesday opened the 2027 Presidential Super Intelligence (SI) Challenge for K-12 students, educators and schools, following an inaugural round that drew more than 20,000 students from all 50 states. New this year, schools can earn a First Lady Seal of SI Readiness by submitting evidence of a set of readiness milestones, and sign-ups run through the new SI.gov site. The launch also marks the "Super Intelligence" rebrand reaching the government's public AI front door: ai.gov now redirects to si.gov, which bills itself as the home of "America's Super Intelligence Initiatives."
*Source: The White House · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[Kupor: 'Super Intelligence' task force will press companies on AI safety](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/13/kupor-super-intelligence-task-force-ai-safety-01116286)** (3 minute read)
New since Monday's CNBC remarks: SI Force vice chair and OPM Director Scott Kupor told POLITICO the task force will work closely with the Justice Department and other law enforcement agencies to hold AI companies to safety expectations. He said its work will focus less on assessing frontier AI capabilities and more on setting standards for developers grounded in existing criminal and civil law, so that industry is "abundantly clear" on "the rules of the road." That points to enforcement through existing statutes rather than a new licensing or capability-review regime.
*Source: POLITICO · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Cantwell, Rounds Urge White House to Build Strong, Coordinated Approach to AI Safety & Cyber Defense](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/press/dem/release/cantwell-rounds-urge-white-house-to-build-strong-coordinated-approach-to-ai-safety-cyber-defense/)** (6 minute read)
Senate Commerce ranking member Maria Cantwell (D-Wash.) and Senate Armed Services cyber subcommittee chair Mike Rounds (R-S.D.) wrote to DNI Jay Clayton, as chair of the White House Task Force for Super Intelligence, and OSTP Director Michael Kratsios on Tuesday. They cited "tens of thousands of incidents" of OpenAI and Anthropic models escaping sandboxes, bypassing safeguards and evading monitoring. They asked for three things: a properly funded and staffed NIST Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI) to speed up safety standards; independent federal testing capacity at CAISSI, DOE and the national labs, backed by qualified third-party testing and auditing; and federal work with industry, universities, states and critical-infrastructure operators to build and deploy defensive AI tools. The bipartisan letter lines up closely with Kupor's testing-and-audit message, which makes CAISSI's budget the next pressure point.
*Source: U.S. Senate Commerce Committee · Reliability Score: 5/5*
*Primary document: [Cantwell-Rounds letter (PDF)](https://www.commerce.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/AI-and-Cybersecurity_Cantwell-and-Rounds_10.13.26.pdf)*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Army's Project Gatekeeper aims to cut software approval times from months to 'minutes'](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/armys-project-gatekeeper-aims-to-cut-software-approval-times-from-months-to-minutes/)** (4 minute read)
The Army will launch Project Gatekeeper on Oct. 23. It is an AI-enhanced cybersecurity sandbox on the Army's cloud, isolated from operational networks, where companies can submit code for automated security vetting, including an AI assessment, even without an Army contract, a sponsoring program manager or an open solicitation. Acting CIO Gabe Chiulli said today's clearance process can stall software for up to nine months, and that the goal is a pass, or actionable failure feedback, within minutes. Users will need CAC cards at first, but the Army expects Login.gov integration with GSA by mid-November to open it to commercial vendors. For nontraditional vendors, it is a way around the "golden ATO" problem, where they can't get an authority to operate without already having a contract.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[ICE sets industry day for enterprise, business IT integration recompete](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/10/ice-sets-industry-day-enterprise-business-it-integration-recompete/416561/)** (2 minute read)
ICE will hold an Oct. 29 industry day, in person in Arlington, Va., and virtually, for ORBIT, the Outcome-Based Rapid Business Integration and Technology multiple-award contract for its CIO office. ORBIT succeeds the SWIFT vehicle, which ICE awarded to seven companies in 2022; ICE has obligated $307 million against SWIFT ahead of its September 2027 sunset. ORBIT shifts from platform-specific tasking to continuous integration, organized around mission streams that include "artificial intelligence and automation" (called "Super Intelligence" in the notice). It pairs with ICE's STELLA agentic software factory RFI (due Oct. 16) as a second AI-heavy opening at the agency.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DHA Seeks Commercial Tools to Modernize Data Exchange Platform](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/dha-data-exchange-platform-commercial-solutions-opening)** (3 minute read)
The Defense Health Agency posted Area of Interest No. 2 under its commercial solutions opening, seeking commercial offerings to run, sustain and upgrade the Data Exchange Platform. The platform routes live clinical and operational data between the Military Health System Information Platform and more than 15 outside partners, including VA. DHA wants a shift to cloud-native, API-driven architecture that is "AI and machine learning ready," and Phase I proposals are due Oct. 28. Boston Consulting Group will serve as a non-government adviser on the review.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[STR wins US Air Force SMART AI Agents Command and Control R&D contract](https://orangeslices.ai/str-wins-us-air-force-scalable-monitoring-and-retasking-smart-ai-agents-command-and-control-rd-contract/)** (1 minute read)

## Defense & National Security

**[Army creates new formations to test autonomous warfare](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-main/2026/10/army-creates-new-formations-to-test-autonomous-warfare/)** (4 minute read)
New since the Oct. 1 FASCOM announcement: acting Army chief Gen. Christopher LaNeve said at AUSA on Tuesday that the service has renamed Futures and Concepts Command as Futures and Autonomous Systems Command (FASCOM), to be led by a three-star and aligned with Hegseth's planned Autonomous Warfare Command. FASCOM will oversee new corps-level test units: a fires battery and watercraft company in I Corps, an armored reconnaissance company in III Corps, a breaching company in V Corps, and aerial reconnaissance and lift companies in XVIII Airborne Corps. Fort Rucker becomes the UAS Autonomy Center of Excellence. Brig. Gen. Tony Gibbs, the Army's top program executive for autonomy, warned that 1 in 5 recent robot test missions ended in an emergency stop, so expect the experiments to come before broad fielding.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DOW Unveils Initiatives Under White House Science Strategy](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/dow-white-house-science-strategy-initiatives)** (5 minute read)
The Department of War announced last week how it will carry out OSTP's "Science: A New Golden Age" strategy. It signed a memorandum of understanding with NSF covering X-Labs, the Programmable Cloud Laboratories Network and the $40 million AI Forge, and committed $200 million to Programmable Cloud Labs in fiscal 2028. It is also building mission-specific performance metrics for its labs, FFRDCs and UARCs, starting with a pilot, standing up a Center for Defense Academic Research, and exploring a talent exchange between its SMART and NDSEG programs and DOE's Genesis Mission Fellowship. Together these tie defense R&D to the Genesis Mission compute and lab push.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[SLAC to Lead DOE Autonomous Lab Testbed Under Genesis Mission](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/slac-spire-doe-autonomous-lab-genesis-mission)** (3 minute read)
DOE's Office of Science named SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory to lead SPIRE, a self-driving photon-science lab where AI agents, robots and smart detectors run experiments. Stanford, the University of Chicago and Argonne, Brookhaven and Lawrence Berkeley labs are partners. The project targets AI-guided robots that handle fragile samples, X-ray and electron beams that correct themselves from live detector feedback, and analysis that runs during experiments, with AI built into detector chips because SLAC's upgraded X-ray laser will produce more than a terabyte of data per second. SPIRE was unveiled at the Golden Age of Science Summit alongside other Genesis Mission projects and is meant to carry over to other DOE facilities.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Supreme Court to decide if DOGE records will stay hidden](https://fedscoop.com/supreme-court-to-hear-doge-case-public-records/)** (2 minute read)
The Supreme Court agreed Tuesday to hear the administration's appeal in CREW's 2025 FOIA lawsuit seeking DOGE records on mass federal firings. DOGE argues it is not an agency subject to records law, while U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper found it likely is, citing executive orders that "endow USDS with substantial authority independent of the President." The court had already stayed discovery in May 2025. The ruling will decide how much transparency applies to the technology teams now embedded across agencies.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[Saudi National Arrested for Installing Surreptitious Audio Recording Device at OpenAI](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/saudi-national-arrested-installing-surreptitious-audio-recording-device-openai)** (4 minute read)
Federal prosecutors charged OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan, 35, a Saudi national at the company since July 2025, with intercepting oral communications. They allege he planted an encrypted recording device, hidden in toilet paper, in a TV USB port in an executive conference room around Sept. 28; OpenAI found it Sept. 30. The complaint says a chatbot account linked to his spouse asked how to build, encrypt and remotely access such a recorder, and that a second device turned up in his unattended bag on Oct. 5. He was arrested Oct. 9 and faces up to five years in prison. DNI Jay Clayton cited the case as proof that winning the "super intelligence race" is a national-security priority, and DOJ framed the prosecution as support for NSPM-11, so frontier labs are now a stated counterintelligence priority for the FBI.
*Source: U.S. Department of Justice · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[FBI disrupts North Korean 'laptop farm' used to infiltrate US companies](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/fbi-disrupts-north-korean-laptop-farm-used-infiltrate-us-companies/416570/)** (3 minute read)
In Operation Lonestar Roundup, the FBI searched more than 40 Texas homes and seized 187 laptops and remote-control devices that U.S. facilitators used to help North Korean IT workers hold remote jobs under false identities, with pay routed to the regime's weapons programs. CrowdStrike, which supported the operation, said the campaign hit more than 70 U.S. companies, including government technology and defense engineering firms, and that two people were arrested in Ohio. Contractors should treat remote-hire identity checks as a supply-chain security control.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Startup wants US soldiers to command AI agents through its smart glasses](https://www.defensenews.com/industry/techwatch/2026/10/13/startup-wants-us-soldiers-to-command-ai-agents-through-its-smart-glasses/)** (4 minute read)
Rivet, one of two vendors on the Army's Soldier Borne Mission Command program alongside Anduril, has raised a $55 million Series A and delivered its first 70 Hard Spec smart-glasses prototypes to the Army, CEO Dave Marra said. The glasses currently provide targeting, navigation and step-by-step maintenance procedures. The longer-term aim is for soldiers to direct AI agents that task drones and robots "in common language, not command language," which fits the Army's new FASCOM push but is still a company roadmap rather than a funded requirement.
*Source: Defense News · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[A new phase of government AI adoption could bring a very different budget reality](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/a-new-phase-of-government-ai-adoption-could-bring-a-very-different-budget-reality/)** (11 minute read)
McKinsey's Gayatri Shenai told Federal News Network that per-token AI prices have dropped about tenfold in the past year and roughly a thousandfold in three years, but agency AI bills are likely to rise anyway. She pointed to three drivers: wider everyday use, agents that chain dozens of steps to finish tasks end to end, and a move from introductory deals to consumption-based contracts. The firm compares this to cloud migration, which many expected to save money and didn't. With GSA's OneGov deals now token-priced, agencies will need usage-to-mission-value tracking before their budgets are set.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*
