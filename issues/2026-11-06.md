## White House & Executive Action

**[White House NSTM-6: federal funders must buy nucleic acids only from screened suppliers](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/NSTM-6-NASS-Policy.pdf)** (6 minute read)

National Security Advisor Marco Rubio and OSTP Director Michael Kratsios issued NSTM-6 on Nov. 5, the National Policy for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening under EO 14292. Federally funded life-sciences customers must buy synthetic nucleic acids (≥50 nucleotides) and benchtop synthesizers only from adherent suppliers that run NIST-verified screeners, know-your-customer checks, and flagged-order reporting to authorities such as the FBI. Clock starts Nov. 5: NIST screening/benchtop standards in ~90 days (~Feb. 3, 2027), HHS adherent-supplier list in ~160 days (~Apr. 14, 2027), customer NA compliance in ~250 days, benchtop in ~340 days. The memo explicitly frames SI uplift of biology as both opportunity and misuse risk — the clearest SI–biosecurity procurement rule of the administration so far.

*Source: The White House · Reliability Score: 5/5*
*Primary document: [NSTM-6 National Policy for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening (PDF)](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/NSTM-6-NASS-Policy.pdf)*

**[DOJ launches Super Intelligence Task Force to police AI misuse](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-launches-super-intelligence-task-force)** (3 minute read)

The Justice Department announced Thursday the DOJ Super Intelligence Task Force (DOJ-SITF), co-chaired by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss. Attorney General Todd Blanche’s Oct. 13 memo folds the January AI Litigation Task Force into the new body, directs it to organize criminal and civil SI enforcement, set misuse priorities against foreign/state-sponsored actors, TCOs, and fraud, and serve as DOJ’s principal contact with the White House Super Intelligence Force led by ODNI Director Jay Clayton. Components must notify the task force when opening SI-related investigations or seeking charges (except in exigent cases). It hard-wires DOJ into the administration’s SI apparatus as a standing enforcement hub.

*Primary document: [Blanche memorandum establishing DOJ-SITF (Oct. 13, 2026)](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1470291/dl?inline=)*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Chamber of Progress urges Democratic Congress to pass federal AI safety standards](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/tech-policy-coalition-calls-new-congress-set-ai-safety-standards/417077/)** (3 minute read)

In a Nov. 4 memo to House and Senate Democratic leadership (reported Nov. 5), Chamber of Progress VP Koustubh Bagchi called for a national AI transparency and safety framework — published safety frameworks, independent evaluation, enforceable safeguards for the most capable models, critical-incident reporting, and whistleblower protections — building on the bipartisan FRONTIER Act (Obernolte/Trahan) and floating a FINRA-like industry-funded, federally supervised oversight body. The ask lands amid post-midterm Democratic AI pledges and an NBC exit poll finding 56% of voters said AI and data centers were a crucial or major factor in their congressional vote. It is an early industry roadmap for what a blue Congress may try in 2027.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Army and Air Force leaders: AI wins on targeting and logistics, still loses on intel analysis](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/05/where-ai-is-and-isnt-accelerating-military-missions-today/)** (4 minute read)

At Datadog’s Threat Response Forum on Nov. 5, Army intel technical adviser Paul Damchik said automated target recognition has “really earned its place,” compressing training that once took analysts days or months, while automated intelligence analysis “doesn’t exist today” because analysis remains art as well as science. Air Force deputy CIO Keith Hardiman cast AI as an augmenter that pulls people off non-value work in HR and logistics and keeps a “same pane of truth,” not a replacement for human-led ops. The candid split is useful for CDAO, service AI shops, and vendors pitching agentic analysis vs. perception and enterprise automation.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Navy Innovation Unit pipes Silicon Valley AI talent into the Reserve](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/05/navy-innovation-unit-private-sector-technology-talent/)** (3 minute read)

Assistant Navy Secretary for Manpower and Reserve Affairs Ben Kohlmann said the Navy is rolling out an early Navy Innovation Unit that pairs about 15 Reservists already in high-caliber tech jobs with about 10 private-sector founders and CEOs brought in as O-4s/O-5s via direct commission to work hard problems in AI, robotics, cyber, and cloud. A June launch call drew roughly 300 applications for 10 slots. It is a concrete talent experiment for getting “AI natives” inside Navy systems without forcing full-time military careers.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[America.gov phase two: Login.gov will gate Medicare, SSA, and passport flows](https://fedscoop.com/america-gov-integration-login-national-design-studio-cio/)** (4 minute read)

National Design Studio CIO Mary Bati told Fed Gov Today that Login.gov integration is “one of the biggest things” for America.gov phase two — moving the Sept. 29 chatbot from a Q&A switchboard to authenticated in-platform services such as Medicare and Social Security, with Login as the mandated identity gate under the launch EO. Passport applications are expected to start coming live ~December 2026, with most Americans able to apply online by end of 2027; NDS/GSA host the doorway while State still adjudicates. The real test shifts from chat answers to secure transaction delivery.

*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CBP and Commerce say AI coding and USAi APIs are compressing modernization timelines](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/ask-the-cio/2026/11/ai-tools-are-helping-cbp-commerce-hasten-it-modernization/)** (8 minute read)

At ATARC’s AI Summit, CBP Cargo Systems Program Director Thomas Mills said AI coding and data-extraction tools are delivering about a 40% acceleration moving ACE legacy components (C/C++ stored procedures, parsers) into modern stacks, with two-week sprints and human review of generated code. Commerce acting CTO Matt Passos said Census, NIST, and NOAA systems are using APIs into GSA’s USAi so mission experts can clean “dumb data,” including capital-planning TCO analysis, while GSA USAi architecture lead Ryan Palmer said more than 20 agencies are on the platform and urged renting models across FedRAMP clouds to limit lock-in.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[USCIS seeks AI-enabled contact-center recompete (ECX2); RFI due Nov. 12](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/uscis-ai-enabled-contact-center-recompete-rfi)** (3 minute read)

USCIS posted an RFI for Enterprise Customer Experience 2, a FedRAMP Moderate cloud contact center to handle ~15 million calls a year across ~1,500 agents (room for 3,000), with Salesforce/ServiceNow links and AI IVR targets of >95% intent match, <700 ms response, and at least 75% (goal 85%) of calls kept off agents. AI add-ons must align with DHS Directive 139-08 and the NIST AI RMF; responses are due Nov. 12 at 8:00 a.m. ET. Forecast value is $50–100M, with solicitation expected ~March 1, 2027, as the current EIS arrangement expires July 2, 2027.

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[CISA, FBI and HHS warn of refrigeration OT hacks after suspected commissary attacks](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/us-agencies-warn-refrigeration-hacks-amid-suspected-attacks-military-bases/417071/)** (5 minute read)

CISA, the FBI, and HHS, USDA, and DOT issued joint guidance Thursday urging operators to pull temperature-control systems off the public internet after a wave of grocery and cold-storage sabotage in Europe and suspected Iran-linked freezer failures at roughly six U.S. military commissaries since late August. Attackers can force continuous heating/defrost cycles, blind operators to actual temperatures, spoil inventory, and create fire hazards. Hospitals, pharma, chemicals, and transport also depend on these controls — a force-protection and critical-infrastructure OT problem even when the payload is not classic IT malware.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
