## White House & Executive Action

**[DOJ launches Super Intelligence Task Force to police AI misuse](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-launches-super-intelligence-task-force)** (3 minute read)

The Justice Department announced Thursday the DOJ Super Intelligence Task Force (DOJ-SITF), co-chaired by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss. Attorney General Todd Blanche’s Oct. 13 memo folds the January AI Litigation Task Force into the new body, directs it to organize criminal and civil SI enforcement, set misuse priorities, and serve as DOJ’s principal contact with the White House Super Intelligence Force led by ODNI Director Jay Clayton. Components must notify the task force when opening SI-related investigations or seeking charges (except in exigent cases). It is the clearest signal yet that federal AI enforcement will run through a standing DOJ hub tied to the administration’s SI apparatus, not ad hoc cases.

## Defense & National Security

**[Army and Air Force leaders: AI wins on targeting and logistics, still loses on intel analysis](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/05/where-ai-is-and-isnt-accelerating-military-missions-today/)** (4 minute read)

At Datadog’s Threat Response Forum on Nov. 5, Army intel technical adviser Paul Damchik said automated target recognition has “really earned its place,” compressing training that once took analysts days or months, while automated intelligence analysis “doesn’t exist today” because analysis remains art as well as science. Air Force deputy CIO Keith Hardiman cast AI as an augmenter that pulls people off non-value work in HR and logistics and keeps a “same pane of truth,” not a replacement for human-led ops. The candid split is useful for CDAO, service AI shops, and vendors pitching agentic analysis vs. perception and enterprise automation.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Navy Innovation Unit pipes Silicon Valley AI talent into the Reserve](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/05/navy-innovation-unit-private-sector-technology-talent/)** (3 minute read)

Assistant Navy Secretary for Manpower and Reserve Affairs Ben Kohlmann said the Navy is rolling out an early Navy Innovation Unit that pairs 15 Reservists already in high-caliber tech jobs with about 10 private-sector founders and CEOs brought in as O-4s/O-5s via direct commission to work hard problems in AI, robotics, cyber, and cloud. A June launch call drew roughly 300 applications for 10 slots; expertise sought includes data science, AI/ML, autonomy, quantum, DevOps, and offensive/defensive cyber. It is a concrete talent experiment for getting “AI natives” inside Navy systems without forcing full-time military careers.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[NIST IR 8621: traditional incident response breaks on SI agents](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/11/workshop-si-incident-management-nist-publishes-ir-8621)** (4 minute read)

NIST published Internal Report 8621 from its May 14 SI Incident Management workshop with more than 400 government, industry, and civil-society participants. The report frames SI incidents as both attacks on AI systems and harms from misuse, malfunction, or unintended agent actions, and flags gaps in reasoning-layer telemetry, non-human identity governance, authorization-scoped containment, forensics when outputs are non-deterministic, and contract language for vendor evidence and notification. Planned follow-ons include an AI Incident Management Community of Interest, AI Consortium Task Group 8 on agent monitoring/containment, and updates to SP 800-61 (and related logging/sharing guidance). For CAIOs and CISOs buying agentic tools, this is the federal baseline for what “AI IR” still lacks.

*Source: NIST · Reliability Score: 5/5*
*Primary document: [NIST IR 8621 (PDF)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2026/NIST.IR.8621.pdf)*

**[CBP and Commerce say AI coding and USAi APIs are compressing modernization timelines](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/ask-the-cio/2026/11/ai-tools-are-helping-cbp-commerce-hasten-it-modernization/)** (8 minute read)

At ATARC’s AI Summit, CBP Cargo Systems Program Director Thomas Mills said AI coding and data-extraction tools are delivering about a 40% acceleration moving ACE legacy components (C/C++ stored procedures, parsers) into modern stacks, with two-week sprints and human review of generated code — after retiring a 1980s mainframe. Commerce acting CTO Matt Passos said Census, NIST, and NOAA systems are using APIs into GSA’s USAi so mission experts can clean “dumb data,” including capital-planning TCO analysis, while GSA USAi architecture lead Ryan Palmer said more than 20 agencies are on the platform and urged renting models across FedRAMP clouds to limit lock-in. It is one of the clearer public measurements of AI-assisted federal legacy modernization and shared-service adoption.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[CISA, FBI and HHS warn of refrigeration OT hacks after suspected commissary attacks](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/us-agencies-warn-refrigeration-hacks-amid-suspected-attacks-military-bases/417071/)** (5 minute read)

CISA, the FBI, and HHS, USDA, and DOT issued joint guidance Thursday urging operators to pull temperature-control systems off the public internet after a wave of grocery and cold-storage sabotage in Europe and suspected Iran-linked freezer failures at roughly six U.S. military commissaries since late August. Attackers can force continuous heating/defrost cycles, blind operators to actual temperatures, spoil inventory, and create fire hazards; officials did not name a perpetrator in the U.S. notice. Hospitals, pharma, chemicals, and transport also depend on these controls — a reminder that OT/IoT compromise remains a federal and DoD force-protection problem even when the payload is not classic IT malware.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Shield AI to buy Echodyne for up to $5.3B to expand counter-drone radars](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/companies/2026/11/shield-ai-acquires-radar-maker-echodyne-53-billion-deal/417086/)** (4 minute read) ⚠️

Shield AI announced Thursday a deal valued at up to $5.3 billion for Kirkland, Wash.–based Echodyne ($4.8B at close plus up to $500M earnout), financed with a $2.5B raise that includes $1B Series H at a $28.8B post-money valuation, $1B Blackstone structured equity, and $500M JPMorgan-led debt. Echodyne’s MESA solid-state radars feed counter-UAS and security systems in 40+ countries and hold a $250M-ceiling Army JIATF 401 radar contract; closing is expected in early 2027. Combined with Shield’s X-BAT autonomy push, the deal deepens a defense-tech stack spanning autonomy software, air vehicles, and mission sensors that DoD and allies already buy.

*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Leidos pitches Parcata LLM patching, born from DARPA’s AI Cyber Challenge, to intel customers](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/companies/2026/11/leidos-hones-cyber-product-bet-whole-ai-powered-patching-process/417068/)** (4 minute read) ⚠️

On Leidos’s Q3 earnings call, CEO Tom Bell said Parcata — built by Kudu Dynamics, which Leidos bought for $300M — uses multiple LLMs to find and patch first- and third-party software vulnerabilities, including zero-days, “in minutes” without manual intervention, and is in early use with intelligence-agency customers with broader commercial release eyed later this year. Kudu started the work in DARPA’s AI Cyber Challenge on autonomous cyber reasoning for open-source critical infrastructure. If the intel pilots hold, it is another bid to productize agentic defensive cyber for both classified and commercial markets.

*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*
