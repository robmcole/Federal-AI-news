## White House & Executive Action

**[US signs emerging technology deal with Finland](https://www.nextgov.com/emerging-tech/2026/10/us-signs-emerging-technology-deal-finland/416927/)** (1 minute read)
Secretary of State Marco Rubio signed a Technology Prosperity Deal with Finland on Thursday that names AI, quantum, and research and industrial security as key areas of cooperation. On AI, the two countries plan to promote development and exports across the full AI and network technology stack, help finance exports of trusted tech stacks, and expand 5G/6G research. On quantum, they'll back joint research on quantum manufacturing and push adoption of post-quantum cryptography standards. They also signed a Critical Minerals Framework. It's the latest in a series of bilateral deals the administration is using to promote exports of the American AI stack.

## Congress & Legislation

**[House Democrats call on Trump to preserve AI chatbot records](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/house-democrats-call-trump-preserve-ai-chatbot-records/416913/)** (2 minute read)
Reps. Jamie Raskin and Robert Garcia, the top Democrats on Judiciary and Oversight, wrote to President Trump demanding the White House preserve any prompts he or his staff have entered into chatbots such as Grok, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Perplexity, along with the outputs. They cite the Presidential Records Act. They also asked for a closed-door briefing by Nov. 11 on retention policies, AI chatbot contracts and security safeguards. The letter follows a Time report that Trump asked Grok how Venezuelans would react to the capture of Nicolás Maduro. A White House official said it "preserves all required records in accordance with the law." Nextgov notes the letter previews the oversight Democrats could pursue if they win the House next week.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Treasury, IRS use of third-party ID verification under fire from top Senate Finance, Banking Dems](https://fedscoop.com/treasury-direct-irs-id-me-login-gov/)** (4 minute read)
Treasury started requiring ID.me this week to log in to TreasuryDirect, the only electronic platform for buying and redeeming U.S. savings bonds. Sens. Ron Wyden and Elizabeth Warren asked Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and IRS CEO Frank Bisignano to push the deadline back to at least after the 2027 tax filing season, arguing that bondholders skew older and that a government-run option, Login.gov, already exists. They also asked why the IRS still uses ID.me after saying in 2022 that it would move away from it. The IRS awarded ID.me a sole-source identity contract worth more than $1 billion in January. Treasury and ID.me both defended the service's security.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[ODNI seeks economic data platform](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/10/odni-seeks-economic-data-platform/416919/)** (1 minute read)
ODNI's Office of Economic Security and Emerging Technologies issued an RFI Monday for a platform to ingest, organize and correlate unclassified economic and financial data, including commercially and publicly available information, so it can track foreign investment and supply chain developments. Its main goals are data management and integration, automated entity resolution to tie records to the same company, and bulk API access. ODNI wants the platform to become the government's primary reference point on economic security for the intelligence community and other agencies, and it will take either a complete offering or components. Responses (a capability matrix plus a 15-page approach) are due Nov. 16 at 5 p.m. ET.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Jules Hurst: DOW Obligated $148.5B in OBBBA Funding Before FY26 Ended](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/jules-hurst-dow-148-5b-obbba-funds-before-fy26-end)** (4 minute read)
Pentagon comptroller Jules Hurst said the department obligated $148.5 billion, or 97.5%, of its $152.4 billion from the One Big Beautiful Bill Act before fiscal 2026 ended, which he called a record rate. The Navy and Air Force each obligated 99.2% of their shares ($48.7 billion and $34 billion), the Army 98.1% ($10.5 billion), and other components 94.9% ($55.2 billion). After consulting Congress, the department held back some money for the Office of Strategic Capital, the Defense Innovation Unit and the Economic Defense Unit so it can invest in fiscal 2027. Contractors waiting on reconciliation-funded awards should expect most of this year's money to be on contract already.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Industry pushes back on DoD's proposed rules for moving program](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-news/2026/10/industry-pushes-back-on-dods-proposed-rules-for-moving-program/)** (4 minute read)
Movers for America, which represents companies that handle military household goods moves, is urging the Defense Personal Property Activity to revise proposed program rules. Comments closed Oct. 26. Its biggest objection is a requirement to meet CMMC Level 2, which it calls disproportionate for small movers and their independent contractor drivers. In the group's survey, nearly half of moving professionals said they might scale back DoD work if the requirement stands. It's an early test of how CMMC lands on low-tech, small-business supply chains.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Army selects Lockheed Martin for billion-dollar Future Interceptor program](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/29/army-selects-lockheed-martin-future-interceptor-program/)** (3 minute read)
The Army awarded Lockheed Martin an other transaction agreement to develop the Future Interceptor, a missile meant to beat legacy interceptors on range, altitude, velocity and maneuverability against hypersonic, ballistic and cruise missiles and jet-powered drones. The project is valued at more than $1 billion under a cost share: about $660 million from the government and $423 million from Lockheed. Lockheed has pitched its PAC-3 Edge for the program, with testing starting in 2027 and the aim of being ready to deploy by 2031. The Army says future production decisions will depend on how the prototype performs.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Navy CTO Justin Fanelli Details IT Modernization, Technology Pilot Strategy](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/justin-fanelli-navy-it-modernization)** (3 minute read)
Navy CTO Justin Fanelli said the service has moved 10 technology pilots into enterprise services or programs of record. The Navy ran about 25 pilots in 2025, up from a handful a year before, and has dozens underway in 2026 covering AI, edge computing, hosting hardware and autonomy. Some are paid for by companies proving out commercial tech. One example is Project Dynamis, tested in September 2025 to close the "sensor-to-shooter" gap. It now delivers software including the Maven Smart System to major commands and is a Marine Corps command-and-control program of record. The approach, built around retiring legacy systems under Operation Cattle Drive, shows vendors that a funded Navy pilot can lead to a program of record.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[VA OIG Plans Hybrid AI Platform to Control Token Spending](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/va-oig-plans-hybrid-ai-platform-to-control-token-spending/)** (2 minute read)
VA inspector general CIO Lance Jenkinson said his office is working toward a platform that automatically sends each AI task to a model based on cost, complexity and governance rules. Cheaper open-weight models would handle routine work like transcribing interviews, and pricier models would get specialized analysis. "I don't want to have a well-intentioned developer go burn a billion tokens spinning up six agents, and then I get the bill," he said, describing widespread "token anxiety." It's a goal, not a working system yet, with no timeline. As GSA's OneGov deals move to token-based pricing, model routing is becoming a practical way for agencies to manage AI budgets.
*Source: MeriTalk · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[PNNL-Led Consortium Applies AI to Battery Manufacturing Scale-Up](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/battman-accelerator-pnnl-doe-ai-battery-manufacturing)** (3 minute read)
Pacific Northwest National Laboratory is leading BattMan, a Battery Manufacturing Accelerator backed by DOE's Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation, with Argonne, Oak Ridge, the National Laboratory of the Rockies and five founding universities. AI models trained on the consortium's shared manufacturing data are meant to catch process drift and contamination early. The first phase automates materials synthesis and testing across partner sites, and the long-term goal is autonomous pilot lines with digital twins. Battery and materials manufacturers can now apply to join. It's another lab-led AI-for-science effort aimed at cutting U.S. reliance on foreign battery supply chains.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[FCC Plans Spectrum Changes for Drone Communications, Counter-Drone Radar](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/fcc-spectrum-rule-changes-drone-communications)** (3 minute read)
The FCC will vote at its Nov. 19 open meeting on proposals that would allow safety data links for crewed and uncrewed aircraft in the 450 MHz band under a nationwide license instead of site-by-site authorizations. Another proposal would let ground-based radar use 24.45–24.65 GHz to detect and track unauthorized drones near airports, borders, prisons, stadiums and critical infrastructure. The package also covers 800 MHz air-ground broadband and opening 28.35–28.6 GHz to satellite earth stations in motion. Chairman Brendan Carr says the goal is to reduce regulatory barriers for drone and counter-drone technology, which could open spectrum for detection systems agencies are already buying.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[Limited reports of PHP bug exploitation emerge](https://therecord.media/php2shell-successful-exploitation-reports-emerge)** (4 minute read)
Update: security firms are now reporting successful exploitation of the PHP2Shell flaws CISA ordered patched by Oct. 29. GreyNoise counted at least six successful exploits of CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619. The firm Dream says a Chinese actor chained PHP2Shell with Linux bug CVE-2026-46300, which CISA added to its exploited-vulnerabilities catalog Wednesday, to compromise 24 firewalls from one large vendor, most of them on Oct. 23–24. Two of those servers appeared to belong to a U.S. government-affiliated environment. Dream says the attacker used the Chinese search engine FOFA to narrow about 19,000 exposed firewalls to 240 targets, and found evidence of Claude Code installed alongside DeepSeek and Kimi. It's another sign that attackers are using AI tools to speed up mass exploitation. CISA declined to comment beyond its earlier advisories.
*Source: The Record · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Officials suspect Iran in series of cyberattacks on military base freezers](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/officials-suspect-iran-series-cyberattacks-military-base-freezers/416934/)** (2 minute read)
Two people familiar with the matter say U.S. officials suspect Iran-linked hackers caused freezer malfunctions at about six military commissaries starting around late August. Cyber Command and Defense Department law enforcement units are investigating, along with French authorities: hackers recently overrode refrigeration controls at about 350 French establishments. It isn't clear how the intruders got in, and the attribution could change. If confirmed, it would show Tehran can disrupt military-related equipment inside the U.S., and it puts a spotlight on internet-exposed building and industrial controls on bases.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DHS leak exposes contact information on intelligence personnel](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/dhs-leak-exposes-contact-information-intelligence-personnel/416916/)** (3 minute read)
Update: the 41,000-record DHS leak reported yesterday includes staff of the Office of Intelligence and Analysis, DHS's intelligence community element. Nextgov/FCW found about 75 records that appear tied to I&A field and headquarters staff, and phone numbers it called for two officials appeared to be theirs. Huntress's Dray Agha called it a potentially "severe operational failure," saying it gives hostile actors "a complete map" of sensitive offices that foreign intelligence services or criminals could use for targeted outreach. DHS still describes it as a "potential breach." The hacktivists' claim that they got the data through an Okta single sign-on service remains unconfirmed.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CISA Official: AI Is Finding More Vulnerabilities, but Not All Need Fixing](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/cisa-official-ai-is-finding-more-vulnerabilities-but-not-all-need-fixing/)** (3 minute read)
Jay Gazlay, CISA's acting associate director for vulnerability management, said his team pointed a frontier model at widely used software and turned a minor denial-of-service bug into remote code execution. He added that "most vulnerabilities can be safely ignored" and urged agencies to focus on internet-facing systems and flaws with proven exploitation. CISA is preparing Gold Eagle, an AI-assisted platform for vulnerability remediation across critical infrastructure, to handle hundreds of thousands of cases with an AI front end for triage. He also urged agencies to keep full asset inventories and require SBOMs from vendors. It's a candid look at how CISA plans to handle the flood of AI-discovered bugs.
*Source: MeriTalk · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Understaffed CFPB's security program has foundational 'weaknesses,' OIG says](https://fedscoop.com/cfpb-cybersecurity-program-watchdog-report/)** (3 minute read)
The Federal Reserve's inspector general rated the CFPB's information security program at maturity level 2 of 5, meaning its policies are documented but not consistently followed. The watchdog partly blamed staff losses of nearly 30% since September 2024. It flagged inconsistent tracking of data assets, possibly unsecured hardware left at closed regional offices, and slow vulnerability fixes. Ten of 14 earlier recommendations remain open, and it added eight more. CIO Christopher Chilbert said the report "lacks important context" and that the bureau plans to close all open findings by year-end. It's the second watchdog report this week, after OPM's, tying workforce cuts to weaker IT and security.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*
