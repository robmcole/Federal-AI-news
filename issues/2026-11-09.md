## White House & Executive Action

**[Washington's AI safety testing center drawn closer to the White House, still without a permanent leader](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/09/washingtons-ai-safety-watchdog-left-leaderless-as-it-is-drawn-closer-to-white-house)** (3 minute read)

Two former government officials told the Associated Press that staff at Commerce's Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI) were recently asked to report informally to a White House office. The center runs on about $10 million a year (roughly an eighth of its UK counterpart), has stopped publishing its safety research, and is led on an acting basis by NIST chief Arvind Raman after director Collin Burns was pushed out after four days in April and successor Chris Fall resigned three months later. The White House didn't answer questions about CAISSI and pointed instead to Jay Clayton's Super Intelligence Force; critics worry the government's only frontier-model testing shop could see its findings softened while firms largely police themselves under the voluntary pact.

*Source: Euronews (Associated Press reporting) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Democrats plan a flood of AI bills when they take control of Congress](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/democrats-planning-flood-of-ai-bills-when-they-take-control-of-congress-5007213)** (5 minute read)

Reuters reports incoming Democratic majorities plan bills to shield consumers from data-center-driven electricity costs, require independent testing of advanced AI models, and clarify when AI companies are liable for harm. Expected Majority Leader Chuck Schumer named data-center energy costs a 2027 priority; Rep. Lori Trahan plans independent-evaluation and liability bills; Rep. Zoe Lofgren, seeking the House Science gavel, promised "responsible regulations"; and Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed campaigned on an independent AI regulator. Trump said Friday he'll work with states that "want to become RICH" by embracing AI, so expect Democrats to lean on hearings, sworn testimony, and riders on must-pass bills such as the NDAA rather than standalone laws that would face a veto.

*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Walkinshaw: Congress should push "permanent, serious" AI dialogue with China and frontier-model safety testing](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/ongoing-ai-dialogue-china-necessary-coming-congress-top-tech-lawmaker-says/417099/)** (4 minute read)

At GovExec's post-midterms market outlook event Thursday, Rep. James Walkinshaw (D-Va.) argued that the US and China share an interest in stopping rogue AI agents from hacking critical systems, pointing to the July OpenAI sandbox-escape incident and last month's China-linked Tencent Cloud agents, and building on the U.S.-China Super Intelligence Dialogue and AI incident "hotline" that Xi agreed to in September. He likened frontier-model safety testing to FAA inspections before planes leave the hangar, said he's agnostic on whether an agency or an overseen third party does the testing, and doesn't expect a House select committee on AI. Instead he expects the House Democratic AI commission's work to turn into committee bills. The Northern Virginia member's views carry weight with the region's contractor base.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Deloitte wins FAA's $488M ATLAS contract to modernize legacy apps with AI-assisted refactoring](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/deloitte-488m-faa-atlas-legacy-it-modernization-contract)** (4 minute read)

The FAA posted a SAM.gov notice Friday awarding Deloitte Consulting the Accelerated Transformation of Legacy Applications and Systems (ATLAS) contract, worth up to $488.2 million over as many as 10 years. It covers more than 200 applications and roughly 3,000 databases in the FAA's mission-support portfolio. ATLAS ran as a four-phase challenge-based acquisition, with Deloitte beating fellow Phase 2 finalists Booz Allen, EY, IBM, Kentro, and KSGC, and it explicitly calls for AI and machine learning to speed code refactoring and application analysis. It's a big civilian example of an agency buying a "modernization factory" built around AI coding tools rather than hourly sustainment labor.

*Source: GovConWire · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DIA opens market research on SIA 4, the classified follow-on to its $17.1B intelligence analysis vehicle](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/dia-sia-4-classified-intelligence-analysis-rfi)** (4 minute read)

DIA's Virginia Contracting Activity posted an RFI on Nov. 2 for Solutions for Intelligence Analysis 4, with responses due Nov. 24. Everything after that moves to the NRO's classified Acquisition Research Center, and offerors need an active Top Secret facility clearance and the ability to work at TS/SCI. The planned multiple-award IDIQ has a 5-year base plus a 5-year option, task orders funded starting in FY2029, and a requirement that every prime team with all four small-business set-aside categories. DIA is also asking how fixed-price and objective-based task orders could reward analytic quality and rapid tech and AI insertion instead of headcount, which signals a shift in how the IC buys analysis support.

*Source: GovConWire · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Oasis Systems files a second GAO protest over the Army's $665M counter-drone contract](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/oasis-systems-back-gao-challenging-armys-665m-counter-drone-contract/417097/)** (2 minute read)

After winning corrective action in October on its first protest of the Army's C-UAS award to Parsons (over a claimed conflict of interest and the technical evaluation), Astrion subsidiary Oasis filed a new protest on Nov. 5. It argues the Army must rework the solicitation because the end-to-end integration work has moved to another contract, leaving only program management services. GAO's decision is due Feb. 16, 2027, so the counter-UAS program-office support stays in limbo for at least another quarter.

*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Watchdogs sue to halt Pentagon's Project Meridian future-of-warfare panel](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/litigation/2026/11/pentagon-sued-over-its-project-meridian-advisory-committee/)** (4 minute read)

CREW, Public Citizen, and POGO sued Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and DoD technology chief Emil Michael in D.C. federal court (Civil Action No. 26-cv-3856). They allege that Project Meridian, the panel Hegseth created in a Sept. 30 memo with Elon Musk, Palmer Luckey, and Newt Gingrich among its leaders, violates the Federal Advisory Committee Act: it has no charter, no Federal Register notice, closed meetings with no justification, no records, and members with direct financial stakes in its recommendations. The complaint says the panel has met at least twice and has a Jan. 28, 2027, deadline for final findings. It asks the court to vacate the memo, block meetings until the panel complies, and bar DoD from relying on its advice, which puts a cloud over recommendations meant to shape AI-heavy force design.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [CREW v. DoD complaint (PDF)](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/CREW-v-DoD-Complaint.pdf)*

**[Viasat wins up to $450M to build the Space Data Network mission operations center for Golden Dome](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/06/pentagon-mission-operations-center-space-data-network/)** (4 minute read)

Space Systems Command announced Thursday that it awarded Viasat a competitive IDIQ worth up to $450 million, with a $115 million initial task order to design, field, and scale the mission operations center for the Space Data Network Backbone. Viasat will be the network and mission-system integrator orchestrating data across multiple constellations and providers for SSC's Space-Based Sensing and Targeting portfolio. The center rides on SpaceX's $2.3 billion backbone prototype due by the end of 2027 and is the software-defined routing hub meant to move Golden Dome sensor data to shooters with real-time priority routing.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[DOGE's Gleason: America.gov is headed toward AI agents that do the work for you](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/technology-main/2026/11/doge-leader-outlines-ai-driven-future-for-federal-service-delivery/)** (3 minute read)

Building on the Login.gov phase-two plans in Friday's digest, DOGE acting Administrator Amy Gleason told a Digital Services Coalition event Thursday that America.gov will move from an unauthenticated chatbot to an authenticated experience and then to an AI agent that completes tasks for the public, with a dashboard to track them. She also said chatbots could eventually replace individual agency websites. She warned agencies "are really struggling to implement," citing tech-talent shortages, hard-to-procure tools, and AI security, and said it "feels like 2014 again." ACSI's new federal study found satisfaction with government services fell 2.6% this year, with HHS down 4%.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Treasury's Common Services Center rehires IT talent and pushes AI department-wide](https://fedscoop.com/treasury-department-technology-hiring-ai-workforce/)** (4 minute read)

At ACT-IAC's Workforce Innovation Summit Friday, new Treasury Common Services Center administrator Colleen Heller-Stein said the unit, which runs IT engineering, operations, HR, acquisition, and travel services for all of Treasury, is "actively recruiting IT professionals" and leaning heavily on OPM's Tech Force early-career hires. The rebuild comes after Treasury lost about 33,000 staff since January 2025, including nearly 2,200 in IT management roles. She framed AI as "an everybody issue," not a CIO issue, and stressed keeping humans in the loop. It's one of the clearest signs yet that cut-heavy agencies are rehiring tech talent to execute AI mandates.

*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[NIST IR 8621 maps gaps in managing AI incidents and plans SP 800-61 updates](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/11/workshop-si-incident-management-nist-publishes-ir-8621)** (3 minute read)

NIST published IR 8621 on Nov. 2, summarizing a May 14 workshop of more than 400 participants on managing incidents involving AI systems: both cyberattacks on models and harms from misuse, malfunction, or unintended actions. Its key findings point to future work on updating the SP 800-61 incident response guidelines, new incident-readiness resources for model developers, monitoring and evidence collection, incident information sharing, and clearer responsibilities among developers, deployers, and users. It lays the groundwork for agency and contractor playbooks on agentic-AI incidents, just as Congress and the NDAA conferees weigh AI incident-reporting requirements.

*Source: NIST · Reliability Score: 5/5*
*Primary document: [NIST IR 8621, NIST Workshop on SI Incident Management (PDF)](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2026/NIST.IR.8621.pdf)*
