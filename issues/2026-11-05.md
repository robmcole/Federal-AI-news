**TL;DR:** A newly Democratic Congress is gearing up to oversee AI, cyber cuts, FITARA and data centers · The Super Intelligence Task Force will publish an inventory of laws governing U.S. AI labs within one to two weeks · TRANSCOM's embedded tech team built about 33 AI apps in the first two weeks of Operation Epic Fury, and roughly half are still in production.

## White House & Executive Action

**[Pentagon CTO says government will detail laws that apply to AI labs this month](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/pentagon-chief-tech-officer-says-government-will-detail-laws-that-apply-to-ai-labs-this-month-5001816)** (2 minute read)
At a Wall Street Journal tech conference in Napa on Nov. 4, Pentagon CTO Emil Michael — vice chair of the White House Super Intelligence Task Force — said the group will publicize "all the laws and regulations that apply" to domestic AI labs "in the next week or two." He framed the inventory as a way to give the public confidence that companies will "do the right thing," warning that otherwise "535 legislators writing a piece of regulation… could kill the industry." He did not spell out the document's scope or which agencies will appear in it. With Democrats taking Congress in January, the timing looks like an administration bid to define the status quo before a more aggressive oversight agenda arrives.
*Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[What the midterm results mean for federal tech policy](https://www.nextgov.com/people/2026/11/what-midterm-results-mean-federal-tech-policy/417043/)** (8 minute read)
Democratic House and Senate wins on Nov. 3 put tech, cyber and surveillance oversight back in Democratic hands starting in January. Nextgov/FCW's roundup flags a likely FITARA scorecard revival under Rep. James Walkinshaw (who wants AI and cyber metrics baked in), House Homeland investigations of DHS/ICE AI surveillance, and intel-committee scrutiny of contractor donations and politicized assessments. AI also moved from niche issue to campaign centerpiece: NBC exit polling found 56% of voters said AI and data centers were a "crucial or major factor," Abdul El-Sayed won Michigan's Senate race calling for a Cabinet-level AI agency, and Progressive Caucus Chair Greg Casar said Democrats should make taking on AI a top priority. For vendors and agencies, the lame-duck window just got shorter and the 2027 hearing calendar just got longer.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Democrats’ midterm sweep puts CISA cuts, intelligence disputes under scrutiny](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/11/democrats-midterm-sweep-puts-cisa-cuts-intelligence-disputes-under-scrutiny/417050/)** (6 minute read)
Incoming House Homeland Security Chair Bennie Thompson said Democrats will investigate how CISA workforce cuts (roughly a third of staff after Trump returned) affected federal-network and election security help, plus DHS "self-dealing," contractor steering and AI-aided attack readiness. Senate Democrats would also control confirmation of any new CISA or National Counterterrorism Center nominee. The piece also flags that the Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act stopgap still expires Dec. 11, and that Armed Services cyber subcommittees under Ro Khanna and Jacky Rosen would oversee Pentagon software buys and AI programs — including Khanna's prior questions to frontier labs about Chinese model theft. Oversight intensity is about to rise even if new statutes take longer.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[GSA names Jennifer Hill Federal Acquisition Service commissioner](https://www.gsa.gov/about-gsa/newsroom/news-releases/gsa-announces-commissioner-appointments-for-pbs-and-fas-11042026)** (3 minute read)
GSA announced Nov. 4 that Jennifer M. Hill is the new FAS commissioner, ending Laura Stanton's acting tenure since April; Stanton returns to deputy FAS commissioner. Hill joined GSA in February as an adviser to Administrator Edward Forst on procurement consolidation and purchasing power, after 30 years in finance including as CFO of Bank of America Merrill Lynch and roles at Goldman Sachs. FAS oversees more than $125 billion in business volume with about 16,000 companies across procurement, technology, fleet, travel and charge cards for more than 80 agencies — including the OneGov AI deals Stanton helped steer. The permanent FAS lead will shape how the next wave of AI, cloud and software vehicles get priced and competed.
*Source: GSA · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[DHS acquisition forecast page is live after APFS shutdown](https://www.dhs.gov/advance-acquisition-planning-forecast-contract-opportunities)** (2 minute read)

Capture teams that relied on APFS subscriptions should bookmark the new page and pull the spreadsheets directly until alerts return.

## Defense & National Security

**[TRANSCOM embedded tech team built ~33 AI apps in first two weeks of Operation Epic Fury](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/04/prototype-apps-transportation-command-operation-epic-fury/)** (7 minute read)
U.S. Transportation Command Chief Data and AI Officer Ryan Samuelson told DefenseScoop that data scientists were embedded on the global operations floor for the first time during Operation Epic Fury and produced roughly 33 apps in the first two weeks alone, with a 24-hour MVP cadence. Tools included an AI bot that summarized State Department cables affecting contested logistics and overflight, a 15-second overnight movement brief for the Transcom commander (replacing hours of staff work), munitions-demand anticipation, and live data feeds shared with CENTCOM via Maven Smart System / Palantir Ontology and AI Hivemind. About half of those early bots are still in production. It is one of the clearest public case studies of wartime "build next to the operator" AI for joint logistics.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Shield AI completes first full-scale X-BAT prototype; Navy joint investment at $400M](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/shield-ai-cofounder-teases-snowball-of-x-bat-fighter-drone-announcements-in-europe/)** (4 minute read)
Shield AI said its first full-scale X-BAT autonomous VTOL fighter-drone prototype finished assembly in Frisco, Texas, and will test in Newton, Kan., while cofounder Ryan Tseng teased a coming "snowball" of European orders and industry pacts. Shield AI and the U.S. Navy are jointly investing $400 million in X-BAT; Tseng put baseline unit cost around $25 million (about one-quarter of a Eurofighter or F-35 in Europe), or roughly $35 million with a higher-end sensor suite. European sovereign supply-chain talk and active sales campaigns matter for DoD export policy and collaborative combat aircraft planning as autonomous wingmen move from briefing slides toward hardware.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[OpenAI stacks public-sector leadership after a $550M+ federal contracting surge](https://www.nextgov.com/people/2026/11/openai-hones-public-sector-focus-new-leadership-hires/417047/)** (5 minute read)
Beyond yesterday's Dan Driscoll hire, OpenAI added Yvette Cesario (ex-AWS national security) as head of national security, Peter Guerra (ex-Oracle government data/AI) as head of tech success, Jamil Jaffer as head of global government cyber, and Megha Bhattacharya as public-sector policy communications lead. OpenAI for Government said it onboarded 50 agencies in September–October and captured government contracts valued at more than $550 million per GovTribe, led by Treasury ($487M potential / $2.16M obligated), HHS ($30.68M / $6.93M) and VA ($34M / $4.20M), on top of OneGov 2.0 token pricing and GenAI.mil access. The bench build is the clearest signal yet that OpenAI is treating federal IC, cyber and mission deployment as a standing business line, not a side channel.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Voters rebuff data centers in nearly every 2026 midterm ballot measure](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/data-center-ballot-measures-2026-midterm.html)** (5 minute read)
Of 41 local data-center ballot measures across 10 states, voters backed restrictions or rejected pro-development measures in every contest with results available except Harwood, N.D., per Ballotpedia and CNBC. Ohio alone had 21 measures; Democrat Amy Acton won the governorship backing a "conditional moratorium," while Sherrod Brown cast Jon Husted as "the face of data centers." The backlash lands on projects like OpenAI/SoftBank/SB Energy's PORTS-Pike campus (backed by DOE and Nvidia, potentially $500B+), and Progressive Caucus Chair Greg Casar called for AI CEOs to testify under oath. Local permitting risk is now a first-order constraint on the federal-facing AI infrastructure buildout.
*Source: CNBC · Reliability Score: 4/5*
