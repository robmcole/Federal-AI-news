# Federal AI Daily: Thursday, November 26, 2026

**TL;DR:** It's a light holiday news day. Treasury's FinCEN took systems offline after a breach, the latest in a run of federal intrusions after the CIA/ODNI, FBI and USDA. Twenty-seven House Democrats asked 12 DHS technology vendors, including Palantir, Flock Safety and Clearview AI, for their contracts and data policies, a preview of next year's oversight. And Commerce published the rules for the "on-demand" track of its American SI Exports Program, which opens Monday, Nov. 30.

## White House & Executive Action

**[American SI Exports Program; On-Demand Consortia for Specific Foreign Opportunities](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/27/2026-24107/american-si-exports-program-on-demand-consortia-for-specific-foreign-opportunities)** (6 minute read)
New development on the export program we covered Nov. 23: Commerce's International Trade Administration filed the formal notice for the program's second, "on-demand" pathway on Nov. 25. It publishes in the Federal Register Friday. Applications open Nov. 30 at SIexports.gov/on-demand and are accepted on a rolling basis. Commerce says it intends to decide on each application within 60 calendar days and has set no cap on the number of packages. A consortium must be built around a specific foreign deal backed by evidence such as a tender or a buyer's letter of interest. It only has to offer the parts of the AI technology stack that deal needs, and a U.S.-organized subsidiary of a foreign parent can be the anchor member. The notice also lets Commerce de-designate packages for fraud, misrepresentation or other conduct that "would reflect negatively on the Program," and says the pre-set consortia window may reopen later. Expect smaller federal tech vendors to look for places in these deal-specific teams.
*Source: Federal Register (Commerce/ITA) · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[Miller's AI task force involvement still taking shape](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/25/2026/millers-ai-task-force-involvement-still-taking-shape)** (2 minute read)
Stephen Miller's move to White House chief of staff leaves an open question about who steers AI policy at the top of the White House. His predecessor, Susie Wiles, was deeply involved and sat on the president's AI task force. Until recently Miller had recused himself from AI matters because of his wife's business dealings. Those recusals no longer apply, but he hasn't spoken much about AI in the past. A White House official told Semafor that task force members report to Miller under the task force charter, but the White House would not say whether he will also be a member. The answer will shape how quickly AI items like the export program and the "national champions" push get decisions in the final weeks of unified Republican control.
*Source: Semafor · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[US House Democrats seek records from 12 DHS surveillance vendors](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202611/us-house-democrats-seek-records-from-12-dhs-surveillance-vendors)** (6 minute read)
Twenty-seven House Democrats, led by Rep. Delia Ramirez (D-Ill.), wrote to 12 companies that supply DHS with identification, analytics and surveillance technology: Palantir, Flock Safety, Clearview AI, Anduril, Cellebrite, Axon, Skydio, Thomson Reuters, NEC, PenLink, Magnet Forensics and LexisNexis. They want contracts going back to the start of Trump's second term, the companies' policies for data collected for DHS, and any nondisclosure agreements that limit public disclosure. Ramirez is seeking to chair Homeland Security's oversight subcommittee next year. Separately, ranking member Bennie Thompson has given DHS until Dec. 2 to produce previously requested records. Once Democrats take control in January, committees can issue subpoenas, so vendors with AI and biometric work at DHS should expect demands for unredacted contracts and testimony.
*Source: Biometric Update · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Commerce's cloud BPA signals template for AI, post-quantum buys](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/technology-main/2026/11/commerces-cloud-bpa-signals-template-for-ai-post-quantum-buys/)** (4 minute read)
Commerce CIO Brian Epley said the department merged 13 separate cloud contracts into one blanket purchase agreement. It used GSA's OneGov deals and a GSA FAR deviation that lets agencies set up BPAs on top of multiple-award vehicles, and it buys directly from cloud providers rather than through integrators or resellers. He said Commerce will need similar BPAs on GSA's Multiple Award Schedule for AI and post-quantum cryptography. Commerce got early access to AI models through GSA's USAi platform, and Epley said use really took off once bureaus moved to API access, which also made the "tokenomics" and real costs apparent. His FY27 priority is a multi-cloud, multi-model setup shared across the bureaus. For resellers and integrators, it's another sign that department-wide, direct-to-provider buying is spreading.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[USCIS turns to Sayari to trace financial ties in immigration fraud investigations](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202611/uscis-turns-to-sayari-to-trace-financial-ties-in-immigration-fraud-investigations)** (4 minute read)
A DHS acquisition forecast says USCIS plans to spend $2 million to $5 million on Sayari Graph Analyst for its Fraud Detection and National Security Directorate, mainly for investigations tied to the EB-5 Immigrant Investor Program Office. The platform traces corporate ownership, beneficial owners and trade links across more than 250 jurisdictions. ICE, CBP, the Coast Guard and the Secret Service already use it, and ICE bought licenses in September. The forecast expects a Dec. 4 award, though Biometric Update could not independently confirm one. It's another sign that commercial network-analytics tools are moving into benefits fraud screening, much like FEMA's AI anti-fraud RFI this week.
*Source: Biometric Update · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[VA releases RFI for major TSS recompete spanning enterprise cybersecurity, EHRM security and AI automation](https://orangeslices.ai/va-releases-rfi-for-major-tss-recompete-spanning-enterprise-cybersecurity-ehrm-and-ai-automation/)** (2 minute read)
VA's Office of Information and Technology is doing market research for a five-year cybersecurity and IT material-weakness remediation task order, the recompete of Transformation Support Services (TSS). Booz Allen Hamilton holds the current TSS 2.0 order, worth more than $300 million, which drew eight proposals. The work covers information system security officer (ISSO) support for up to 1,200 FISMA systems, authorizations to operate, continuous monitoring, audit support and security for the Oracle EHR rollout. VA is specifically asking about AI-enabled security automation, automated evidence collection and zero trust. VA expects a firm-fixed-price order and is asking service-disabled and veteran-owned small businesses about teaming and workshare. The posting doesn't give a response date, so check the RFI itself.
*Source: OrangeSlices AI (third-party copy of a VA notice) · Reliability Score: 3/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Coast Guard's updated Force Design plan includes Maven Smart System, other digital advancements](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/25/coast-guard-force-design-update-digital-modernization/)** (4 minute read)
The Coast Guard's new "Force Design: Forging the Future" update, released this month, makes Palantir's Maven Smart System and Tactical Awareness Kits central to faster command and control. It says the service "must drastically compress decision cycles." The plan also includes Coastal Sentinel, a network linking the service's sensors into one shared picture for maritime awareness. Phase 1 starts in fiscal 2027 at Sector San Diego, followed by Houston-Galveston and Miami. The Digital Seabag mobile-device program has delivered more than 2,000 devices and aims to reach 8,000-plus boat force operators by March 2027. About $20 billion of the Coast Guard's $24.6 billion from last year's reconciliation law is already obligated, and the service says it needs $20 billion a year going forward. Maven keeps spreading beyond DoD, even as it's still unclear who runs the program under the Pentagon's newly renamed Super Intelligence Office.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[IRS buys Clearview access in facial recognition reversal](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202611/irs-buys-clearview-access-in-facial-recognition-reversal)** (5 minute read)
Federal spending records show IRS Criminal Investigation bought a $63,000 Clearview AI "investigator tool web subscription" starting March 16, 2026. The base period runs to March 15, 2027, and the contract could be worth up to $348,124 through March 2031. In May 2024, IRS officials told GAO the unit had stopped using non-federal facial recognition and had "no plans to use the technology in the future," so GAO closed one of its recommendations. The subscription began the same month IRS personnel joined the FBI-led Joint Mission Center created under NSPM-7, which eventually included 10 IRS agents and three analysts. The records don't show whether Clearview was the tool used in that work. It's a case where an agency's commitment to GAO didn't hold, and it gives Democrats another target in their DHS vendor inquiry (above).
*Source: Biometric Update · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[Treasury's financial crimes bureau takes systems offline after hack](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/treasurys-financial-crimes-bureau-takes-systems-offline-after-hack/417442/)** (3 minute read)
On Tuesday evening, Nov. 24, FinCEN disclosed unauthorized access to "a limited subset of its systems" and took some systems offline as a precaution. It has not said what data was accessed or who was responsible. The Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing site is still up, though some services may be unavailable. A FinCEN order gives institutions that used its automated submission channel as of Nov. 18 until Dec. 30 to file reports originally due between Nov. 18 and Dec. 29. FinCEN holds banks' confidential suspicious activity reports, which makes it a valuable intelligence target. The breach comes in the same week as the CIA/ODNI intrusion and after recent incidents at the FBI's NCIC and USDA. That pattern will add pressure on incoming Democratic committee chairs and on DNI Clayton's interim leadership of ONCD.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
