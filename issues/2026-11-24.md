# Federal AI Daily: Tuesday, November 24, 2026

**TL;DR:** Commerce put Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud firm at the center of the Nvidia scandal, and six affiliates on the Entity List hours after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, a move Trump says he brokered. A new executive order requires federal contractors to tell the White House before answering Congress and to withhold anything the administration deems privileged. And Sen. Chris Coons says the $350 billion Pentagon reconciliation push is "dead," which pushes the final FY27 defense number into the new Congress.

## White House & Executive Action

**[Protecting the Executive's Privileges and Enhancing Efficiencies in Federal Contracting (EO 14438)](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/11/protecting-the-executives-privileges-and-enhancing-efficiencies-in-federal-contracting/)** (5 minute read)
President Trump signed Executive Order 14438 on Nov. 20. It requires prime contractors and their subs to notify the White House and their customer agencies of any pending or future congressional request for information about their work with the executive branch. They must also follow White House and agency instructions to withhold communications the administration deems covered by executive privilege. OMB is directed to write compliance guidance, and agencies are told to consider suspension, debarment or contract termination for contractors that don't comply. Washington Technology notes the timing, with Democrats taking both chambers Jan. 3 and vowing contract oversight, and GW Law's Jessica Tillipman argues the order is on weak legal footing because the D.C. Circuit's Navarro ruling says only the president or an authorized representative can invoke presidential communications privilege. Every IT and AI vendor with a federal contract should expect new clauses and a compliance call before answering any Hill letter.
*Source: The White House · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[U.S. adds Megaspeed to trade blacklist over export control concerns](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/24/us-adds-megaspeed-to-trade-blacklist-over-export-control-concerns-.html)** (2 minute read)
New development on the Megaspeed story we've tracked since Nov. 16: BIS added seven entities to the Entity List in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, including Megaspeed International, PT Megaspeed International, MSFusion, Megaspeed (Thailand) and Alibaba's Singapore subsidiary Apex Enterprise Solutions, citing a "significant risk" of diverting advanced computing products. The listing covers all items subject to the EAR, with license applications reviewed under a "presumption of denial." Commerce and Justice said Commerce opened its Megaspeed probe in 2024, Nvidia put the firm on its "no sell" list in June 2025, and DOJ now leads a criminal investigation. Alibaba says Apex never diverted controlled chips. The rule publishes in the Federal Register Nov. 25, and it lands as the administration is also tightening rules on overseas cloud access to U.S. chips.
*Source: CNBC · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [BIS Entity List rule, FR Doc. 2026-23999 (public inspection)](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-23999.pdf)*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Congress is 'out of time' to decide on reconciliation, needs to settle topline spending: Sen. Coons](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/congress-is-out-of-time-to-decide-on-reconciliation-needs-to-settle-topline-spending-sen-coons/)** (3 minute read)
Speaking at the Halifax security forum, Sen. Chris Coons, the top Democrat on Senate defense appropriations, said "we're out of time for reconciliation" and the final FY27 Pentagon number "will be closer to a trillion than $1.5 [trillion]," likely settled in the new Congress. The administration's $1.5 trillion ask relies on $350 billion in reconciliation for Golden Dome, drones and multiyear munitions, and its fate was due from Thune by Thanksgiving. The CR runs out Dec. 11, Senate appropriators have a $1.1 trillion bill and House appropriators a $1 trillion one, and Coons says the urgent need is a bipartisan defense/non-defense topline. Programs that counted on reconciliation money, including the $29.5 billion "AI Arsenal" request, now face a thinner, later budget.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Reps. Whitesides, Gottheimer Introduce Legislation To Defend Critical Infrastructure Against AI-Driven Cyberattacks](https://whitesides.house.gov/2026/11/23/reps-whitesides-gottheimer-introduce-legislation-to-defend-critical-infrastructure-against-ai-driven-cyberattacks/)** (3 minute read)
Reps. George Whitesides (D-Calif.) and Josh Gottheimer (D-N.J.) introduced the Cyber Resilience for Critical Infrastructure Act on Nov. 23. It would direct NIST to set voluntary, sector-specific benchmarks for how fast operators should fix vulnerabilities and would authorize $250 million in CISA grants to patch faster, replace outdated equipment and wall off systems that can't be patched quickly. Grants would cover 100% of eligible costs for smaller operators and state, local, tribal and territorial governments, with 30% reserved for small, rural and municipal operators, and no new regulatory mandates. The sponsors frame it around AI shrinking the time from disclosure to exploit, and both sit on the new bipartisan AI Safety and Innovation Caucus that would chair or shape this agenda next year.
*Source: Office of Rep. George Whitesides · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[Tech bills of the week: Body cameras for DHS law enforcement; Identifying federal IT contracting needs; and more](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/11/tech-bills-week-body-cameras-dhs-law-enforcement-identifying-federal-it-contracting-needs-and-more/417418/)** (6 minute read)
Nextgov's roundup adds several chip and data center bills from last week. Reps. Pat Harrigan (R-N.C.) and Dave Min (D-Calif.) would bar telecom companies that lost their licenses on national security grounds from using U.S. data centers or cloud services, with FCC fines up to $1 million per violation per day and 180 days to comply. Sens. John Cornyn (R-Texas) and Mark Kelly (D-Ariz.) would extend the CHIPS Act's 35% advanced manufacturing investment tax credit. Sen. Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) introduced a bill giving Commerce 30 days to decide on listing Megaspeed, which Monday's Entity List action has now overtaken. Together they show chip and cloud security staying bipartisan heading into the Democratic Congress.

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[New protests challenge the Army's latest MAPS changes](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/new-protests-challenge-armys-latest-maps-changes/417412/)** (1 minute read)
Three companies have protested Amendment 12 to the Army's 10-year, $50 billion Marketplace for the Acquisition of Professional Services, which consolidates RS3 and ITES-3 into one IT and professional services vehicle. The Nov. 12 amendment opened MAPS to commercial-sector vendor teams, created separate pools with seven award slots per domain for small business teams and three for commercial teams (80 awards per domain in total), and moved proposals to Nov. 30. Intelligence Consulting Enterprise Solutions and MindBoard argue the new pools wall those teams off from the main competitions, and AHS Business Services challenged the response time; WT hears the Army plans corrective action on that last point. GAO's decisions on the first two are due by Feb. 25, and the Army can't make awards while protests are pending.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Deloitte awarded Army GenAI Intelligent Governance Sandbox contract](https://orangeslices.ai/deloitte-awarded-army-genai-intelligent-governance-sandbox-contract/)** (1 minute read)
Newly released award data show Deloitte Consulting won a one-year, $2,502,415.20 Army contract (W9128Z26CA014) to deliver a "generative AI front office solutions intelligent governance sandbox," funded through the U.S. Army Resource and Programs Agency. The record lists a single bidder. DoD can delay posting award details for up to 90 days, so the award itself may be older. It's a small but telling buy: the Army's headquarters staff is paying for a governed environment to test generative AI on front-office work, the kind of sandbox other agencies are standing up before enterprise rollouts.
*Source: OrangeSlices AI (third-party copy of federal award data) · Reliability Score: 3/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[DOW Launches Automated SI Solution for Classification Management](https://www.war.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/4658620/dow-launches-automated-si-solution-for-classification-management/)** (2 minute read)
The War Department is rolling out ACME, the Automated Classification Management Environment, to use AI ("super intelligence" in administration usage) to help classify and declassify millions of documents. The Air Force built it in 2025 as "Battering Ram." In October, Deputy Secretary Steve Feinberg adopted it as an enterprise solution and put Air Force Secretary Troy Meink in charge of the system. A $2.35 million investment from the intelligence and security undersecretary and the chief SI officer funded tools to digitize more than 4,000 security classification guides that users now search by hand, and to flag overlaps and inconsistencies across them. The department says records management costs taxpayers more than $15 billion a year and it wants a governmentwide common digital language for classification guidance.
*Source: U.S. Department of War · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[DISA boss highlights need for training, standardization to prepare for new era of ZT and AI-enabled cyber warfare](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/23/ai-cyber-warfare-zero-trust-lt-gen-stanton/)** (4 minute read)
At AFCEA's Annual Data Forum, Lt. Gen. Paul Stanton, who leads DISA and DoD Cyber Defense Command, called AI "an accelerant poured onto everything for which we are responsible." He said DoD must automate configuration changes and run defensive cyber operations "agentically" to keep pace with AI-enabled attackers. He warned that automation is dangerous unless the department first standardizes and simplifies its architecture, and that zero trust fails without trained operators. The Pentagon and services face a self-imposed deadline to reach "target level" zero trust maturity by the end of FY2027. Expect standardized ZT architecture and cyber workforce training to show up in DISA requirements.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Marine Corps moving new chatbot prototyped at recent hackathon into full production](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/23/marine-corps-chatbot-hackathon-full-production/)** (4 minute read)
A chatbot that won a Marine Corps AI "sprint hackathon" at the Naval Postgraduate School in late October is headed for production in the MCCS Connect app, giving Marines and families on-demand answers on childcare, fitness and other quality-of-life services. Its guardrails stop the model from answering messages that signal suicidal intent and route users to content written by clinical suicide-prevention staff. The prototype will go through Operation StormBreaker, the Corps' enterprise DevSecOps platform, which chief digital services officer Dave Raley says aims to "collapse the tech valley of death." It's a concrete example of a hackathon-to-production pipeline and of safety-by-design for a sensitive military AI use.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[1 year into Genesis Mission, Energy official emphasizes need for more funding](https://fedscoop.com/energy-department-genesis-mission-one-year-milestones-whats-next/)** (4 minute read)
A year after the executive order that launched it, DOE's Genesis Mission has about 15 agencies committing more than $5 billion, industry pledging $2.5 billion in compute, model access and tokens, and a consortium that has grown from about 40 to about 70 organizations. Director Darío Gil says "we are limited by funding" and calls Congress "an indispensable partner." Both chambers rejected the White House's $1.2 billion carve-out for DOE's AI and quantum office, and Senate appropriators recommend about $467.8 million for Genesis-related work across three offices. DOE runs at FY26 levels under the CR that expires Dec. 11, so the next two weeks of appropriations decide how fast the AI-for-science platform can scale.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Jensen Huang: Trump sealed deal for Nvidia CEO return](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/trump-nvidia-jensen-huang.html)** (5 minute read)
New development since Huang resigned Nov. 12: Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated him as president, CEO and director on Nov. 23, saying it received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and is confident his return "poses no national security risk." Trump told CNBC "I made the deal," saying the board asked for assurances and "we gave them assurances, at least with everything we know." People close to the board also cited the stock's drop of more than 25% and doubts about interim CEO Colette Kress, who stays CFO. Nvidia says its independent investigation into the reported foreign intelligence targeting of Huang continues, a new National Security and Export Control Committee will oversee trade compliance, and it will hire a Chief Compliance Officer. Watch whether House investigators still hold the Huang (Dec. 1) and Nvidia general counsel (Dec. 2) interviews.
