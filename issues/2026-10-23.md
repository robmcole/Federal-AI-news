## Congress & Legislation

**[Exclusive: bipartisan bill would develop federal guidance for distillation attacks, convene industry on defensive cyber](https://cyberscoop.com/bipartisan-bill-combat-ai-distillation-attacks-nist/)** (4 minute read)
Reps. Pat Harrigan (R-N.C.) and George Whitesides (D-Calif.) are introducing the AI Cyber Defense Act, which would direct NIST to define distillation attacks and develop voluntary standards and best practices for stopping entities from Russia, China, Iran and North Korea from training their models on U.S. frontier models' outputs. It would also create an "AI Cybersecurity Roundtable" at NIST, with government and AI industry members, to write voluntary standards for defensive AI in critical infrastructure, and let the NIST director designate specific entities of concern. Harrigan argued adversaries can stay six to 12 months behind for "5 to 10% of the spend" by distilling U.S. models. It would give NIST a formal role in a problem so far handled by labs' own account bans and export-control talk.
*Source: CyberScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[DHS consolidates software purchasing under $2.7B Carahsoft contract](https://fedscoop.com/dhs-consolidate-procurement-carahsoft/)** (3 minute read)
DHS issued a sole-source justification this week for a nearly $2.7 billion departmentwide blanket purchase agreement with Carahsoft, opening access to 70-plus software makers on Carahsoft's GSA Schedule for off-the-shelf licenses, SaaS and cloud subscriptions, maintenance and limited professional services. Work began in July and runs to July 2031, covering data analytics, cybersecurity, automation and access management. DHS says one enterprise BPA cuts cycle times, standardizes platforms and enforces consistent terms across components, following its departmentwide Cumulus cloud buys (projected $142 million in first-year savings). For software vendors, it means DHS deals increasingly run through a single reseller vehicle rather than component-by-component orders.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Veterans Affairs explores new data integration approaches](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/10/veterans-affairs-explores-new-data-integration-approaches/416762/)** (2 minute read)
VA posted a sources sought notice Tuesday for MOSAIC (Mission-Oriented Services for Application Integration and Capabilities), market research for a potential contract covering data integration across systems like VA Profile, VADIR and the Health Data Repository. VA wants real-time APIs, bulk access for data-lake workloads, event-driven streaming and "AI-ready data products," and asks specifically how AI agents and agent-to-agent interaction could change traditional APIs, middleware and manual integration services. Responses are capped at 25 pages and due Nov. 4 at 3 p.m. ET. It's an early look at how VA may buy integration work over the next five to 10 years.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[SOCOM seeks common PaaS environment to support testing and fielding of new tech](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/22/socom-paas-integrated-contracting-vehicle/)** (3 minute read)
U.S. Special Operations Command published an RFI and draft performance work statement Wednesday night for an "integrated contracting vehicle" to stand up a common platform-as-a-service environment near MacDill Air Force Base, Fla. The vehicle would let traditional and non-traditional vendors, users and program teams rapidly discover, prototype, integrate, test and field software across PEO SOF Digital Applications, PEO Tactical Information Systems and Joint Acquisition Task Force experimentation. It includes integration facility operations, cloud-native software engineering and end-to-end Risk Management Framework support to win and keep authorities to operate. It's an opening for software integrators and ATO specialists in the Tampa market.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Navy awards Applied Intuition $25M to deploy AI sensor fusion at sea](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/navy-awards-applied-intuition-25m-to-deploy-ai-sensor-fusion-at-sea/)** (3 minute read)
Applied Intuition announced a $25 million Navy contract to deploy its Intelligent Perception System, starting on five Arleigh Burke-class destroyers and potentially "across the fleet." IPS fuses feeds from cameras and infrared sensors that weren't built to work together into one picture for sailors, with features like automated collision alerts, and sends recorded sensor data ashore to train better models for crewed and uncrewed vessels. It also supports over-the-air updates at sea, and builds on the company's DECK data-collection pilot tested earlier this year. It shows a self-driving-car data engine moving into fleet operations under the Pentagon's commercial-first push.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[The Pentagon is quietly building an elite new tech sabotage agency](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/10/22/technology-support-office-dod-tech-spyware-sabotage-01100327)** (9 minute read)
Citing 12 current and former officials, POLITICO reports that Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinberg signed an April 14 memo creating a classified Technology Support Office under the undersecretary for intelligence and security, built by merging two smaller tech-sabotage programs running since 2023. TSO reportedly develops hacking tools, supply-chain disruption and deception operations (such as getting adversaries to install faulty components) aimed at foreign defense technology, is led by former Marine special operations officer Grant Hundley, and may also build a catalog of approved commercial AI tools for classified work. A former official estimated classified contract spending "north of $1.5 billion," and sources said SOCOM and Cyber Command objected and that the friction helped prompt Khoi Nguyen's exit from Cyber Command. Pentagon spokesperson Jacob Bliss said the story is "built on information that is simply not true," so treat the details as reported, not confirmed.
*Source: POLITICO · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Space Force shifts into high gear on cislunar warfare, with RFIs coming soon](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/space-force-shifts-into-high-gear-on-cislunar-warfare-with-rfis-coming-soon/)** (6 minute read)
Maj. Gen. Stephen Purdy, the Space Force's portfolio acquisition executive for advanced programs, said Monday the service will soon issue two RFIs: one for commercial technology for foundational cislunar missions such as space domain awareness and positioning, navigation and timing, and a second for AI software that lets spacecraft operate autonomously between Earth and the Moon. The goal is capabilities fielded by the end of fiscal 2029. Purdy's Cislunar Coordination Office, set up in May, is refining a roadmap and a prioritized list of unfunded requirements, and OSD is launching interagency working groups on cislunar policy. Autonomy vendors should watch for the second RFI, since deep-space operations are where onboard AI matters most.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Pentagon sued over data breach affecting more than 3 million people](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-news/2026/10/pentagon-sued-over-data-breach-affecting-more-than-3-million-people/)** (3 minute read)
Two Marines filed a proposed class action Tuesday in D.C. federal court alleging DOD violated the Privacy Act when a Defense Manpower Data Center file-sharing flaw let a "small number of unauthorized users" access names, birthdates, Social Security numbers and other records for nearly nine months. DMDC found the vulnerability in July but didn't notify affected people until late September. The complaint cites inspector general warnings dating to 2021 and seeks more than $5 million for over 3 million people. Coming the same week a judge ruled OPM broke the Privacy Act with DOGE, it adds to legal pressure on how agencies protect personnel data.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[NPS Selects Nine R&D Projects for Digital Trident II Initiative](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/nps-nine-rd-projects-digital-trident-ii)** (4 minute read)
The Naval Postgraduate School picked nine projects for Digital Trident II, which pairs Pacific military needs with university researchers and tech partners. They include Project Adversarial Agents, which uses multiple AI agents for Pacific wargaming and adversarial analysis; an edge space-domain-awareness tool called SPECTRAL; MCOO-X for planning how to deploy and sustain unmanned systems across the Pacific; ANVIL for readiness and talent data; and an "Applied SI" project for an affordable counter-drone interceptor aimed at transition to the Marine Corps. Teams will refine requirements and test concepts with operational users, a route for small firms and academics into INDOPACOM problems.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[Keeping a human in the AI loop is 'untenable' due to cuts, USDA IT official says](https://fedscoop.com/ai-human-in-the-loop-agency-workforces/)** (5 minute read)
At FedInsider's Innovation Summit Wednesday, USDA IT program manager Rudolf Rojas said staffing and contract cuts make keeping a human in the loop "untenable," so USDA will lean on agents with "minimal privileges, minimal role," zero-trust monitoring and a strong risk framework. He also said he "kind of" disagrees with the administration's "free rein" stance and wants national AI rules. CBP's Ajay Phogat argued the opposite, saying humans stay responsible, and GAO's Kevin Walsh said "the government is over its skis" on oversight. It's a candid look at how workforce cuts are pushing agencies toward more autonomous AI than their policies assume.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CPSC Names Beth Puchek Chief Artificial Intelligence Officer (CAIO)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cpsc-names-beth-puchek-chief-artificial-intelligence-officer-caio-302915925.html)** (2 minute read)
The Consumer Product Safety Commission named Chief Data Officer Beth Puchek its chief AI officer on Oct. 21; she'll hold both roles. Acting Chairman Peter Feldman said she'll lead the agency's "super intelligence" strategy and governance to improve operations and data-driven decisions for consumer product safety. It's another agency folding the chief AI officer job into an existing data leadership role rather than creating a separate post.
*Source: CPSC (official release via PR Newswire) · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[PNNL's PermitAI Team Releases 2 Public Resources for Environmental Review Data](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/pnnl-permitai-nepatec-environmental-review-data)** (4 minute read)
DOE's Pacific Northwest National Laboratory released NEPATEC3.0, a corpus of 188,000 National Environmental Policy Act documents (up from 140,000) with more than 30 metadata types, 630,000+ map images and 135,000 GeoTIFFs from a geospatial AI pipeline. It also released NEPA-MCP, an open-source Model Context Protocol toolkit with 23 servers and 56 tools pulling live data from more than 20 government sources, from current federal reviews to wildlife habitat and water data. PNNL says PermitAI is now ready for real-world permitting workflows. Open MCP servers for federal data give agencies and vendors a ready base for permitting agents.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[PHP flaws draw millions of hacking attempts amid global push for fixes](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/php-flaws-draw-millions-hacking-attempts-amid-global-push-fixes/416771/)** (5 minute read)
What's new since yesterday's CISA/NSA/FBI warning on the AI-discovered "php2shell" flaws: Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince said attempts grew from thousands in the 10 hours before disclosure to millions, and Cisco Talos and Arctic Wolf saw attacks within hours, including attempts to pull malware tied to the Potassium botnet. Censys counted about 39.7 million internet-facing services on affected PHP versions (12.8 million in the U.S.), with 1.34 million moved to fixed releases since Oct. 21. OX Security found about 1.97 million of 2.2 million Shodan-listed installs still in the affected range a day after fixes. Most activity is still scanning, but Talos expects more organized attacks, so agencies should confirm with hosting providers and contractors that PHP itself, not just WordPress or Drupal, is patched.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CISA, FBI, Secret Service & Partners Expose SafePay Ransomware Tactics](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/cisa-fbi-secret-service-safepay-ransomware-advisory)** (3 minute read)
CISA, the FBI, Secret Service and international partners issued a #StopRansomware advisory Wednesday on SafePay, one of 2026's most prolific ransomware groups, with roughly 600 victims across the Americas, Europe and Australia, including several critical infrastructure sectors. SafePay typically gets in with stolen credentials or known flaws in internet-facing devices, uses legitimate admin tools to move laterally, deletes backups, steals data before encrypting and sometimes phones victims to press for payment. CISA's Chris Butera urged patching the listed known exploited vulnerabilities and forensic triage. It lands as defenders are already stretched by the PHP patching rush.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*
