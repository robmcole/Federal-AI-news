## Congress & Legislation

**[Senate lawmakers press Pentagon for greater price transparency](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/congress/2026/10/senate-lawmakers-press-pentagon-for-greater-price-transparency/)** (3 minute read)
Sens. Elizabeth Warren, Joni Ernst, Chuck Grassley and Elissa Slotkin wrote to Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinberg asking how DOD will get cost and pricing data from contractors, including on contracts under the $10 million threshold Congress set last year (up from $2.5 million). A House Armed Services analysis found that change cuts DOD off from cost data on more than 60% of its contracts. They want reviews under Feinberg's Aug. 18 memo, which floated pulling cost data straight from contractors' ERP systems through APIs, extended below $10 million, and they ask whether DOD will drop contractors that refuse. Both chambers' FY2027 NDAA versions would require contractors to report price increases within 30 days, which would affect tech and software vendors as well as weapons makers.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**['No question' data centers will be considered critical infrastructure, top House intel Dem says](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/no-question-data-centers-will-be-considered-critical-infrastructure-top-house-intel-dem-says/416746/)** (3 minute read)
Speaking at Brookings on Wednesday, Rep. Jim Himes, ranking member of the House Intelligence Committee, said there's "no question" AI data centers will be designated critical infrastructure. They aren't one of CISA's 16 sectors today. He pointed to Iran's March drone strikes on AWS data centers in the UAE and a commercial site in Bahrain, and to Russia and Ukraine trading strikes on data centers, and said future facilities will need to "fragment and disperse" key services for redundancy. He also called the House-passed Ratepayer Protection Act "not meaningful or substantive." A sector designation would bring new federal security expectations to the companies building AI compute.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[FirstSource III protesters lose bid to revive $10B DHS contract](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/10/firstsource-iii-protesters-lose-bid-revive-10b-dhs-contract/416734/)** (3 minute read)
Court of Federal Claims Judge Edward Meyers ruled that DHS had a rational basis to cancel FirstSource III, its $10 billion IT hardware vehicle, under the March 2025 executive order (EO 14240) consolidating common buys through GSA and governmentwide contracts. He also denied the 23 remaining protesters' request for bid and proposal costs. DHS found 14 of the 15 awardees were already reachable through NASA SEWP or GSA Schedules, and it has bought through those since FirstSource II expired in September 2024. The judge didn't rule on GAO's finding that DHS overstated the cost avoidance. The ruling strengthens agencies' hand in canceling agency-specific vehicles under the consolidation order.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Army unveils first drafts for $7B in HR support contracts](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/companies/2026/10/army-unveils-first-drafts-7b-hr-support-contracts/416731/)** (2 minute read)
The Army released draft RFPs Monday for its Human Resources Solutions GEN VI recompete. It has two multiple-award contracts: Recruiting, Management and Administrative Support, a $1.5 billion small-business set-aside, and Personnel Life-Cycle Support, $6.09 billion and full and open. Both run a five-year base plus one five-year option. PLS covers manpower forecasting, predictive modeling, data management and ancillary IT, while RMAS includes data analytics and technology integration. About $2.7 billion has flowed through the current versions since 2018, and comments are due Oct. 26 at 5 p.m. ET.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[GSA, SBA target more 8(a) firms for audits](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/10/gsa-sba-target-more-8-firms-audits/416735/)** (1 minute read)

For IT services firms that rely on 8(a) set-asides, scrutiny is moving beyond eligibility to the contracts they already hold.
*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Former U.S. officials urge D.C. Circuit to reconsider decision in Anthropic-Pentagon fight](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/21/dc-circuit-decision-anthropic-pentagon-fight/)** (4 minute read)
Seventeen former national security officials, including Avril Haines, Susan Rice and Jake Sullivan, filed a 26-page brief Tuesday asking the full D.C. Circuit to rehear Anthropic's challenge to Defense Secretary Hegseth's February "supply-chain risk" designation. A panel ruled 2-1 for the Pentagon last month, while a San Francisco judge earlier sided with Anthropic. Because Congress gave the D.C. Circuit exclusive jurisdiction over these designations, the brief argues the ruling is now nationwide law that "will threaten all defense contractors." It calls the designation pretextual and retaliatory, saying it came after Anthropic refused uses for mass domestic surveillance and lethal autonomous weapons. Together with HHS's planned sole-source Claude award, it shows civilian and defense agencies treating the same vendor very differently.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Air Force starting to game out sustainment framework for CCA drones](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/21/air-force-cca-drones-sustainment-plan/)** (4 minute read)
Lt. Gen. Linda Hurry, head of Air Force Materiel Command, said Wednesday at a Mitchell Institute event that the service is "just starting to develop the sustainment framework" for Collaborative Combat Aircraft, with a life cycle sustainment plan not yet "fully fleshed out." The Air Force plans 500 CCAs by 2032. Anduril and General Atomics are building the first increment, and Shield AI and Collins Aerospace are providing mission autonomy. AFMC is already struggling to hire depot maintainers for existing fleets. Sustainment is a coming opportunity for supply-chain and maintenance vendors, and a risk if it lags fielding.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Army Special Forces leaders see promise, high demand for new robotics technician integrator job](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/21/army-special-forces-new-robotics-technician-integrator-job/)** (5 minute read)

Joseph Wortham said "the demand just inside of our formation is huge." The Army is now working out course throughput, which makes a trained workforce a pacing item for its autonomy push.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[What's new in Homeland Security's updated AI policy](https://fedscoop.com/dhs-ai-policy-updates/)** (4 minute read)
DHS quietly released Directive 139-08 Rev. 01, its updated AI use and acquisition policy, which took effect in September. It keeps the ban on AI for improper "systemic or large-scale monitoring, surveillance, or tracking" and emotion or behavior prediction. It adds a provision letting the chief AI officer waive any requirement, including prohibited uses, which CDT's Tom Bowman called "a rule that comes with its own off switch." The update comes after ICE awarded Thomson Reuters Special Services a $125 million contract in September for services enabling monitoring of 1 million people. It also narrows the privacy officer's role from ensuring AI "does not erode" privacy to ensuring it "complies," and requires DHS to share AI code and models and release custom code as open source. It's a template other agencies may follow, and the waiver is likely to draw oversight attention.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[OPM illegally shared millions of employee records with DOGE, federal judge rules](https://fedscoop.com/opm-illegally-shared-millions-employee-records-doge-judge/)** (5 minute read)
Judge Denise Cote of the Southern District of New York ruled Tuesday that OPM violated the Privacy Act and the APA by giving DOGE agents sweeping, expedited access to systems with records on tens of millions of past, current and prospective federal workers. She found no credible reason why modernization needed that PII. She rejected arguments that the case is moot because DOGE ended in July and access was revoked, and said OPM appeared to be matching litigation deadlines rather than changing its behavior. Remedies, including the unions' request to delete extracted data, come later. The ruling sets a marker for any future modernization or AI team seeking broad access to federal personnel data.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[NASA Seeks Data & AI Chief](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/nasa-data-ai-chief-search-genesis-mission)** (4 minute read)
NASA posted a permanent Senior Executive Service opening on USAJobs for a "chief data and super intelligence officer," using the September executive order's terminology. The officer will set agencywide data and AI policy, serve as vice chair of the Data and Super Intelligence Governance Board and represent NASA in the OSTP/DOE Genesis Mission. Applications close Oct. 29, and the job requires an SCI clearance. Kevin Murphy is currently acting chief AI officer and chief data officer. It would give NASA a permanent, single owner for AI as Genesis funding grows.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[US agencies urge fixes for programming language flaws putting millions of web systems at risk](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/us-agencies-urge-fixes-programming-language-flaws-putting-millions-web-systems-risk/416726/)** (3 minute read)
NSA, FBI and CISA told organizations Wednesday to immediately patch two critical PHP flaws, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 ("php2shell"), that allow unauthenticated remote code execution, or to take systems offline if they can't. PHP runs about 70% of websites with an identifiable server-side language, Unit 42 counts more than 22.6 million potentially exposed instances, and Nextgov found about 900 U.S. government-linked results on Shodan. Security firm Calif found one flaw using OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol, and Wiz found both. OpenAI said it engaged the SI Force, the national cyber director's office and CISA before disclosure. CISA hadn't seen exploitation as of publication, and the fixed versions are PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35. Agencies running WordPress, Drupal or Joomla sites need to check now.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [CISA alert: Critical Vulnerabilities in PHP Require Immediate Patching](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/21/critical-vulnerabilities-php-require-immediate-patching)*

**[PHP says AI review of critical bugs first sparked alarm in August, but signs surfaced in 2024](https://therecord.media/php-foundation-statement-critical-vulnerabilities)** (4 minute read)
The PHP Foundation said one php2shell bug was flagged in a 2024 external audit, but fixing it stalled for months over compatibility worries and a small core team. It became top priority only after AI code-review tools from Anthropic and OpenAI were used to scan PHP and Calif reported the bugs Aug. 11. A leaked Hacker News post, a mistakenly published Calif proof-of-concept video and Chainguard's early release of patched images forced disclosure a day early. Patches only cover PHP 8.2 and newer, and Cisco Talos estimates about half of vulnerable systems may never get one. It's a clear case of AI-found vulnerabilities outpacing open-source maintainers, and a warning for agencies running end-of-life software.
*Source: The Record · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Microsoft pins ConnectWise supply-chain attack to China-state aligned threat group](https://cyberscoop.com/microsoft-attributes-connectwise-supply-chain-attack-china-storm-0940/)** (3 minute read)
Microsoft attributed the compromise of ConnectWise Cloud Backup customers' Microsoft 365 environments to Storm-0940, a China-aligned group that used ARTEX, an open-source agentic AI penetration-testing framework. Microsoft says the tool let it hit more than 2,250 tenants at 2,900 sign-in attempts per minute. In some environments the agents went on to Microsoft Graph reconnaissance, credential changes for persistence and privileged-role changes. Targets included government, defense, aerospace and think tanks, and ConnectWise began a phased, multi-day restoration Wednesday. Microsoft's Igor Tsyganskiy called it "one of many" AI-driven attacks since early October, which fits the governmentwide AI-attack tabletop planned for early November.
*Source: CyberScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[The AI Force the United States Needs is a Cyber Force](https://www.csis.org/analysis/ai-force-united-states-needs-cyber-force)** (7 minute read)
CSIS's Joshua Stiefel and Rob T. Lee argue that if the president wants an "AI Force," the Space Force model fits cyberspace, not AI, since AI is a technology every service will use, like radar. They point to Anthropic's reports of a Chinese state group running 80–90% of an espionage campaign with Claude Code and a Yemen cell using it for missile guidance code. They also note China's April 2024 Cyberspace Force and Israel's Unit 8200 pipeline, and say the U.S. has built neither a cyber service nor that kind of talent pipeline. It's a useful counterpoint as the SI Force's 120-day review shapes what the White House builds next.
*Source: CSIS · Reliability Score: 3/5*
