## Congress & Legislation

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Pentagon issues guidance on AI procurements to support Trump's 'truth-seeking' and 'ideological neutrality' directives](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/23/pentagon-guidance-ai-procurement-trump-directive/)** (4 minute read)
An Oct. 20 memo from John Tenaglia, principal director for Defense Pricing, Contracting, and Acquisition Policy, tells the military departments, SOCOM, Cyber Command, TRANSCOM and defense agencies that complying with the "Preventing Woke AI" executive order and OMB's "Unbiased AI Principles" memo is mandatory. It comes with three templates: a memo for the requiring activity to pick transparency requirements, standard solicitation language telling vendors what documentation to submit, and contract language for the statement or performance work statement. When buying an LLM, DOD must get enough information to judge compliance, and the memo notes resellers will usually have less of that information than the original model developer. In practice, vendors reselling or wrapping frontier models should expect to be asked for documentation from the model maker itself.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[HHS sole-source award to Anthropic continues to raise questions](https://fedscoop.com/hhs-sole-source-award-to-anthropic-continues-to-raise-questions/)** (5 minute read)
What's new since last week's notice: an HHS spokesperson told FedScoop the Claude award will be "a temporary six-month sole-source bridge contract" to keep AI services running when Anthropic's $1 GSA OneGov deal expires Oct. 31, while HHS pursues a longer-term contract it wants in place before the bridge ends. GWU's Jessica Tillipman said the added detail "weakens" HHS's case, calling the rationale circular and asking why HHS didn't prepare for a public end date. Burr and Forman's David Timm said uniqueness and continuity are "tough sells," especially since HHS already switched Claude off during the governmentwide ban, and that a protest "would be some there there." The cited authority suggests a value under the $350,000 threshold, though HHS hasn't disclosed the cost.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[(US)AI becomes GSA's first direct OneGov contract with a small business](https://www.nextgov.com/acquisition/2026/10/usai-becomes-gsas-first-direct-onegov-contract-small-business/416789/)** (2 minute read)
GSA signed its first direct-to-government OneGov deal with an 8(a) small business, North Bethesda, Md.-based (US)AI, which will offer up to 30% off software licenses and $1 two-week proof-of-value pilots through Sept. 30, 2028. The FedRAMP High-authorized Intelligent Computing Platform lets agencies use approved models of their choice, with tools for software development, fraud detection, data orchestration, cyber defense and governance, risk and compliance. GSA has negotiated more than two dozen OneGov deals, with an estimated $1.6 billion in savings. It shows OneGov reaching beyond the big model makers to smaller platform vendors.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [GSA OneGov agreements](https://itvmo.gsa.gov/onegov/?tabName=agreements-tab#us-ai)*

**[Sigmatech Wins $249M Army Digital Decision Environment Deal](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/sigmatech-army-digital-decision-environment)** (2 minute read)

## Defense & National Security

**[Space Force reveals first 6 'operational' contracts for civil reserve support in space monitoring](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/space-force-reveals-first-6-operational-contracts-for-civil-reserve-support-in-space-monitoring/)** (5 minute read)
Space Systems Command awarded the first "operational" contracts under its Commercial Augmentation Space Reserve (CASR), the space version of the airlines' Civil Reserve Air Fleet, to six space-monitoring firms: EOS Space Systems, NorthStar Earth and Space, InTrack Radar, Anduril (via ExoAnalytic), Slingshot Aerospace and LeoLabs. Together they're worth $1.37 million, run through the GSA framework the Joint Commercial Operations cell uses, and are meant as short-term boosts in space domain awareness data to gauge military demand. CSIS's Clayton Swope said firms won't build idle surge capacity "for sport" without being paid, and key legal and financial terms are still unresolved. It's a small start for a program the Space Force says will grow to more companies and missions.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[NOAA seeks to improve weather forecasting through cloud migration](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2026/10/noaa-seeks-improve-weather-forecasting-through-cloud-migration/416790/)** (4 minute read)
NOAA is moving its Weather and Climate Operational Supercomputing System, two 14.5-petaflop on-premises supercomputers, to cloud high-performance computing with Google Cloud as the primary provider, aiming for a full-scale system by the end of 2026 and go-live by the end of 2027. Administrator Neil Jacobs said his bigger problem than trapped data is "data that we've deleted and will never get back." NOAA also plans to move forecasters' AWIPS workstation system to the cloud by early 2028, so National Weather Service staff can work from anywhere. It's one of the largest operational HPC moves to commercial cloud in civilian government.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[NOAA, Cowboy Space Will Test In-Orbit Satellite Data Processing Under New Agreement](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/noaa-cowboy-space-in-orbit-satellite-data-processing)** (3 minute read)
NOAA's satellite service, NESDIS, signed a two-year cooperative R&D agreement with Cowboy Space Corporation to test AI-enabled processing of environmental data in orbit, sending only key results to Earth instead of raw data. Cowboy Space will fully fund, build, launch and operate its Reason-2 satellite, scheduled for 2027, while NOAA supplies an existing open-source AI model and historical raw data from its VIIRS instrument. If it works, onboard AI could cut downlink needs and speed weather data delivery for next-generation satellites.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[NOT-OD-27-011: USG Policy for the use of Super Intelligence](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-27-011.html)** (2 minute read)
NIH issued an Oct. 23 notice telling the research community it is switching from "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI" to "Super Intelligence" and "SI" under Executive Order 14434. It defines SI using the existing statutory AI definition in 15 U.S.C. 9401(3), a machine-based system that makes predictions, recommendations or decisions for human-defined objectives. For grantees, the terminology changes but the underlying definition and rules don't.
*Source: NIH · Reliability Score: 5/5*

**[Commerce Plans $389M in CHIPS R&D Awards for Power Semiconductors, Fusion](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/commerce-389m-chips-rd-awards-power-semiconductors-fusion)** (3 minute read)
Commerce signed letters of intent for up to $389 million in CHIPS R&D awards for power semiconductors: Helion (up to $175 million), Commonwealth Fusion Systems (up to $85 million), Vertical Semiconductor (up to $73 million, including a planned 2.5-megawatt AI data center rack demo) and Sibelco (up to $56 million for high-purity quartz). Commerce will take a minority, non-voting equity stake in each company. Power electronics for the grid and AI data centers are becoming a CHIPS priority alongside logic chips.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [NIST announcement](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/10/department-commerce-announces-letters-intent-4-companies-389-million-rd)*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[CISA, Allies Warn of Denial-of-Wallet Risks in AI Token Guidance](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/cisa-allies-ai-token-guidance-denial-of-wallet)** (4 minute read)
CISA and cyber agencies from Australia, Canada, New Zealand and the U.K. released "Managing AI Token Consumption for Operational Resilience," warning that usage-based AI pricing is hard to forecast and agentic workflows multiply token use. It proposes three tiers: cyber defense and incident response first, compliance and core business functions monitored, and routine tasks like email drafting throttled at peaks. It describes "denial-of-tokens" attacks that burn through quotas and "denial-of-wallet" attacks that run up the bill, and suggests feeding token telemetry into SIEMs, since spikes can also signal data exfiltration. For agencies, it means checking token pricing and quotas before signing AI service agreements and keeping manual fallbacks.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [CISA guidance page](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/managing-ai-token-consumption-operational-resilience)*

**[FLRA rules against HHS on telework, flags fake citations in agency brief](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/workforce-rightsgovernance/2026/10/flra-rules-against-hhs-on-telework-flags-fake-citations-in-agency-brief/)** (4 minute read)
The Federal Labor Relations Authority upheld an arbitrator's award restoring telework for HHS employees represented by NTEU, and found more than half the legal or record citations in HHS's brief were "fictitious, erroneous or irrelevant." The authority wrote that HHS "may have used a generative AI tool" and cautioned parties to verify AI-generated material. It's a rare official finding of AI hallucinations in a federal agency's own legal filing.
*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[CISA vulnerability response chief departing agency after nearly six years](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/cisa-vulnerability-response-chief-departing-agency-after-nearly-six-years/416786/)** (4 minute read)
Lindsey Cerkovnik, CISA's branch chief for vulnerability response and coordination, whose office helps oversee the CVE program, is leaving government this week and starts a new role in mid-November; it's unclear who takes over. Her tenure covered the Log4Shell response, the coordinated vulnerability disclosure platform and the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. The exit comes as AI-assisted research, such as the php2shell PHP flaws, is driving up the number of vulnerabilities defenders must sort through.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[FCC bans two devices with surveillance chips linked to Huawei](https://therecord.media/fcc-bans-devices-huawei-surveillance-chips)** (2 minute read)
The FCC blocked a wireless video transmitter and a dashboard camera from the U.S. market because they use chips from Huawei subsidiary HiSilicon, the first ban under rules that took effect Oct. 13 barring devices with components from Covered List companies. Before those rules, otherwise lawful devices could contain covered-list parts. Chairman Brendan Carr called the chips an "obvious and unacceptable risk," signaling component-level supply chain enforcement is now live.
*Source: The Record · Reliability Score: 4/5*
