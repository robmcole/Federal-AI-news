## White House & Executive Action

**[Trump administration plans to name Nvidia, OpenAI, Anthropic and others as AI "national champions"](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/us-plans-to-name-nvidia-among-ai-national-champions-wsj-reports-5028365)** (1 minute read)

The Wall Street Journal reported Sunday that the administration plans to designate more than two dozen companies, including Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic, as AI "national champions" to help them expand overseas and compete with Chinese firms such as Huawei. Designees would reportedly get expedited export licence approvals for chips and other technology, government support for overseas operations and access to federal financing. The program would bundle U.S. chips, models and other technology into packages foreign buyers can purchase. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick could unveil it within a week, though timing could change. The plan lands just as Congress pushes to tighten chip exports after the Nvidia scandal. Reuters said it couldn't immediately verify the report.

*Source: Investing.com (summarizing The Wall Street Journal) · Reliability Score: 3/5*

**[US and nine partners sign the San Francisco Declaration on securing AI supply chains](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesman/2026/11/the-san-francisco-declaration-on-the-secure-foundations-of-enduring-prosperity/)** (4 minute read)

On Nov. 15, at a Pax Silica Founders Retreat convened by Under Secretary of State Jacob Helberg, the U.S., Australia, India, Israel, Japan, South Korea, Qatar, Singapore, the UAE and the U.K. signed a declaration that "economic security is fundamental to national security." It defines the sensitive technologies and critical infrastructure (ports, communications, energy, ICT and digital platforms) that underpin "Super Intelligence" supply chains, and declares that protecting them is a shared job. It proposes five working groups: pro-innovation policy, SI adoption in key sectors, supply chain cooperation across the SI stack, safe harbors and supply chain security hubs, and SI security and resilience. It's a political framework, not new export controls, but it sets the baseline the coalition plans to act on.

*Source: U.S. Department of State · Reliability Score: 5/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Cantwell accuses Commerce of failing to stem the flow of AI chips to China](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-11-16/senator-accuses-us-commerce-department-of-failing-to-stem-flow-of-ai-chips-to-china)** (3 minute read)

In a Nov. 15 letter to Commerce Secretary Lutnick, Senate Commerce ranking member Maria Cantwell said Commerce "delayed action on repeated national security warnings." She also said the department suspended a probe into Megaspeed, the Singapore company at the center of the Nvidia scandal. **What changed since Friday's digest:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Friday that Treasury, the Pentagon, State and USTR had favored putting Megaspeed on a trade blacklist for months, but Commerce, which controls the list, had not. Cantwell also argues that the administration's 25% fee on certain Nvidia chips sold to China creates "a perverse financial incentive to approve exports." The letter adds pressure on the NDAA chip-export provisions now in conference. Lutnick's spokesperson declined to comment.

*Source: Reuters (via U.S. News & World Report) · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Tech bills of the week: a frontier AI licensing bill, no data centers on public lands, and a VA innovation office](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/11/tech-bills-week-no-data-centers-public-lands-creating-va-office-innovation-and-more/417230/)** (7 minute read)

Reps. Josh Gottheimer and Mike Lawler introduced a bill directing Commerce to regulate frontier AI models above a "minimum parameter threshold." Covered developers would need NSA pre-deployment evaluations and would have to disclose training and safety-test results, with civil penalties of $500,000 a day. A new "Artificial Intelligence Security Review Court" would hear appeals. Rep. Maxine Dexter would bar new data centers on Interior and Forest Service lands, where her office counts at least 17 proposed campuses, and Rep. Tim Burchett would block federal funds for data centers without state and local approval. Reps. McGarvey and Luttrell's INNOVATE Act would create a VHA Office of Innovation with up to $10 million in OTA authority. Most of these face a short lame-duck clock, but they preview the 120th Congress agenda.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[Court of Federal Claims hands Saildrone the first-ever win in an OTA protest](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/contracting/2026/11/we-may-be-on-the-way-to-more-clarity-on-when-vendors-can-protest-other-transaction-agreements/)** (10 minute read)

In a challenge over the Navy's medium unmanned surface vessel program, which Congress funded with $2.1 billion available through 2029, the court rejected Blue Water Autonomy's protest but sustained Saildrone's. It found that the Navy evaluated Saildrone unequally when it cut the company in phase one. Attorney Zach Prince says it's the first OTA protest a protester has won, and the case may give the Federal Circuit its first real chance to rule on whether the court has jurisdiction over OTAs. That matters because DoD's OT commitments topped $30 billion in FY26, and an executive order makes OTAs the department's preferred vehicle.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[GAO denies General Dynamics' protest; Lockheed keeps the $10.5B SOCOM logistics contract](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/11/gao-sides-lockheed-fight-over-105b-contract/417210/)** (4 minute read)

GAO's docket shows GD's challenge to the 12-year Special Operations Forces Global Logistics Support Services award as "denied," with a decision dated Nov. 11. GD had argued the best-value decision was flawed. Lockheed has held the work since 2017, with some elements dating to 2010. The vehicle also covers IT management, supply chain and facility operations, and other agencies can use it. The written decision will follow once the parties agree on redactions.

*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[GSA asks industry about post-quantum cryptography products](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/gsa-rfi-post-quantum-cryptography-readiness)** (4 minute read)

GSA's Federal Acquisition Service posted an RFI on Nov. 9 seeking commercial products and services that support NIST's post-quantum standards (FIPS 203, 204 and 205). It asks about validation status, implementation maturity, interoperability, migration assistance and how agencies can buy them. The RFI cites EO 14412 and OMB memo M-26-15 on the PQC migration, and stresses that agencies must use CMVP-validated modules. It's market research only, with no commitment to a solicitation, but it signals how GSA may package PQC buys. Responses are due Dec. 7.

*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 3/5*

**[Air Force Special Warfare seeks a vehicle-agnostic mobile C2 system](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/13/air-force-special-warfare-mobile-communication-system/)** (3 minute read)

An RFI for the Mobile Communication System-Universal (MCS-U) program seeks a mobile command-and-control node for TACP operators that passes voice, data and video on the move. It would plug into any AFSPECWAR vehicle and connect to the Advanced Battle Management System. The RFI asks about mesh networking, antenna auto-tracking, ruggedized mounting kits and power integration. A draft requirements document and draft RFP are tentatively planned for FY2027, pending acquisition and funding approval. Responses are due Dec. 11.

*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[Pentagon rebrands its principal AI shop as the "Super Intelligence Office"](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/pentagon-rebrands-principle-ai-shop-as-super-intelligence-office/)** (3 minute read)

On Nov. 13 the Department of War redesignated the Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office (CDAO) as the Super Intelligence Office (SIO). It's still led by Cameron Stanley, and its public resources, acquisition pathways and partnership portals now live at SI.mil. DoD says the mission is unchanged, and existing programs, including Maven Smart System and the War Data Platform (formerly Advana), along with solicitations and partnerships, continue "uninterrupted." The change rolls out across systems and component headquarters over the coming weeks. Vendors should update their bookmarks and capture tracking, since CDAO-branded opportunities will now appear under SIO.

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[Legacy tech issue led to the FAA's nationwide ground stop](https://www.nextgov.com/modernization/2026/11/legacy-tech-issue-led-faas-nationwide-ground-stop/417232/)** (7 minute read)

Friday's ground stop lasted more than four hours and hit at least 10,000 flights and more than 2 million passengers. Secretary Duffy said routers "got overloaded with data," causing intermittent radar and communications. On Saturday he said the trouble may have started when L3Harris, which owns the FAA's network, was moving a remote site from copper to fiber. L3Harris says more than 70% of that conversion is done. DHS Secretary Mullin said there's no sign of a cyberattack but hasn't ruled one out. Duffy is asking Congress for another $50 billion beyond the $12.5 billion in OBBBA air traffic modernization funds, which could make this a major lame-duck and FY27 IT funding fight.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[IRS pilots an agentic AI tool to work fraud cases](https://fedscoop.com/irs-identity-theft-fraud-fighting-agentic-ai-tool/)** (4 minute read)

Jim Clifford, IRS director of return integrity and compliance services, said at ACT-IAC's Service Delivery and Experience Summit that the IRS is piloting agentic AI for the "rote" back-end steps after machine-learning models flag suspicious returns. The agent would review taxpayer responses, summarize them, build a case and hand it to an employee ready for a decision, and humans keep the judgment calls. There's no deployment timeline yet. Clifford also cited about 1 billion fraud attempts a month and GAO's estimate of $116 billion to $304 billion lost to tax fraud each year. He called for legal changes to allow more cross-agency data sharing.

*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[State Department launches an online first-time passport pilot through America.gov](https://www.nextgov.com/digital-government/2026/11/state-department-pilot-online-passport-applications-first-time-applicants/417213/)** (5 minute read)

**What changed since our Nov. 6 America.gov item:** a Federal Register notice says the online Form DS-11 pilot runs from today, Nov. 16, through Nov. 20, 2027. It starts with U.S.-based adults who held a passport but can't renew online, and first-time adults with a Certificate of Naturalization. Applicants are screened through the AI chatbot America.gov, verify their identity with Login.gov, upload a photo and record a video of themselves reciting the oath for an agent to review. State processed about 13.9 million DS-11s in FY2025, and the paper form takes about 85 minutes to complete. It plans to expand the pilot to birth-certificate holders and applicants overseas.

*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[FedRAMP forces cloud providers onto AI-era vulnerability rules by Dec. 7](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/fedramp-pushes-new-cyber-vulnerability-rules-to-keep-pace-with-ai/)** (5 minute read)

A Nov. 10 FedRAMP notice requires more than 550 certified cloud services to migrate to the new Vulnerability Detection and Response and Vulnerability Evaluation and Reporting rules by Dec. 7. The rules replace monthly scans and severity scores with exposure- and threat-based prioritization, in line with CISA's July binding operational directive. Providers can file corrective action plans and get a grace period through March 7, 2027. On March 8, any service that hasn't migrated and isn't on an approved plan loses certification and is banned for a year. FedRAMP also starts quarterly "security inbox emergency tests" in early December, and it cites last month's "Black October" wave of AI-driven incidents as the reason for the urgency.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Questions swirl around FAA's new SMART AI system, though officials deny a link to the ground stop](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/technology-main/2026/11/questions-swirl-around-faas-new-smart-ai-system-though-officials-say-no-link-to-ground-stop/)** (6 minute read)

The FAA expanded its $875 million SMART AI traffic-management system, built by Air Space Intelligence, to all 22 air traffic control centers on Nov. 10, three days before the ground stop. DHS, DOT, the FAA and ASI all say SMART wasn't the cause. House Democrats had already asked GAO in an Oct. 13 letter to audit the system, warning that controllers had little input and that it could "hallucinate safety-critical information." SMART only makes recommendations, and humans decide. The FAA says a fall test in the Washington area rerouted 180 flights and saved an average of 10 minutes and 80 gallons of fuel per flight. Expect pressure for that GAO review and for clearer guardrails on safety-critical AI.

*Source: Federal News Network · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Industry & Vendor News

**[Kudu has given Leidos a cyber boost and an AI surprise](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/companies/2026/11/kudu-has-given-leidos-cyber-boost-and-ai-surprise/417205/)** (7 minute read)

Less than two years after its $300 million purchase of Kudu, Leidos has booked nearly $260 million in new Cyber Command work: a $160 million operations support contract and a $99 million OTA to keep developing a DARPA-born cyber mission planning platform. Leidos Intelligence President Jason O'Connor said Kudu's AI work produced Parcata, a tool that finds, ranks and patches software vulnerabilities and grew out of DARPA's AI Cyber Challenge. Leidos is running it on its own code ahead of a commercial release. It's a sign of large integrators turning to product-based, fixed-price AI offerings as agencies demand "ready to go" solutions.

*Source: Washington Technology · Reliability Score: 4/5*
