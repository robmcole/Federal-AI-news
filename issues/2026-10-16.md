## White House & Executive Action

**[White House's AI task force gets to work](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/15/2026/white-houses-ai-task-force-gets-to-work)** (2 minute read)
Update: yesterday's digest said the Super Intelligence Force was expected to meet for the first time. It met Thursday, formally adopted its charter, started its 120-day window and agreed to commission a report from the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology on three risk areas: cybersecurity and critical infrastructure protection, biosecurity and dual-use applications, and model control and accountability. Chair Jay Clayton and National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross will lead a working group to implement the White House Accord on Super Intelligence that AI CEOs signed this month. Vice chair Emil Michael will write protocols for labs to report breaches, hacks and other cyber issues to the government. FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson will lead a law enforcement and consumer protection committee with DOJ, and OPM's Scott Kupor takes critical infrastructure protection plus a study with the Labor Department of AI's effects on workers. It is still unclear how compliance will be enforced. A White House official pointed to export controls as one possible stick, which means Michael's incident-reporting protocol is the piece most likely to reach labs and federal AI vendors first.
*Source: Semafor · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[US Treasury pushes global partners to rename AI as 'super intelligence'](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/us-treasury-pushes-global-partners-rename-ai-super-intelligence-6462536)** (3 minute read)
Treasury Under Secretary for International Affairs Erin Browne told Reuters on Thursday at the IMF/World Bank meetings in Bangkok that the U.S. is negotiating with other IMF shareholders to use "super intelligence" in the statement the International Monetary and Financial Committee is drafting. Browne would not say whether Washington would refuse to sign off without the change. A source said U.S. officials had suggested they would insist on the new language, which raised eyebrows among some members. The committee plans to release any agreed statement on Saturday. The administration is now taking the "SI" terminology from last month's executive order into international forums, and Browne said the U.S., which holds the G20 presidency, wants the change adopted internationally.
*Source: Reuters via CNA · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Congress & Legislation

**[Moreno pushes 100% tax on data center subsidies and incentives](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/technology/4767582/bernie-moreno-federal-tax-data-centers-subsidies-incentives/)** (3 minute read)
Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-Ohio) introduced the RECLAIM Act on Thursday. It would put a 100% federal excise tax on any state or local incentive a covered data center receives, bar federal grants, loans, tax credits and other incentives for those facilities, and express the sense of Congress that states and localities should repeal their own data center incentives. Under the bill text, "covered" means a facility or campus with at least 100 megawatts of contracted peak power demand where at least half of the installed compute accelerators are AI chips used for training, fine-tuning or inference. Treasury and Commerce would have one year to define "AI accelerator chip." Those thresholds aim squarely at large AI campuses. The bill comes as data centers become a central issue in Ohio's Senate race, and Sens. Sanders and Hawley have offered proposals of their own.
*Source: Washington Examiner · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [RECLAIM Act bill text](https://www.moreno.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Data-Center-Bill.pdf)*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[AI slop responses to RFPs, RFIs are overwhelming federal agencies](https://fedscoop.com/ai-slop-responses-rfp-rfi-federal-agencies/)** (3 minute read)
"We used to get maybe 10 responses to an RFI or RFP. Now, we're getting 500 because everybody puts it in a chatbot and sends it to us," the Transportation Department's senior AI adviser said Wednesday at the SAP Public Sector Summit. He called it a "whole-of-government problem." In a recent RFI, ICE asked vendors to recommend evaluation methods that tell "genuine, demonstrated hands-on capability" apart from "purely generative AI responses." A DOD RFP earlier this year disclosed that the government may use AI to help review proposals, while keeping final source-selection decisions with government personnel. Bidders should expect agencies to look harder for proof of hands-on capability and to use AI to screen what they receive, so polished boilerplate will count for less.
*Source: FedScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[RFC-0036 FedRAMP 20x Class D Cloud-Hosted Pilot Requirements](https://www.fedramp.gov/rfcs/0036/)** (8 minute read)
FedRAMP published proposed rules on Oct. 15 for its Phase 4 pilot of 20x Class D, the highest assurance level of FedRAMP certification for cloud-hosted services. The RFC adds two definitions, updates six rulesets and introduces 10 new Key Security Indicators. One example: Class D providers must verify and validate the status of machine-based resources at least once every three days. The largest addition covers foreign ownership, control or influence (FOCI). FedRAMP says it will not make FOCI determinations itself; it will only confirm that providers persistently review the relevant indicators, and agencies will still make their own calls. This follows RFC-0035 on cryptographic module rules on Oct. 14. Cloud and AI providers seeking the most sensitive federal workloads should weigh in now, since FedRAMP warns the rules will change during the pilot.
*Source: FedRAMP · Reliability Score: 5/5*

## Defense & National Security

**[GAO Urges Pentagon to Fix Responsible AI Council, Staffing](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/gao-pentagon-responsible-ai-council-cdao-workforce)** (3 minute read)
In an Oct. 14 report, GAO found that the CDAO's Responsible AI Working Council, the department's working-level coordination body, did not meet from late 2024 until August 2025. Four components said they hit implementation problems during that gap that joint input could have eased. The council still has no charter, and only 3 of the Responsible AI Division's 10 authorized positions were filled as of February 2026. GAO recommended a charter that sets roles and meeting frequency and a fix for the division's staffing; the department concurred with both. GAO also asked Congress to consider directing DOD to act on older, still-open recommendations on AI collaboration and workforce planning. With the 2026 AI strategy prioritizing speed, the report warns that a weak responsible-AI office could slow fielding rather than speed it.
*Source: ExecutiveGov · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [GAO-27-107837, Artificial Intelligence: Actions and Opportunities to Implement Responsible Use in DOD](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-27-107837)*

**[US moves to staff leaders for industry-partnered offensive cyber program](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/10/us-moves-staff-leaders-industry-partnered-offensive-cyber-program/416613/)** (4 minute read)
According to three sources, the administration has begun selecting managers for the program announced in August that lets vetted U.S. companies gather intelligence on and disrupt foreign criminal hacking groups. Firms would work under Justice Department or DHS contracts, with written approval for each mission. Key questions are still open, including how to pay companies (officials are weighing success-based payments, but recovered crypto may have to go back to victims) and who is liable if a firm hits the wrong target or damages an innocent party's routers. The managers will also have to sort out overlapping law enforcement and intelligence equities and decide which groups count as state-tied, since those are excluded. For cyber firms, this is the first sign the memo is being put into practice.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Pentagon averts cyber pay cuts — for now — with one-year C-MIP 'transition plan'](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/15/pentagon-cyber-pay-c-mip-transition-plan/)** (5 minute read)
The Pentagon authorized the services to keep their legacy cyber incentive pay for one year. Some troops had been set to lose $100 to $1,000 a month when the new Cyber Mastery Incentive Pay program replaced the older models on Oct. 1. Through Sept. 30, 2027, eligible Cyber Mission Force members in five designated work roles will get whichever is higher, C-MIP or legacy pay, and service secretaries must submit updated incentive pay plans by Jan. 1, 2027. The Pentagon says more than 21% of the Cyber Mission Force has earned higher qualifications since June. Retention is critical as AI changes how fast cyber operations move, and the department says it will reevaluate whether a year is long enough to upskill.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Military service members to square off against AI in 'Mindgame' tournament](https://defensescoop.com/2026/10/15/humans-vs-artificial-intelligence-wargames-drone-swarms/)** (2 minute read)
A Marine Corps message released Wednesday announced that Marines and other members of the joint force will play online wargames against an AI adversary built by Strategy Robot. The scenarios include drone swarms and carrier strike groups, and the games run on Marine Corps University's Wargaming Cloud using Command Professional Edition. The Marine Corps says the AI is not scripted. It uses strategic reasoning and computational game theory to adapt its courses of action, and players get a large language model after-action tool to review their decisions. Active and reserve troops, government civilians and contractors can apply by Nov. 14, and the tournament runs Dec. 1 to April 1, 2027. The message calls it a pathway to building AI adversaries into military wargaming.

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[CMS hopes to quadruple provider suspensions over next six months, official says](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/cms-hopes-quadruple-provider-suspensions-over-next-six-months-official-says/416640/)** (3 minute read)
CMS Deputy Administrator Kimberly Brandt said Thursday that CMS is piloting AI and machine learning to flag Medicaid billing anomalies before payment, including upcoding, phantom services and geographic outliers. CMS will soon publish results from an Indiana pilot with Oracle and is working with five other states. Her slides credit the fraud push with $41.9 billion in FY2025 savings, up 59% from the year before, and say the "Medicaid War Room" stopped $342 million in improper payments as of September. Brandt hopes to quadruple provider suspensions in six months under new HHS exclusion authority. She also admitted "a lot of things about WISeR," the AI-supported Medicare prior authorization pilot, "are not working." The stated goal is a national platform, shared by CMS, states, Medicaid Fraud Control Units, program integrity contractors and law enforcement, that stops fraudulent Medicaid payments before they go out.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[AI offers Secret Service unique mission enhancements, official says](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/ai-offers-secret-service-unique-mission-enhancements-official-says/416627/)** (2 minute read)
Secret Service CIO and Chief AI Officer Chris Kraft said Thursday that AI can help the agency investigate and monitor threats, connect disparate information, and scale facial recognition, biometrics and object detection. Director Sean Curran recently said threats against full-time protectees are up more than 40% from last year, with about 10,000 since January. The agency lists three AI use cases in its public inventory, two of them deployed: facial recognition matching for criminal investigations and a tool that reads license plates from poor-quality images or video. It is also embedding AI specialists across its offices. Expect more biometric and video analytics demand from a short-staffed agency facing rising threat volumes.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Transportation turns to AI to trace research grant recipients' links to foreign adversaries](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/10/transportation-turns-ai-trace-research-grant-recipients-links-foreign-adversaries/416621/)** (3 minute read)
DOT's senior adviser for AI said the department has built AI tools, with partners, that examine principal investigators on short-listed research grants for direct links to Chinese Communist Party leadership or Russian oligarchs. As an example, he described tracing a startup's venture funding through foreign VCs back to a Chinese industrial bank. He said the screening is based on funding sources, not nationality. The capability is not in DOT's latest public AI use case inventory, and DOT did not respond to Nextgov's request for comment. This kind of AI-driven research-security vetting could spread to other grant-making agencies, which makes inventory disclosure and due-process questions more pressing.

**['Genesis Agent Skills' Release to Support Innovation Across the Genesis Mission](https://www.energy.gov/science/articles/genesis-agent-skills-release-support-innovation-across-genesis-mission)** (3 minute read)
DOE released Genesis Agent Skills, the first technical resources from the Genesis Mission's ModCon and American Science Cloud efforts. It is a free GitLab library of instructions and workflows that AI coding assistants can call on for SI safety, agent evaluation and monitoring, data governance, scientific workflows and multi-agent systems. A team co-led by PNNL and Berkeley Lab built it with contributors from across the national labs. DOE calls it the first building block of the Genesis Mission's shared research infrastructure. This is the first usable software from the Genesis Mission, and the formats it sets could shape how labs and partners build agents on the program's shared infrastructure.

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[Federal CISO: New AI Security Governance Group Established](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/federal-ciso-new-ai-security-governance-group-established/)** (2 minute read)
Acting Federal CISO Michael Duffy said Wednesday at the ACT-IAC Cybersecurity Summit that the Federal CISO Council has set up a working group on AI security governance. It will plan for decision-making "with super intelligence in the future" and build frameworks so agencies can combine workforce and AI capabilities now, moving gradually toward machine-to-machine operations. The council's four priorities are reducing exposure (including faster replacement of outdated edge devices), operational resilience, integrating AI into cyber tools and securing a modernized government. It is also preparing its second governmentwide CISO-level tabletop exercise. This could become the place where agency CISOs coordinate on agent and model security while the SI Force works out lab incident reporting.
*Source: MeriTalk · Reliability Score: 4/5*
