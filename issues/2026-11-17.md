## White House & Executive Action

**[Commerce moves to close the AI chip remote-access loophole](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/commerce-department-moves-close-ai-chip-loophole/417255/)** (2 minute read)
What changed since yesterday's Cantwell letter: on Monday, Nov. 16, BIS issued an interim final rule, which Commerce says was "written by and at the direction of" Treasury and the National Security Council. The rule requires licenses for advanced chip exports to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. Data centers will also need a license before giving remote access to companies headquartered in China or other restricted destinations, and those already serving such firms get 90 days from the effective date to obtain one. The rule also formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. It is the administration's first policy response to the Nvidia/Megaspeed scandal, and it lands days after Treasury Secretary Bessent publicly criticized Commerce over Megaspeed. The reported AI "national champions" announcement hadn't come as of this morning.

**[White House taps Eric Ueland, deputy to Russell Vought, as GAO nominee](https://fedscoop.com/white-house-nominates-eric-ueland-for-gao/)** (3 minute read)
Last week's reports are now official: on Nov. 16 the White House sent the Senate Ueland's nomination for comptroller general, a 15-year term. Ueland is OMB's deputy director for management. Senate HSGAC will take up the nomination at a hearing Thursday morning (Nov. 19) alongside a dozen other nominees. GAO has had only an acting head, Orice W. Brown, since Gene Dodaro retired at the end of 2025. FedScoop notes that the usual congressional commission list of candidates doesn't appear to have been sent first. Ueland has served as a top adviser to Vought, who has repeatedly tried to circumvent the watchdog, so the nomination is likely to draw scrutiny over GAO's independence, including its AI and IT oversight work.

## Congress & Legislation

**[Senate appropriators unveil $1.1T Pentagon spending bill, folding in reconciliation priorities](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/11/senate-appropriators-unveil-1-1t-pentagon-spending-bill-folding-in-reconciliation-priorities/)** (6 minute read)
The Senate Appropriations defense subcommittee proposed nearly $1.1 trillion for FY2027. That is well short of the administration's $1.5 trillion ask, which was $1.15 trillion in base budget plus $350 billion through reconciliation. The bill moves nearly $69 billion of the requested one-time mandatory money into discretionary funding, including $9.4 billion for Golden Dome and $3 billion for the Defense Autonomous Warfare Group (DAWG), both far below the Pentagon's request. The bill still needs a Senate floor vote and must be reconciled with the House before the Dec. 11 continuing-resolution deadline. With the $350B reconciliation package stalled, this bill is the most likely vehicle for autonomy and AI-heavy priorities.
*Source: Breaking Defense · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Federal tech fellows would get an expedited path to permanent jobs under a new bill](https://www.nextgov.com/policy/2026/11/federal-tech-fellows-expedited-path-permanent-jobs-new-bill/417258/)** (3 minute read)
Rep. James Walkinshaw (D-Va.) introduced the Keep Federal Tech Talent Act (H.R. 10960). It would let agencies convert qualified people in time-limited STEM roles, including AI, cybersecurity and data science, into career competitive-service jobs. It would also allow student loan repayment and professional development incentives, and require annual reporting on use of the authority. The bill targets fellowships like the administration's Tech Force, which places early-career technologists in two-year stints. NFFE and IFPTE have endorsed it. It's a preview of the workforce agenda a Democratic House may push in January.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [H.R. 10960 on Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/10960)*

## Acquisition & Contracts

**[DISA weighs combining SETI and ENCORE into a new "ZEUS" contracting vehicle](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/disa-weighs-combining-seti-and-encore-in-potential-new-contracting-vehicle/)** (3 minute read)
At DISA's Forecast to Industry Day on Nov. 16, procurement services executive Brian Leyda said the agency may merge its SETI and ENCORE III Full and Open IT vehicles into a successor called ZEUS (Extensible Environment for Unified Services). DISA would keep the features that work and drop commodity services that GSA can supply. DISA expects to start issuing RFIs in early 2027 and hasn't disclosed scope or value. ENCORE III Full and Open expires in 2028, and SETI options run from 2028 to 2030. This is one of DoD's biggest enterprise IT and emerging-tech on-ramps, so primes and small businesses should start positioning now.
*Source: MeriTalk · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DOE seeks input on partnerships for quantum computing testbeds](https://www.nextgov.com/emerging-tech/2026/11/doe-seeks-input-partnerships-quantum-computing-testbeds/417265/)** (2 minute read)
A SAM.gov RFI posted Monday outlines two linked DOE testbeds. One is a cryogenic environment for developing quantum computing, quantum sensing and classical cryogenic logic. The other integrates quantum computers with progressively scalable high-performance computing. DOE wants rough cost estimates for cryogenic infrastructure supporting two or four scaled experimental systems plus roughly 10 to 40 smaller experiments, along with input on architecture and software. It invites businesses, academia, national labs and vendors interested in partnering. The coverage doesn't list a response deadline, so check the notice.

## Defense & National Security

**[Pentagon names seven teams for high-energy laser counter-drone "shoot-off"](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/16/counter-drone-high-energy-laser-shoot-off/)** (2 minute read)
JIATF-401, the Army-led counter-UAS task force, picked seven vendor teams for phase 2 live-fire trials at Dugway Proving Ground starting by mid-December: Allen Control Systems (Bullfrog), Aurelius Systems (Archimedes), BlueHalo/AV (LOCUST X1), Kord Technologies (FIREFLY), IPG Photonics (Crossbow), Thor Dynamics (Laser Armor) and Yrikka (TALOS drone-based HEL). JIATF-401 director Brig. Gen. Matthew Ross said systems that perform well "can immediately result in a purchase order." That's a fast lane from demo to production for directed energy.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[Major changes to the Pentagon's audit strategy hinge on the War Data Platform](https://defensescoop.com/2026/11/16/pentagon-financial-auditing-strategy-war-data-platform/)** (4 minute read)
With the FY2026 Agency Financial Report out Monday (DoD's ninth straight failed audit), comptroller Jules Hurst said FY2026 is the last year the DoD IG oversees the audit. An independent public accounting firm takes over. DoD is targeting clean opinions on a defense-wide working capital fund for FY2027 and agency-wide for FY2028. Rather than modernize legacy systems, DoD is pulling data from 70 critical financial systems into the War Data Platform (formerly Advana, built on Databricks, with Accenture Federal as integrator). There it will use AI to reconcile transactions and flag bad data sources. It's one of the largest bets yet on AI-driven data validation in government finance.
*Source: DefenseScoop · Reliability Score: 4/5*

**[DOD CIO to release implementation plan for its post-quantum cryptography strategy soon](https://www.meritalk.com/articles/dod-cio-to-release-implementation-plan-for-pqc-strategy-soon/)** (3 minute read)
Speaking at DISA's Forecast to Industry Day in Towson, Md., DOD CIO official Sonu Shankar said a forthcoming plan will guide the department's move to quantum-resistant cryptography. He gave few details but stressed flexibility and integration. The plan builds on CIO Kirsten Davies' June 23 strategy, which calls for PQC on high-impact systems by 2030 and across the force by 2031. Vendors selling into DoD networks should expect PQC-readiness requirements to show up in solicitations.
*Source: MeriTalk · Reliability Score: 4/5*

## Civilian Agency Initiatives

**[Trump administration releases a roadmap to boost clinical AI](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/11/trump-administration-releases-roadmap-boost-clinical-ai/417254/)** (3 minute read)
An HHS roadmap published Friday lays out how the department plans to speed up clinical AI adoption: partnering with state AI sandboxes like Utah's prescription-refill pilots, risk-based regulation and post-market surveillance, clarifying who is liable when AI harms patients, and expanding outcomes-based payment through CMS's ACCESS model, with more models planned, including in Medicaid. It sets a "better than today" safety standard. Separately, FDA floated a discussion draft requiring chatbots to pass "competency-based" tests before marketing. This is the first written federal policy on working with state testbeds, though it doesn't settle whether FDA will regulate the AI tools being piloted.
*Source: Nextgov/FCW · Reliability Score: 4/5*
*Primary document: [Clinical SI Roadmap (CMS)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/clinical-si-roadmap.pdf)*

## AI Safety, Security & Incidents

**[AI hacking tool used in US aerospace sector intrusion, Google says](https://www.nextgov.com/cybersecurity/2026/11/ai-hacking-tool-used-us-aerospace-sector-intrusion-google-says/417218/)** (3 minute read)
Google Threat Intelligence said Monday that attackers used Cairn, a publicly available agentic penetration-testing framework tied to the Chinese open-source security community, in an intrusion targeting an unnamed U.S. aerospace organization. Google didn't attribute the hack to a government or say what was taken. Google also flagged other open-source agentic tools (ARTEX, CyberStrikeAI and PentAGI) that let attackers run multi-stage intrusions faster. ARTEX also appeared in an FBI advisory Friday. It's concrete evidence that autonomous AI attack tooling is reaching the defense industrial base, which matters for FedRAMP's AI-era patching rules and CMMC.
